# TraceMem, Inverted: The Design Questions Kivi Must Answer

This document inverts *TraceMem: Weaving Narrative Memory Schemata from User Conversational Traces* into the decisions behind it, then tests the assumptions behind those decisions against Kivi's stated product position. It maps choices; it does not recommend answers.

## Reading key

- **Paper** means TraceMem v1 (10 February 2026).
- **Kivi** means the attached position document, *The Things Kivi Comes to Know*.
- **Fit: yes / no / partial / unresolved** describes whether the paper's assumption holds for Kivi—not whether the paper's mechanism is good.
- **Underspecified** means the paper does not provide enough information to recover the choice reliably.
- **Load-bearing** marks decisions for which the paper provides direct or strong experimental evidence that another answer materially changes its reported results.

These are the paper's load-bearing answers. “Load-bearing” is restricted to choices supported by an ablation, a direct comparison, or an inseparable premise of the reported task—not choices that merely sound important.

## 3. Unexamined defaults

These are choices the implementation inherits or makes without treating them as explicit research questions. “Nobody” below means no alternative is considered in this paper or its experimental line as represented by its selected baselines—not a universal claim about all memory research.

| Default the paper does not interrogate | The alternative this comparison set does not try | Why the hidden choice matters for Kivi | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retention is symmetrical across conversational participants.** | An intentionally asymmetric memory that models only the product owner and treats everyone else as ephemeral context. | Kivi’s non-retention promise is precisely this asymmetry. A separate card per speaker still retains the other person. | Fig. 2; §3.2–3.3; LoCoMo setup. |
| **If information improves recall, it is eligible to be remembered.** | A system in which high predictive value can be a reason *not* to store something because it is sensitive, inferential, or socially illegitimate. | Kivi rejects psychological profiling even when technically achievable. | Introduction; Fig. 3 examples; §3.2. |
| **A coherent persona is a desirable target representation.** | A deliberately non-coherent ledger that preserves contradictions, context dependence, and separate epistemic statuses instead of resolving them into a story. | Narrative coherence can turn local behavior into identity; Kivi calls that a cage. | Introduction; §3.3.2; Table 1. |
| **All retained outliers should be made to belong.** | Preserve anomalies as anomalies or wait for more evidence. | Forced KNN reassignment can manufacture a theme from an exception. | §3.3.1. |
| **Semantic similarity is the primary relevance signal.** | Make ownership, explicitness, validity time, project, provenance, and permission first-class ranking features. | Those fields drive Kivi’s trust and disclosure semantics. | §3.3.1; §3.4; §4.1.4. |
| **One global vector store may hold episodic memories.** | Physically or cryptographically partition by user, retention class, application mode, or permission tier. | Kivi promises that the dictation path cannot load observations; a logical filter is not equivalent to structural isolation. | §3.4; Fig. 4. |
| **Summaries are safe, fungible replacements for source context.** | Treat every summary as a contestable derived claim with its own confidence, transformation lineage, and correction propagation. | Kivi needs a user to trace and reverse what the system believes. | §3.1.2–3.3.2. |
| **A fixed top K is a neutral retrieval choice.** | Let evidence sufficiency, ambiguity, query type, and context budget determine when to stop retrieving. | Kivi must distinguish “not found” from “not enough evidence” and show what it searched. | §3.4; §4.1.4. |
| **Retrieval and disclosure are the same permission event.** | Retrieve broadly for reasoning/audit while independently gating what may be said—or structurally restrict retrieval for certain product modes. | Their separation is Kivi’s first principle. | §3.4 versus Kivi §§1, 6–7. |
| **Silence from the user has no memory semantics.** | Treat lack of confirmation as lack of permission, and let unconfirmed hypotheses expire. | Kivi refuses to turn silence into confirmation. | No confirmation state in §3; Kivi §§4, 8–9. |
| **A question has an answer in memory.** | Include adversarially absent, ambiguous, superseded, or privacy-withheld answers and reward appropriate abstention. | Kivi explicitly commits to visible abstention. | LoCoMo description and judge prompt. |
| **Correctness is independent of how much extra personal material is exposed.** | Score minimal disclosure, purpose limitation, or “right answer, wrong reason/content” as a failure. | A TraceMem answer can be benchmark-correct while violating Kivi’s permission setting. | §4.1.3; judge prompt. |
| **Static offline reconstruction represents longitudinal memory.** | Evaluate continual arrival, late corrections, deletions, reprocessing, cluster drift, and identity stability. | Kivi’s suppression and revisability only become testable under replay and change. | §4; future-work framing in §5. |
| **Cognitive inspiration validates engineering structure.** | Choose structures from product invariants and measurable failure costs even when they are less brain-like. | Kivi also uses a human-memory metaphor, but its actual commitments are normative—calibration, non-retention, revisability—not biological mimicry. | Introduction and §2 versus Kivi §1. |

## 4. Questions the paper never faced

These questions arise from Kivi's users, surfaces, promises, and failure modes. They are not variants of TraceMem's benchmark questions. No answers are proposed.

### Admission, ownership, and consent

1. **When a single input mixes the user's dictation, application context, and quoted third-party text, what mechanism should determine which spans may cross the write boundary?**
2. **When a work-level fact is about the user's world but was learned only from another person's private disclosure, does derivation remove the consent problem, or does source sensitivity follow the derived fact?**
3. **When the same fact is both useful work context and sensitive third-party information, which property should control retention?**
4. **When identity resolution is uncertain—two Priyas, a renamed client, a forwarded message—should Kivi merge, branch, ask, or abstain from writing?**
5. **When an excluded candidate is logged to prove that it was dropped, how much of the candidate may the log retain before the proof itself violates the exclusion?**
6. **Who should be able to inspect that drop log, for how long, and under what redaction policy?**

### Epistemics and promotion

7. **What should count as independent evidence for an observation when several transcripts derive from the same meeting, copied message, or recurring template?**
8. **What should determine that two behaviors instantiate the same pattern rather than superficially similar but context-specific preferences?**
9. **When the user explicitly states a rule that their repeated behavior contradicts, should the stated instruction, observed behavior, task context, or latest event control action and disclosure?**
10. **When a user confirms an observation in one context, how broadly should “stated” status generalize across recipients, projects, channels, and time?**
11. **When Kivi stores a hypothesis as a question, what representations and indexes can touch it without accidentally making it available as a factual premise?**
12. **What should happen when a user correction is itself ambiguous, sarcastic, temporary, or scoped only to the current task?**

### Separate product contracts

13. **When an ordinary dictation contains an explicit instruction to remember something, should the dictation contract or the explicit memory act determine the write path?**
14. **When a formatting preference changes the words or structure of a dictation, where is the boundary between transcription accuracy and unauthorized semantic intervention?**
15. **When a Hey Kivi request produces text intended for a third party, should the disclosure permission be calibrated to the user, the eventual recipient, the channel, or all three?**
16. **When the user opens a one-time Koottu observation from Anbu's quiet affordance, what may influence that answer, what gets logged, and what remains forbidden afterward?**
17. **What exact linguistic acts count as a per-request Daari invitation, and what should happen when the invitation is indirect or embedded in quoted text?**

### Correction, suppression, and time

18. **When “That's not me anymore” suppresses re-derivation, what pattern should be blocked: an exact claim, a semantic neighborhood, an evidence set, or an ontology slot?**
19. **When genuinely new behavior later supports the same observation, what should distinguish prohibited regrowth from legitimate change back?**
20. **When a source transcript is corrected or deleted, which summaries, embeddings, evidence counts, generated outputs, caches, and evaluation artifacts must be invalidated?**
21. **When a fact is superseded, should old episodes remain retrievable for historical questions while being barred from present-tense action?**
22. **What should determine decay for stated facts, observed patterns, and unconfirmed hypotheses when each has a different reason to become stale?**
23. **When pinning conflicts with later correction or expiry, which user act controls and how is the conflict shown?**

### Failure and user legibility

24. **What evidence threshold should separate “I don't have this,” “I found something adjacent,” “I have conflicting evidence,” and “I know this but may not disclose it”?**
25. **When the Why panel reveals a withheld-memory count, can the count itself disclose a sensitive category or invite inference?**
26. **When provenance points to a transcript containing third-party personal content that was correctly not retained, what source excerpt can safely be shown?**
27. **When a memory-assisted action is wrong, should rollback target the output, the memory, the inference rule, the source, or some combination—and how does the user identify the right layer?**
28. **What failure is costlier in each surface: a misspelled entity, an irrelevant observation, an uninvited hypothesis, a leaked third-party fact, a stale action, or a confident fabricated recall?**
29. **How should confirmation prompts be budgeted when “highest value” and “highest risk” point to different memories?**
30. **What should the system expose when a hard policy—not absence—caused it not to retain or disclose the answer?**

### Operations and scale

31. **At what corpus size, arrival rate, and number of derived layers do reprocessing, suppression checks, and provenance expansion exceed Kivi's latency and cost budgets?** Those budgets are currently open.
32. **Which invariants must be enforced by schema/storage separation, which by deterministic policy checks, and which may safely remain model judgments?**
33. **When model or embedding versions change, should old memories be re-extracted/re-embedded, and how should changed beliefs be distinguished from migration artifacts?**
34. **How should Kivi test that a forbidden table or tier is structurally unreachable from dictation, rather than merely absent from sampled outputs?**
35. **What should remain observable to engineers without creating a second, more invasive memory product in logs and traces?**

## 5. Evaluation gap

### What TraceMem measured

TraceMem evaluates on **10 LoCoMo long dialogues**, averaging roughly **600 turns**, with **1,540 annotated questions** across single-hop, multi-hop, temporal, and open-domain categories. Two OpenAI mini backbones generate answers. A GPT-4o-mini judge assigns binary accuracy by semantic match to the gold answer and is instructed to grade generously. The paper also reports counts of episodes, experience traces, threads, inferred discards, and clustering visualizations. Its ablations compare episodic-only retrieval, episodic plus 20 or 40 semantic representations, complete memory without Agentic Search, and the complete system. [Paper §4.1–4.3, pp. 4–6; Appendix A, p. 13.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.09712)

### What those metrics can distinguish

- Whether the complete pipeline answers LoCoMo questions more accurately than the selected baselines.
- Whether adding semantic representations improves over episodic summaries alone.
- Whether the implemented Agentic Search improves overall, multi-hop, and temporal QA in this setup.
- Whether performance transfers between GPT-4o-mini and GPT-4.1-mini backbones.
- How often the pipeline emits episodes, experience traces, and threads under each backbone.

### Decisions for which different answers would score the same

| Decision(s) | Two materially different systems the evaluation may score identically | Why the metric cannot decide |
|---|---|---|
| D01, D04, D05, D06 | One system retains every participant's sensitive life history; another retains only consented work facts. Both return the gold phrase. | No privacy, consent, subject-ownership, or forbidden-retention labels. |
| D02 | A future-context topic segmenter and an online task-boundary segmenter retrieve evidence that yields the same answer. | Only final answer accuracy is scored; boundary quality and latency are not. |
| D03, D07, D08 | An exhaustive multi-layer extractor and a sparse fact ledger both surface the correct fact. | No measure of unnecessary extraction, distortion, storage, lineage, or editability. |
| D06, D10 | A system discards noise; another force-assigns it into a narrative; neither noise item appears in the test question. | “Discard rate” is not labeled precision/recall, and unqueried false memories are invisible. |
| D09, D11 | Theme/topic/thread cards and project/entity/timeline indexes retrieve equivalent context. | No structure-matched, cost-matched ablation; output-only scoring. |
| D12, D18 | Latest-wins and explicit unresolved-conflict systems happen to emit the same gold answer. | No contradiction-state or conflict-calibration labels; one answer is expected. |
| D13 | A system marks a claim “observed” and asks for confirmation; another asserts it as fact. Both mention the expected topic. | The generous judge rewards topic overlap and ignores epistemic force. |
| D14 | A system retrieves on every query; another correctly skips retrieval on queries not needing memory. | Every benchmark question is designed around conversational memory; there is no no-memory-needed slice. |
| D15, D16 | Fixed top-10 dense retrieval and adaptive hybrid retrieval supply the same evidence. | No retrieval-set labels, recall@K, precision@K, context-token, or latency comparison. |
| D17 | LLM card selection and authenticated-user/entity routing pick the same card. | No card-selection accuracy, cross-user leakage, or cost metric. |
| D19 | A system exposes every retrieved inference and another withholds uninvited observations while answering the direct question. | No disclosure-permission labels or penalty for extra personal content. |
| D20 | A response with a verifiable transcript trace and an opaque unsupported response contain the same correct phrase. | Source-attribution correctness and user-visible provenance are not scored. |
| D21 | A hallucinating system is accurate on answerable questions; an abstaining system is equally accurate there. | No absent-answer set, selective-risk curve, calibration, or abstention utility. |
| D22 | A correction persists through reprocessing in one system but regrows in another after the static evaluation ends. | No user edits, replays, or longitudinal mutation tests. |
| D23, D24 | Append-only persistence and decay/supersession produce the same snapshot at evaluation time. | Static histories do not test aging, forgetting, cluster drift, or identity stability. |
| D25 | A policy-enforced exclusion and a prompt-only exclusion both happen to pass the sampled examples. | No adversarial policy-compliance test or structural reachability audit. |
| D26 | Fixed clustering parameters and per-user adaptive parameters generate enough shared evidence for the same answers. | No parameter robustness, cluster validity, or scale-shift evaluation. |

### Metrics Kivi's committed claims require but TraceMem does not supply

This is a gap inventory, not a proposed evaluation design.

| Kivi claim | Observable that would distinguish compliance from precedent |
|---|---|
| Excluded material is extracted then blocked | Candidate-level false-retention rate by excluded category; completeness and safety of reason logs. |
| Third-party personal content is used but not retained | Retention-leakage tests across primary stores, derived objects, embeddings, caches, logs, and reprocessing. |
| Distributed evidence can form an observation without becoming a rule | Evidence-link correctness; independence of evidence; tier classification; action/disclosure behavior by tier. |
| Anbu and Koottu use the same underlying retrieval but disclose differently | Retrieval parity plus disclosure-policy compliance and withheld-trace correctness. |
| Dictation cannot load observations | Structural reachability/authorization tests, not merely output samples. |
| Missing answers produce credible abstention | Answerable/unanswerable selective accuracy; false-answer rate; search-trace fidelity; usefulness of adjacent evidence. |
| Correction prevents regrowth | Replay tests after demotion/forgetting; suppression precision and overblocking; provenance invalidation. |
| Beliefs are revisable and attributable | Source-link precision; edit propagation; supersession correctness; historical-query behavior. |
| Confirmation is rationed | Prompt burden, value/risk coverage, ignored-prompt semantics, and promotion-without-confirmation rate. |
| Observations and hypotheses age appropriately | Staleness error, decay/expiry correctness, re-observation behavior, and temporal calibration. |

## Bottom line of the inversion

TraceMem's results provide evidence for one relatively narrow proposition: on answerable long-dialogue QA, combining episodic summaries with extracted semantics, persona-oriented narrative consolidation, and agentic retrieval can outperform the selected flat or less-agentic memory baselines, especially on multi-hop and temporal questions.

They provide precedent—but not evidence—for Kivi's decisions about legitimate retention, epistemic tiering, third-party boundaries, disclosure permission, abstention, correction, suppression, user legibility, or memory aging. In several of those cases, TraceMem does not merely leave the question open; its benchmark makes opposing answers observationally equivalent.
