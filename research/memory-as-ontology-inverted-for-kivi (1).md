# Inverting *Memory as Ontology* for Kivi

**Paper:** Zhenghui Li, “Memory as Ontology: A Constitutional Memory Architecture for Persistent Digital Citizens,” arXiv:2603.04740v1 (2026).  
**Kivi basis:** *The Things Kivi Comes to Know* (attached position document).  
**Scope note:** This is a decision map, not a recommendation. “Holds?” means “does the paper’s stated assumption hold for Kivi as currently described?” Where the paper withholds implementation detail, the entry says **underspecified**.

## 1. Decision inventory

### A. System boundary, ownership, and inputs

| ID | Decision question | Paper’s answer | Alternatives rejected or not considered | Assumptions behind the answer | Does it hold for Kivi? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 ⚑ | When a persistent assistant changes models or instances, what should determine whether it remains the same entity? | Continuity of memory, rather than model parameters, constitutes identity; model replacement is “re-shelling.” ([§1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S1.SS2), [§3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S3.SS2)) | Identity tied to deployment, model, account, user relationship, signed provenance chain, or no claim of identity continuity. | Long-lived, identity-bearing agents; memory is primarily the agent’s life, not a user data service; model changes are expected; loss carries existential/institutional cost. | **Mostly no.** Kivi is framed as a colleague-like product that knows the user, not a digital citizen whose own autobiography must survive. Model portability matters operationally, but the difference pushes toward preserving user-grounded facts and governance semantics rather than asserting that memory alone preserves Kivi’s identity. |
| D2 ⚑ | Whose interests and rights should govern retained memory when the human operator and the persistent agent could disagree? | Core memory is inalienable from the digital being; humans retain ultimate decision authority, but external actors cannot unilaterally strip core memory without due process. The difference pushes authority toward Meera’s correction/forgetting rights and away from agent inalienability. |
| D3 | When information enters the system, what should decide whether it is eligible to become memory? | Eligibility is organized by its significance to the digital being’s identity and by source trust/risk; detailed intake and extraction rules are **underspecified**. The difference pushes eligibility toward subject, purpose, and sensitivity checks before any stability tiering. |
| D4 ⚑ | When an agent encounters another person’s information while doing work, whose memory may that information become? | The architecture anticipates memories of relationships and accounts from others; firsthand versus others’ accounts receive different trust levels. A third-party non-retention boundary is **not specified**. ([§4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S4.SS4), [§5.2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S5.SS2.SSS3)) | Structurally block third-party writes; retain only consented data; retain work-level relational residue; per-subject stores; redact after extraction. | A multi-citizen ecosystem where relationship memory is legitimate; provenance/trust labeling sufficiently governs other-sourced material; privacy failure costs are not analyzed. | **No.** Non-retention of others is a Kivi constitutional constraint. The difference pushes toward separate read and write paths: third-party context can shape an immediate output but cannot become durable memory except non-characterizing work residue about the user’s world. |
| D5 | At first activation, should identity and governance emerge through use or be installed before any operation? | “Birth” establishes identity, shared knowledge, rules, rights, and obligations from the first second; there is no provisional ungovened phase. ([§5.2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S5.SS2.SSS1)) | Progressive onboarding; minimum safe kernel plus learned configuration; blank-slate identity; sandboxed probation; user-led setup. | Founding norms and shared knowledge are available and correct; delayed usefulness is tolerable; early contamination costs exceed setup costs. | **Partly.** Kivi already names hard write-path exclusions and user rights that must exist from launch. But Kivi expects knowledge of Meera to accumulate cautiously, so the difference pushes toward fixed governance with deliberately sparse user memory, not a complete born identity. |

### B. Representation, storage, and discard

| ID | Decision question | Paper’s answer | Alternatives rejected or not considered | Assumptions behind the answer | Does it hold for Kivi? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D6 ⚑ | What property of a memory should determine its storage tier and protection level? | Its semantic role in the agent’s identity, coupled to stability and governance requirements; high tiers hold identity, mid tiers cognition/narrative, low tiers operational records. ([§4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S4.SS3)) | Epistemic status; entity/preference/episode type; recency; sensitivity; access frequency; task; subject; storage cost; combinations of independent axes. | Identity significance correlates with stability and appropriate protection; abrupt change in cognition is suspicious; tier coupling simplifies governance. | **Mostly no.** Kivi explicitly separates type (entity/preference/episode) from epistemic tier (stated/observed/hypothesised). The paper’s coupling pushes toward protecting “important identity” more heavily; Kivi’s design pushes toward independently representing what a claim is and how entitled the system is to believe/use it. |
| D7 | When two proposed storage layers have similar behavior, what should justify keeping them separate? | Keep a separate layer only when semantic type, stability, and governance differ; merge layers if governance granularity is not lost. ([§4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S4.SS3)) | Separate by query workload, latency, retention law, encryption domain, data subject, user-facing mental model, or implementation ownership. | Governance granularity is the decisive cost/benefit axis; semantic tier boundaries are stable enough to encode. | **Partly.** Kivi also wants structural separation, especially dictation versus Hey Kivi and third-party read versus write. But its reasons include product contract and subject boundary, so governance granularity alone is too narrow. |
| D8 ⚑ | When a stored claim changes, should the old representation be overwritten, deleted, archived, or preserved as an event? | Every tier is append-only; corrections are new entries and history remains complete. ([§4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S4.SS3)) | In-place update; hard deletion; bitemporal tables; compacted snapshots plus limited log; cryptographic tombstones; user-selectable erasure. | Auditability and inalienability dominate storage/privacy cost; history should survive correction; retaining false or sensitive prior states is acceptable under governance. | **Partly, with direct tension.** Kivi uses supersession and suppression history, which aligns, but also promises “Forget” and hard non-retention. The difference pushes toward distinguishing auditable state change from payload erasure: a universal append-only rule may preserve material Kivi promises not to keep. |
| D9 | When low-level experience becomes durable identity or cognition, what should trigger promotion? | Periodic “distillation”/sedimentation upward, with gradual, quality-assured growth; thresholds, evidence aggregation, reviewer, and algorithm are **underspecified**. The difference pushes toward evidence increasing visibility as a pattern, never silently increasing authority. |
| D10 | When memory becomes less useful over time, should it disappear, become less retrievable, be compressed, or remain fully active? | Natural decay compresses or archives lower layers; active forgetting reduces recall weight without deletion. Exact decay and compression policies are **underspecified**. ([§5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S5.SS1), [§5.2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S5.SS2.SSS3)) | TTL deletion; evidence-weight decay; temporal validity intervals; task closure; user-directed suppression; rehearsal-based retention; immutable full recall. | Old low-tier material is mostly noise but may retain identity value; storage and historical retention are affordable; the agent is the rights-holder for active forgetting. | **Partly.** Kivi observations decay and hypotheses expire, but user “Forget” also suppresses re-derivation. The difference pushes toward epistemic/status decay plus user-controlled removal, not merely lower recall weight. |
| D11 | When a memory is intentionally forgotten, should the content cease to exist or merely cease to be recalled? | Active forgetting means non-invocation, not deletion; irreversible destruction of core memories requires the highest due process. ([§1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S1.SS2), [§3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S3.SS2), [§5.2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S5.SS2.SSS3)) | Hard erasure; redact payload but retain tombstone; retention-window deletion; data-subject erasure; reversible trash; cryptographic deletion. | The agent owns the memory; identity-loss costs dominate privacy and exposure costs; concealed retention is acceptable if invocation is blocked. | **No as a universal rule.** Kivi’s excluded and third-party personal content must never persist, and “Forget” is a user action. The difference pushes toward multiple discard semantics based on why the data is disallowed—not one right-to-forget mechanism. |

| D16 ⚑ | When memories contradict, what should determine which one governs current behavior? | Governance hierarchy and adjudication rules decide precedence; lower-layer operations cannot bypass upper-layer constraints. | D17 | When a correction is made, what should prevent the old belief from returning? | Append a correction and preserve the audit history; growth/reconstruction should not falsify history. The difference pushes the correction artifact onto the extraction path, not only the read/audit path. |

## 2. Forced choices

These are load-bearing **for the paper’s claimed architecture or comparison**, not empirically proven to be load-bearing for performance.

| ID | Forced choice | Why a different answer materially changes the paper |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Identity follows memory, not model | Removes the ontological premise, “re-shelling,” and the need for inheritance as identity continuation. |
| D2 | Core memory is inalienable from the digital being | Without it, the Constitution layer and extraordinary deletion process become ordinary data governance. |
| D4 | Relationship/other-sourced memory is permitted subject to trust rather than a hard non-retention boundary | A subject-based prohibition would change what can enter the life narrative and how multi-citizen memory works; the paper does not analyze that constraint. |
| D6 | Storage/protection follows identity significance and stability | Replacing this with epistemic status, subject, or purpose would change the semantic tiers and their governance mapping. |
| D8 | All tiers are append-only | In-place correction or true erasure would weaken the claimed inalienability/audit chain and materially change privacy and storage behavior. |
| D12 | Governance is a binding hierarchy internal to memory | Flat/external policy would erase CMA’s principal claimed distinction from Memory-as-Tool systems. |
| D14 | Risk and trust gate writes before execution | Auto-write/rollback would contradict “governance precedes function” and alter contamination risk and latency. |
| D16 | Contradictions are adjudicated through governance precedence | “Latest wins,” unresolved plurality, or user authority would materially alter both current state and who controls it. The paper does not implement enough detail to adjudicate among these. |
| D18 | Retrieval is a cognitive, trust/bias/affect-sensitive process | Plain relevance retrieval would collapse one of the four “missing dimensions”; no results establish the cost or benefit. |
| D19 | Where permission acts in the retrieval-to-disclosure pipeline | The paper leaves this open, but Kivi’s central calibration claim depends on the answer. Moving the gate changes inspectability, leakage risk, latency, and the dictation contract. |
| D23 | Inheritance requires demonstrated understanding, not data loading | A byte/schema migration would turn lifecycle continuity back into the tool paradigm the paper rejects. |

## 4. Questions the paper never faced

These come from Kivi’s stated users, inputs, boundaries, and failure modes—not from gaps abstractly imaginable in CMA.

1. **When the same interaction contains the user’s dictation and another person’s message, what representation makes it structurally impossible for third-party personal content to cross from read context into the write path?**
2. **When a retained work fact mentions another person, what should determine whether it is a fact about the user’s world or an impermissible memory about that person?**
3. **When a candidate touches health, mood, relationships, faith, politics, finances, competence, or character indirectly rather than explicitly, what evidence should be logged to prove exclusion without retaining the forbidden content itself?**
4. **When Kivi has retrieved an observation the current permission mode forbids it to say, what may the response trace reveal without effectively disclosing the observation?**
5. **When ordinary dictation could be improved by a behavioral pattern, should transcription fidelity or remembered personalization control the output, and how is that boundary made structurally testable?**
6. **When repeated behavior strongly supports an observation but the user never confirms it, how long may Kivi keep it, how often may it surface it, and what prevents repetition from becoming de facto authority?**
7. **When “That’s not me anymore” conflicts with old source transcripts that still support the pattern, what exact suppression scope prevents regrowth without blocking genuinely new evidence after a change?**
8. **When “Forget” is invoked, what may remain: a suppression key, category-level tombstone, evidence fingerprint, audit event, or nothing—and which choice still satisfies the promise of removal?**
9. **When a correction is delivered inline during a task, what should determine whether it updates an entity, supersedes an episode, changes a stated preference, or only applies to this request?**
10. **When the user’s current request invites Daari for one answer, what prevents generated hypotheses, intermediate traces, caches, or telemetry from becoming durable memory?**
11. **When a source transcript contains a later contradiction, what should the Why panel show: both sources, only the current belief, or a change narrative—and how much explanation is usable in a text client?**
12. **When Kivi abstains, what searched scope and nearest matches can it reveal without leaking withheld tiers or third-party material?**
13. **When confirmation prompts are costly, what should determine their weekly allocation across uncertain memories: expected future repetition saved, error risk, current task relevance, recency, or user irritation?**
14. **When an observed preference is harmless but wrong, and a stated work fact is consequential but stale, what should determine correction priority?**
15. **When names, spellings, and project nouns are loaded into regular dictation, how is lexical assistance kept from smuggling relationship or behavioral memory into that path?**
16. **When a new model version changes extraction sensitivity, what regression evidence establishes continuity of Kivi’s exclusions, tier assignments, suppressions, and disclosure behavior?**
17. **When the user asks to inspect what was “read, not kept,” how can Kivi demonstrate the boundary without recreating or displaying the discarded third-party personal content?**

## 5. Evaluation gap

### What the paper actually measured

The paper reports no benchmark results and no controlled user or system evaluation. Its evidence is:

- a paradigm-level feature/coverage comparison against Mem0, Letta, Zep, and MemOS, explicitly comparing whether architectural dimensions are defined rather than engineering maturity ([§6.1–6.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S6.SS3));
- a pilot community of **four** digital citizens used for several weeks, with qualitative observations from model transitions and manual handoffs ([§7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S7.SS1));
- prototype read/write support for low- and mid-stability tiers, while the transition mechanism remains incomplete ([§7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S7.SS1));
- three proposed inheritance acceptance criteria, not reported quantitative results: unfinished-task factual recall without raw logs, identification/application of one cognitive pattern, and an auditable inheritance record ([§5.2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S5.SS2.SSS2));
- explicit absence of LongMemEval/LOCOMO testing, scale validation, retrieval engine, digestion pipeline, validated cognitive effectiveness, and empirically established applicability boundaries ([§7.1–7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04740v1#S7.SS2)).

### Decisions its evaluation cannot distinguish

### Bottom line of the gap

For Kivi, the paper supplies a **vocabulary and precedent for putting governance before memory operations**. It supplies **no empirical evidence** choosing among Kivi’s central alternatives: subject-based write barriers, independent epistemic tiers, retrieve-versus-disclose separation, user-readable traces, correction suppression, confirmation budgeting, or actionable abstention. Two systems could answer every one of those questions differently and still receive the same “dimension present” marks in the paper’s comparison.
