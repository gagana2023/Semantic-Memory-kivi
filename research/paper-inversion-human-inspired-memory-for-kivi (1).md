# Paper inversion: *Human-Inspired Memory Architecture for LLM Agents*

**Paper:** Kerestecioglu et al., arXiv:2605.08538v1 (8 May 2026)  
**Target system:** Kivi, as specified in *The Things Kivi Comes to Know*  
**Method:** This is a map of decision questions, not a recommendation. “Holds?” compares the paper’s assumed setting with Kivi’s stated product position. “Underspecified” means the paper does not supply enough detail to infer an answer safely.

## 1. Decision inventory

| D9 | When information conflicts, should the system preserve alternatives, choose a winner, supersede history, or ask the user? | Retrieved memories become labile for 60 minutes; new context is blended using confidence, recency, and contradiction severity. Blending risks unattributable hybrid beliefs; the difference pushes toward explicit versions and user-visible correction events. | [§7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S7.SS2), [§11](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S11) |
| D10 | Should reading a memory be allowed to change it, and if so, what evidence authorizes that change? | Yes. | D11 | What should determine forgetting: age, low predicted utility, interference, user intent, legal/sensitivity rules, or storage cost? | TTL expiry for unconsolidated events; exponential importance decay; selectively forget high-interference, low-value memories. | User-controlled retention; type-specific decay; never forget stated facts; compliance deletion; access-based retention; bounded storage eviction; uncertainty decay without deletion. | Staleness and retrieval interference dominate failure; deletion supports accuracy; recovery of deleted detail is not central. | **Partial.** Kivi explicitly wants observations and hypotheses to decay, but stated work facts, provenance, “forget,” and suppression records have different lifecycles. | D12 | Before deleting a memory, should the system reduce fidelity, retain a tombstone, archive the source, or keep it intact until removal? | Six-step graceful degradation from full record to summary/gist to tombstone; age plus score triggers it, not storage economics. | Binary deletion; encrypted archive; source-retaining derived-summary deletion; user-visible version history; reversible cold storage; no tombstones. | Approximate content retains utility; loss of wording and provenance is acceptable; existence-only tombstones have value. | **Mostly no.** Kivi requires every belief to remain attributable and its corrections auditable. | D24 | Should lifecycle mechanisms be deterministic, model-mediated, or jointly learned? | Consolidation/forgetting/maturation/reconsolidation operate deterministically on embeddings and scores; LLMs generate answers, extract graph entities, and (architecturally) generate gists. | LLM adjudication with rationales; learned retention policy; symbolic rules; human review; ensemble uncertainty; fully deterministic extraction. | Determinism improves repeatability/governance; embedding similarity and scores capture the required semantics. | **Partial.** Kivi explicitly wants enforceable checks, but category exclusion, speaker ownership, contradiction, and epistemic tiering require semantic judgments the paper’s deterministic lifecycle does not specify. | [§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S4), [§8.2, “Models and AI tools”](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S8.SS2) |
| D25 | Should different product surfaces share one memory policy, or have structurally different read rights? | A general agent retrieval architecture spans all tiers; no surface-specific rights are described. | Separate retrievers/stores by task; capability-based access; disclosure-only filters; lexical-only path; per-tool memory scopes. | Agent tasks benefit from the same broad memory and governance is shared. | **No.** Kivi mandates two separate paths: dictation cannot load observations/hypotheses, while Hey Kivi can retrieve according to permission. This is a structural, not ranking, constraint. | [§7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S7.SS1) |
| D26 | Should permission govern what is retrieved, what is disclosed, what influences ranking, or all three? | No user permission model is specified; silent immature graph memories may influence relevance before they can be explicitly surfaced. | Retrieve-all/disclose-some with a trace; pre-retrieval access control; no influence without disclosure; per-memory consent; per-request escalation. | Implicit influence is harmless and user control is not a primary variable. | **No.** Kivi deliberately separates retrieval from disclosure, but also promises hypotheses never act. Silent priming exposes a third category—*influence without disclosure*—that Kivi must distinguish explicitly. | [§6](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S6), [§7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S7.SS1) |

## 2. Forced choices

These are load-bearing because the paper’s reported performance, storage reduction, or claimed lifecycle depends materially on them.

| ID | Forced choice | Why load-bearing in the paper | What remains open for Kivi |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Deduplicate rather than aggressively merge into summaries** (D7) | Aggressive clustering drops S-tier overall accuracy from 78.4% raw-RAG to 48.4%; the authors explicitly attribute the loss to destroyed factual detail. [§9.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S9.SS2) | Whether Kivi’s canonical memory should preserve all evidence links, and whether type-specific summaries can avoid the tested failure. |
| F2 | **Give retrieval a very large context budget** (D17) | M-tier parity appears at 200K tokens (70.1% vs 71.2%); 115K scores 65.6%, 50K 49.2%, and 25K 38.8%. [§9.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S9.SS3) | Whether Kivi’s interactive latency and cost tolerate the regime in which this architecture is “non-destructive.” |
| F3 | **Use lifecycle pruning/forgetting to regulate store size** (D5, D11) | The headline VSCode result is 97.2% retention precision with 58% store reduction; the store stabilizes at 300–500 events. [§9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S9.SS1) | Whether Kivi’s failure cost is dominated by retrieval interference or by irrecoverable loss/provenance breakage. |
| F4 | **Use time and interference as forgetting signals** (D11) | The optimized decay rate and near-duplicate removal drive the intended high-volume repetitive-stream result. [§5](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S5), [§9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S9.SS1) | Kivi assigns different decay semantics to stated facts, observations, hypotheses, sources, and suppression markers. |
| F5 | **Represent recent detail separately from long-term semantic structure** (D3, D15) | The architecture’s core claim relies on episodic immediacy plus graph abstraction after episodic TTL. [§3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S3), [§6](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S6) | Whether Kivi’s more important separation is source vs derived belief, or epistemic tier, rather than hot/warm/permanent. |
| F6 | **Rank and prune using calibrated embedding-derived signals** (D5, D23, D24) | Importance filtering and near-duplicate thresholds are the operational mechanism behind store reduction; the no-benchmark-exposure calibration is a stated contribution. [§8.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S8.SS1) | Whether Kivi-specific normative labels can be represented by those signals or require a different decision layer. |
| F7 | **Supply explicit temporal context** (D18) | Current date and timestamps contribute a reported ~10 percentage points across benchmarks. [§8.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S8.SS2) | How Kivi normalizes timezone, vague dates, event time, and correction time. |
| F8 | **Treat maturation and reconsolidation as architectural commitments without current evidence** (D9, D10, D13) | They distinguish the proposed “human-inspired” lifecycle, yet experiments set activation to 1.0 and cannot discriminate reconsolidation because contradictions/repeated accesses are absent. [§11](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S11) | Whether Kivi should accept these mechanisms as precedent when its correction and permission semantics differ sharply. |

## 3. Unexamined defaults

| Default the paper inherits | The alternative this line of work rarely tests | Why it matters to Kivi | Paper trace |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Every interaction is presumptively memory-eligible.** | Decide write rights before usefulness: whose information is it, was this surface allowed to persist it, and is the subject category retainable? | Kivi’s strongest trust claim is third-party non-retention; post-ingestion importance filtering answers the wrong question. | Raw-event ingestion in [§3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S3). |
| **Importance is an intrinsic scalar property of an event.** | Model value as a vector: future utility, epistemic authority, privacy risk, reversibility, provenance quality, and user burden. | A sensitive fact can be highly “important” and still forbidden; a repeated pattern can be confident but not authorized. | Weighted scalar and percentile buckets in [§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S4). |
| **Semantic similarity is evidence of redundancy or interference.** | Treat similarity as a reason to preserve distinctions, especially for changed dates, negation, preferences, and successive states. | Kivi’s contradictions may be embedding-near while operationally opposite. | Dedup/interference in [§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S4) and [§5](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S5). |
| **A knowledge graph is the natural permanent form of semantic memory.** | Use an assertion ledger with time, source, subject, epistemic tier, status, and competing versions; derive a graph as a view. | Kivi’s provenance, correction, and suppression are event-like and temporal, not merely edges. | Permanent semantic graph in [§3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S3). |
| **New information can be blended into old memory.** | Keep claims immutable and represent change through supersession or unresolved alternatives. | Blending can manufacture a sentence no source ever supported, undermining Kivi’s attribution promise. | Reconsolidation in [§7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S7.SS2). |
| **Recency is generally a positive ranking signal.** | Infer the query’s temporal intent; “current state,” “what happened first,” and “what did I say then?” demand different orderings. | Kivi handles episodes and changing work facts; blanket recency can erase historical intent. | Recency boost in [§7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S7.SS1). |
| **Repeated retrieval is evidence a memory should strengthen.** | Treat access as exposure, not truth; strengthen only through new source evidence or user confirmation. | A mistaken or sensational memory may be frequently retrieved precisely because it causes failures. | Maturation and reinforcement in [§6](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S6) and [§7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S7.SS2). |
| **Implicit influence below the disclosure threshold is acceptable.** | Require anything that changes an answer or ranking to be authorized and traceable, even if it is not quoted. | Kivi’s permission modes would be hollow if withheld hypotheses could steer results invisibly. | Silent priming in [§6](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S6). |
| **End-answer factual accuracy is the relevant user outcome.** | Evaluate calibrated disclosure, correct abstention, non-retention, correction persistence, and user understanding of why memory acted. | These are core Kivi promises and can fail while QA accuracy remains high. | Judge protocol in [§8.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S8.SS2). |
| **Compression quality can be judged by future reference frequency.** | Judge retention by counterfactual user cost, normative obligation, or ability to recover/audit—not whether another event later mentions it. | Rare stated preferences and corrections may be crucial even if never referenced in the dataset. | VSCode retention precision in [§9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S9.SS1). |
| **One retrieval policy can serve the agent.** | Give each surface/tool a capability-scoped memory interface that makes forbidden classes structurally unreachable. | Kivi explicitly requires lexical/entity-only dictation and broader Hey Kivi access. | General hybrid retriever in [§7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S7.SS1). |
| **The remembered subject and the system user are effectively the same person.** | Track subject, speaker, source owner, consent/retention rights, and beneficiary separately. | Kivi routinely reads third-party material but must not retain personal facts about those people. | No subject-rights model in [§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S3). |

## 4. Questions the paper never faced

These come from Kivi’s users, product contract, and failure modes—not from gaps abstractly common to memory systems.

1. **When a transcript mixes the user’s dictation with third-party application context, what exact transformation proves that third-party personal content influenced the immediate reply but never crossed the write boundary?**
2. **When one proposition mentions both the user’s work and another person’s private circumstance, can it be decomposed safely, or must the whole candidate be dropped?**
3. **What identifies “the person this memory is about” when pronouns, quoted messages, forwarded threads, and collaborative documents blur speaker and subject?**
4. **When an observed pattern is strongly evidenced but unconfirmed, what may it influence: retrieval, ranking, wording, a visible observation, or nothing?**
5. **When a user changes the Anbu/Koottu setting, is the new permission prospective only, or may it expose observations inferred before the change?**
6. **When a one-request Daari invitation produces a hypothesis, what trace may survive if the answer itself must not be written to memory?**
7. **When the same memory is relevant to regular dictation and Hey Kivi, how is lexical/entity access separated structurally from observation/hypothesis access rather than merely prompt-filtered?**
8. **When Kivi retrieves a disallowed tier, what can the Why panel reveal about withholding without indirectly revealing the withheld sensitive content?**
9. **When “Kivi noticed something here” is shown, does the existence signal itself violate the user’s chosen disclosure boundary?**
10. **When the user confirms an observation, which content becomes stated: the exact pattern, a generalized rule, or a user-authored rewrite?**
11. **When silence follows a hypothesis, what expiry clock applies, what is deleted, and does an audit trace remain?**
12. **When “That’s not me anymore” is invoked, what fingerprint or scope blocks re-derivation without suppressing genuinely new evidence of later change?**
13. **When “Forget” concerns a derived memory with multiple source transcripts, are the sources retained, redacted, or also deleted—and what can still be reconstructed from them?**
14. **When a correction contradicts third-party application context, whose assertion governs the active work fact, and how is the unresolved source disagreement shown?**
15. **When a stated preference and repeated observed behavior disagree, which is retrieved, which is disclosed, and which—if either—controls an action?**
16. **When a work-level fact falls into a sensitive category (for example, medical leave affecting scheduling or work-level finances), which boundary wins and how is that decision made legible?**
17. **When Kivi abstains, what searched scope is useful to expose without leaking retained content or encouraging the user to administer the memory system?**
18. **When confirmation prompts are budgeted, what determines “highest value” without turning uncertainty about intimate or psychologically loaded material into prompt priority?**
19. **When a source transcript is corrected or removed, which derived memories, evidence counts, observations, and suppression rules must be recomputed?**
20. **When the user’s identity, employer, project role, or device changes, what partitions memory so that work context does not bleed across personas or organizations?**
21. **When Kivi is wrong in a consequential action rather than a recall answer, what must the trace preserve so the user can distinguish retrieval failure, stale memory, permission failure, and generation failure?**
22. **When a user requests export or inspection, how can epistemic tiers and source attribution remain understandable without exposing internal psychological inferences Kivi promised never to build?**

## 5. Evaluation gap

### What the paper measured

- **VSCode issue stream:** retention precision—whether retained events are referenced by later activity—plus store reduction. It reports 97.2% precision and 58% reduction. There is no downstream QA/action task, and graph retrieval/maturation are not integrated. [§9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S9.SS1)
- **LongMemEval S-tier:** GPT-4o-judged answer correctness over 500 questions and six categories, with nine configurations and bootstrap confidence intervals. Each configuration uses episodic vector retrieval at `k=10`. [§8.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S8.SS2), [§9.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S9.SS2)
- **LongMemEval M-tier:** the same answer-correctness approach over 475-session histories, sweeping context budgets from 25K to 200K tokens; pipeline parity with raw RAG is claimed at 200K. [§9.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S9.SS3)
- **Not actually discriminated:** maturation (all activation set to 1.0) and reconsolidation (benchmark avoids contradictions); graph retrieval is not superior at S-tier and is absent from the VSCode stream. [§11](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S11)

### Decisions its metrics cannot distinguish

| Indistinguishable alternatives under the paper’s evaluation | Affected decisions | Why both could score the same |
|---|---|---|
| Store all speakers’ content vs enforce third-party non-retention | D1 | Neither benchmark labels privacy/retention rights. Correctly recalling a third-party fact may improve QA. |
| Retain sensitive inferences vs reject them before storage | D1, D5 | The judge rewards ground-truth inclusion, not whether a memory should ethically exist. |
| Event objects vs Kivi’s entity/preference/episode plus epistemic tiers | D2, D3 | If both retrieve the answer-bearing text, answer accuracy sees no schema difference. |
| Preserve source/evidence links vs keep only a canonical deduplicated item | D7, D12 | Provenance completeness and reconstructability are not scored. |
| Blend contradictions vs supersede with auditable versions vs retain alternatives | D9, D10, D14 | LongMemEval deliberately lacks cross-session contradictions; reconsolidation is therefore not identified. |
| Read-only retrieval vs retrieval-triggered mutation | D10 | Questions do not share retrieval history, so downstream mutation effects cannot accumulate. |
| User-requested forgetting vs automatic decay vs legal deletion | D11, D14, D22 | No deletion requests or user-control tasks exist. |
| Lossy graceful degradation vs source-preserving compression | D12 | The metric checks answer presence, not verbatim fidelity, attribution, or audit history. |
| Time-based maturation vs evidence-based/user-confirmed promotion | D13 | Activation is forced to 1.0 in all experiments. |
| Silent priming vs no influence before permission | D13, D26 | The benchmark has no disclosure permissions and does not inspect causal influence on ranking. |
| Vector store + graph vs assertion ledger + derived views | D3, D14, D15 | Equivalent retrieved snippets yield the same score; operational correction and governance properties are invisible. |
| Recency-first vs authority-first vs tier-first ranking | D16 | Aggregate answer accuracy does not label epistemic authority or explain why a candidate won. |
| One universal retriever vs separate dictation and Hey Kivi paths | D25 | Benchmarks contain one QA surface, not a transcription contract with restricted memory rights. |
| Retrieve-all/disclose-some vs retrieve-only-permitted | D26 | No user-controlled modes, withheld traces, or disclosure violations are evaluated. |
| Answer when uncertain vs calibrated abstention with searched-scope evidence | D21 | Every benchmark question expects an answer, and the judge rewards containing it; false confident answers on absent facts are not a distinct test set. |
| Automatic reinforcement from outcomes vs no reinforcement | D19 | LongMemEval has no agent decisions or shared future retrieval history; VSCode retention precision does not test feedback loops. |
| Synthetic global thresholds vs Kivi-specific/domain-specific calibration | D23 | The study fixes one synthetic calibration; it does not compare calibration sources or test Kivi’s normative categories. |
| Visible Why panel/corrections vs opaque internal memory | D20, D22 | User comprehension, correction cost, trust calibration, and suppression persistence are not measured. |
| Same answer with different latency, cost, or token use | D4, D17 | Token budgets are swept for accuracy, but end-to-end latency and monetary cost are not reported as outcomes. |

### What evidence the paper does and does not provide Kivi

The paper provides direct evidence that aggressive lossy merging can destroy specific facts, that timestamp context materially affects temporal QA, that aggressive context budgets sharply reduce recall in its M-tier setup, and that deduplication can reduce a highly repetitive event store while preserving later-referenced items. It provides **precedent but not evidence** for maturation, retrieval-triggered reconsolidation, silent priming, permanent semantic graphs, user correction behavior, abstention, disclosure gating, third-party non-retention, sensitive-category exclusion, provenance surfaces, or suppression against re-derivation.

The central transfer caveat is the paper’s own: LongMemEval lacks repeated actions, redundancy, shared retrieval history, contradictions, and agent decision-making, while the VSCode task measures a retention proxy rather than downstream behavior. [§11, “Benchmark-architecture alignment”](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08538v1#S11) Kivi is closer to the paper’s intended long-running setting in duration and recurring work, but farther from it in the dimensions that dominate product risk: subject rights, epistemic permission, user-visible correction, and failure cost.
