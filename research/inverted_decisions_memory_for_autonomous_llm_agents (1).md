# Inverting *Memory for Autonomous LLM Agents*

**Paper:** Pengfei Du, “Memory for Autonomous LLM Agents: Mechanisms, Evaluation, and Emerging Frontiers,” arXiv:2603.07670v1, 8 March 2026. [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07670)  
**Target setting:** Kivi, as specified in *The Things Kivi Comes to Know* (attached position document).  
**Method note:** This paper is a survey, not the report of one implemented system. “Paper answer” below means the survey’s own prescription where it gives one; otherwise it means the design pattern it presents as the practical default. Empirical results belong to the systems and benchmarks it cites. “Underspecified” means the survey does not settle the choice.

## 1. Decision inventory

## 2. Forced choices

These are load-bearing in the paper’s synthesis: changing them would materially alter either the cited results, the recommended architecture, or the survey’s central conclusions. They are also the places where Kivi cannot safely inherit precedent without making an explicit choice.

| Decision | Why it is load-bearing in the paper | Why Kivi has to think rather than copy |
|---|---|---|
| **F1 — External selective memory versus long context alone (D1, D7, D9, D20)** | The paper’s main conclusion depends on external stores plus selective retrieval. It cites MemoryArena’s drop from over 80% with active memory to about 45% for a long-context-only baseline (§2.4, p. 2) and argues that long context neither persists nor governs memory (§8, p. 10). | Kivi needs persistence and provenance, but the paper does not determine the authoritative representation, retention duration, or deletion/re-derivation semantics. |
| **F2 — What is written (D1–D3)** | The paper calls the write path part of the recursive policy-memory loop: one bad write can pollute many downstream steps (§2.1, p. 2). It also says storing everything degrades precision (§7.1, p. 9). | Kivi’s highest-cost error is not merely irrelevant retrieval; it is retaining sensitive or third-party material. Admission policy therefore carries product trust, privacy, and evaluation. |
| **F3 — Episode-to-semantics transition (D5–D6)** | The taxonomy’s four-layer integration depends on consolidation, while the paper calls this transition “particularly underserved,” fragile, and hard to validate (§3.1, p. 2). Reflective systems’ gains and failure modes both depend on it (§4.3, pp. 5–6). | This is exactly Kivi’s stated/observed boundary. Automatic promotion would collapse the product’s core epistemic distinction; never consolidating would remove Koottu/Daari value. |
| **F4 — Retrieval policy and ranking (D17–D20)** | The paper says the bottleneck shifts from storage to relevance at scale (§4.2, p. 3), and benchmark results show passive recall does not imply agentic use (§5.2–5.5, pp. 7–8). | Kivi adds tier, permission, provenance, and surface rights that generic relevance metrics do not encode. Whether these are filters, rank features, or separate retrieval paths changes behavior materially. |
| **F5 — Contradiction, staleness, and forgetting (D11–D14)** | The paper says most systems fail conspicuously at selective forgetting (§5.2, p. 7) and treats stale or contradictory memory as potentially worse than none (§2.3, pp. 2–3). | Kivi promises revisability. Newest-wins, decay, supersession, forgetting, and suppression are not interchangeable; each produces different histories and re-derivation behavior. |
| **F6 — Heuristic versus learned control (D16, D26)** | AgeMem’s reported advantage comes from making five memory operations RL actions (§4.5, p. 6), while the survey’s production recommendation is to delay that complexity (§7.6, pp. 9–10). | Deterministic exclusions and disclosure permissions must survive any optimizing controller. The boundary between model discretion and non-bypassable policy is architectural. |
| **F7 — Evaluation at the agent level (D23–D26)** | The paper’s central evaluation claim is that retrieval metrics cannot show correct use; LoCoMo-near-perfect systems fall to 40–60% on MemoryArena (§5.2 and §5.5, p. 7). | Kivi’s trust claims require measuring correct non-action, non-retention, withholding, abstention, correction durability, and user legibility—not only successful answers. |

## 3. Unexamined defaults

These are choices the survey often presents as normal engineering practice without fully treating them as contestable product decisions.

| Default | Alternative this line of work rarely tests | Why the default matters for Kivi | Paper location |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The agent is the subject of memory control.** Store/retrieve/discard are framed as operations the agent or its controller chooses. | The user owns a declarative memory constitution that the agent cannot override; some decisions are capabilities, not policy suggestions. | Kivi’s exclusions, surface isolation, and disclosure dial are constitutional constraints. | §3.3, pp. 2–3; §4.5, p. 6. |
| **Usefulness is the primary admission criterion.** Privacy is addressed later through controls. | Begin with relational entitlement: ask whether the system is entitled to retain the fact, then whether it is useful. | This reverses the order for third-party and sensitive content. | §7.1 versus §7.5, p. 9. |
| **A memory is fundamentally a record to retrieve.** | Treat memory as a claim plus evidence, scope, epistemic status, effective time, and permissions, with retrieval only as one projection. | Kivi’s editable beliefs and provenance need claim semantics, not only record similarity. | §3.2 and §4.2, pp. 2–4. |
| **Semantic similarity is the retrieval baseline.** Other signals rerank it. | Start from exact entities, temporal intervals, provenance, and causal/event links; use embeddings only for recall expansion. | Kivi’s small cast and structured types make exact relationships unusually important. | §4.2, pp. 3–4; §9.2, pp. 10–11. |
| **Recency generally increases truth or utility.** | Model validity intervals and context-specific coexistence; newer evidence need not supersede a durable rule. | “Priya is at Northwind now” is temporal; “client emails in prose” may persist. One decay model cannot serve both. | §4.3, p. 5; §7.3, p. 9. |
| **Importance can be self-assessed by the model.** | Let users pin importance, infer it only from downstream reliance/correction cost, or enforce category-level priority. | Model-assessed importance can privilege vivid personal inference over mundane but stated work facts. | §4.3, p. 5. |
| **Developer observability is the main explanation target.** | Make the same trace intelligible and actionable to the person represented by memory. | Kivi explicitly treats the Why panel and memory surface as the whole inspection story. | §7.7, p. 10. |
| **Deleting stored copies satisfies forgetting.** | Guarantee non-regeneration from surviving evidence via suppression, source redaction, or a negative constraint. | Kivi says corrections that regrow are a direct trust failure. | §7.5, p. 9; §9.4, p. 11. |
| **One generic agent loop covers interaction surfaces.** | Give transcription, command, recall, and mentoring surfaces distinct memory capabilities at the schema/API layer. | Kivi’s dictation contract requires structural separation from observation/hypothesis retrieval. | §2.1, p. 2; §7.2, p. 9. |
| **Task success is the top-level outcome.** Governance is a separate lower evaluation layer. | Treat policy compliance and calibrated non-action as co-equal success conditions that can veto task utility. | A perfectly drafted reply that retains a third party’s family health detail is a Kivi failure. | §5.4, p. 7. |
| **Memory of a user is an acceptable personal-assistant primitive.** | Remember only user-declared operational facts, or maintain task-local/project memory without a durable user model. | Kivi explicitly rejects psychological profiling and restricts memory to attributable work-level facts. | §6.1 and §6.8, p. 8 refer to user preferences and “profiles” without defining a boundary. |

## 4. Questions the paper never faced

Derived from Kivi’s stated users, text surfaces, trust promises, and failure modes—not from gaps in the literature alone.

1. **When a transcript mixes the user’s dictation with quoted application context from another person, what exact boundary determines which tokens may influence the immediate response, which may produce user-world residue, and which must never reach any durable log?**
2. **When a work-level fact concerns another person (“Priya is the Acme contact”), what makes it a fact about the user’s world rather than retained third-party data?**
3. **When exclusion filtering itself produces an audit log, how much rejected content may that log reveal before the proof of non-retention becomes a second retention channel?**
4. **When a candidate spans allowed and excluded content, should the system redact, generalize, split, or drop the entire candidate?**
5. **When the same utterance could be an entity fact, a preference, or an episode, who chooses the type, and how can a user correct a misclassification without rewriting the content?**
6. **When repeated behavior supports an observation, what minimum diversity of evidence prevents fourteen near-duplicate dictations from masquerading as fourteen independent instances?**
7. **When an observed preference conflicts with a stated preference, should the system surface the discrepancy, obey the stated rule silently, or ask for reconfirmation—and under which permission mode?**
8. **When “That’s not me anymore” suppresses re-derivation, what is the scope of suppression: exact wording, semantic pattern, source transcripts, time interval, or all future related evidence?**
9. **When later behavior genuinely re-establishes a suppressed pattern, what event, if any, may reopen it without violating the promise that corrections stick?**
10. **When Anbu retrieves an observation but may not disclose it, may that observation influence generation indirectly, or must it be excluded from the model context as well as the output?**
11. **When the UI says “Kivi noticed something here,” does revealing that an observation exists already exceed Anbu’s disclosure permission?**
12. **When Daari is invited for one request, what context proves the invitation’s scope, and what prevents its hypothesis from leaking into later answers, caches, traces, or extraction?**
13. **When regular dictation uses formatting preferences, which preferences count as lexical/formatting aids versus behavioral observations that the dictation store must be structurally unable to load?**
14. **When a user corrects a fact midway through a request, must the correction affect the current output, only future requests, or both—and what happens if synchronous updating would breach the latency budget?**
15. **When Kivi abstains, how much nearby memory can it reveal while explaining what it searched without disclosing an observation or third-party fact?**
16. **When the Why panel shows a source transcript, should quoted third-party context be redacted even if it was necessary evidence for the retained work-level residue?**
17. **When a memory is editable, does editing alter the claimed source, create a new user-stated memory, or preserve both the extracted and corrected versions?**
18. **When the user chooses Forget, must Kivi delete source transcripts, retain them but suppress extraction, or make a narrower claim-level deletion—and how is each promise explained honestly?**
19. **When confirmation prompts are capped weekly, what defines “highest value” without optimizing toward intrusive or emotionally salient observations?**
20. **When no speech recognition is in scope and transcripts are replayed, which memory failures are artifacts of the curated corpus rather than evidence about real dictation use?**
21. **When the product demonstrates a hard boundary on a fixed cast and corpus, what adversarial paraphrases, indirect references, and mixed allowed/disallowed facts must pass before the boundary is credible?**
22. **When an answer uses several memories across tiers, how should the trace distinguish retrieved, eligible, used, influential-but-not-cited, and withheld items in language a normal user can verify?**
23. **When the system is wrong, which failure is worse in Kivi’s product contract: failing to remember, remembering the wrong thing, disclosing something without permission, or retaining something it had no right to keep?**

## 5. Evaluation gap

### What the paper actually measured

The paper generated no primary data (“Data Availability Statement,” p. 12). It synthesizes reported results and proposes an evaluation stack:

- **LoCoMo:** factual QA, event summarization, and dialogue generation over long conversations (§5.2, p. 7).
- **MemBench:** factual versus reflective memory, participation versus observation, measured through effectiveness, operation count, and capacity degradation (§5.2, p. 7).
- **MemoryAgentBench:** accurate retrieval, test-time learning, long-range understanding, and selective forgetting (§5.2, p. 7).
- **MemoryArena:** end-to-end, interdependent multi-session tasks in web navigation, preference-constrained planning, progressive search, and formal reasoning (§5.2, p. 7).
- **Proposed four-layer stack:** task effectiveness; memory precision/recall, contradiction, staleness, and coverage; latency/tokens/calls/storage; privacy leakage, deletion compliance, and access-scope violations (§5.4, p. 7).

The survey itself also says current benchmarks do **not** systematically report efficiency, barely evaluate forgetting, and underexplore cross-session coherence (§5.5, pp. 7–8).

### Decisions the metrics cannot distinguish

### What evidence Kivi therefore does—and does not—get from this paper

The paper provides meaningful evidence that selective external memory, active management, agent-level evaluation, contradiction handling, operation logging, and cost measurement matter. It provides **precedent rather than evidence** for Kivi’s most distinctive decisions: third-party non-retention, hard sensitive-category exclusion, type × tier epistemics, disclosure separated from retrieval, structural dictation isolation, one-request hypothesis permission, user-legible traces, and suppression against re-derivation. Two implementations can differ on every one of those choices and still score the same on the surveyed benchmarks.

That is the central evaluation gap: the paper evaluates whether an agent can remember and use information; Kivi also needs to establish whether it had the right to retain, infer, retrieve, influence, disclose, revise, and regenerate that information.
