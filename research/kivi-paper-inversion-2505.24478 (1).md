# Inverting *Optimizing the Interface Between Knowledge Graphs and LLMs for Complex Reasoning*

**Paper:** V. Markovic et al., arXiv:2505.24478v1 (30 May 2025), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478)  
**Target setting:** Kivi, as specified in *The Things Kivi Comes to Know*: a persistent, user-facing semantic-memory system for work dictation and “Hey Kivi,” with calibrated disclosure, non-retention of third-party personal content, provenance, correction, supersession, decay, and explicit abstention.

## Reading note

This is an inversion of the paper, not an implementation recommendation. “Kivi fit” means whether the paper’s assumption holds in Kivi’s stated setting. “Underspecified” means the paper does not provide enough detail to recover a choice safely. The paper is a preliminary optimization study using 24 training and 12 hold-out questions per dataset; it is evidence that configuration matters, not a complete memory-system design ([§5, pp. 6–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=7)).

## 2. Forced choices

These are load-bearing in the paper: changing the answer would materially alter the reported result or what the experiment means.

| Flag | Why it is load-bearing in the paper | Why Kivi still has to think rather than copy |
|---|---|---|
| **F1 — D3: chunk boundary / size** | Chunk size is one of six optimized variables and high-performing configurations often shared chunk-size settings; it changes both extracted graph structure and retrieval granularity ([§4.2, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=6); [§6.3, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=10)). | Kivi’s privacy and event boundaries are not token boundaries, and the paper reports no per-decision effect sizes or winning values. |
| **F2 — D6: graph-extraction prompt/decomposition** | The prompt changes graph granularity and consistency, thereby changing the substrate later retrieved ([§4.2, pp. 5–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=6)). | Kivi must distinguish permissible facts, observations, and hypotheses; end-QA score cannot identify unsafe extraction. |
| **F3 — D10: summaries or no summaries** | Summary availability is explicitly in the search space and changes what the retriever can see ([§4.2, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=7)). | Summaries may sever claim-level provenance or preserve excluded content; neither is measured. |
| **F4 — D11: text versus graph retrieval** | Retriever type is central to the claimed KG–LLM interface and is among the settings shared by high-performing configurations ([§4.2, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=6); [§6.3, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=10)). | Kivi’s retrieval route is also an authorization boundary between dictation and Hey Kivi, a dimension absent from the paper. |
| **F5 — D12: top-k** | Top-k directly controls the evidence passed to generation and is tuned in every trial ([§4.2, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=6)). | A fixed count ignores tier, recency, evidence, and withheld-state costs. |
| **F6 — D15: output prompt/contract** | Much of the largest EM gain is attributable to shorter, benchmark-shaped answers rather than clearly better factual reasoning ([§6.1, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=9)). | Kivi’s user contract values useful traces, abstentions, and calibrated disclosures that may be punished by EM. |
| **F7 — D20: scalar optimization target** | The “best” configuration is defined entirely by the chosen metric; different metrics produce separate optimization experiments, and metric choice changes outcomes ([§4.1, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=5); [§6.3, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=10)). | Kivi has hard constraints and asymmetric harms that cannot be averaged into answer correctness without changing the product’s meaning. |
| **F8 — D21: task-specific configuration** | No single configuration wins across benchmarks; the paper’s conclusion depends on task-specific tuning rather than reuse ([§6.3, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=10)). | Kivi must decide the safe adaptation unit—surface, request type, or user—while preserving predictable permission semantics. |

The paper’s unreported per-trial configurations prevent a stronger claim about *which values* are load-bearing. It establishes sensitivity, but not an auditable causal ranking among the six parameters.

## 4. Questions the paper never faced

These come from Kivi’s users, constraints, and failure modes rather than from gaps in generic GraphRAG literature.

1. **When one interaction contains the user’s words and another person’s words, what should determine which derived claims may cross into durable memory?**
2. **When a work-level fact about a third party is entangled with their personal information, can the work residue be retained without preserving or making reconstructable the excluded content?**
3. **When a candidate touches a sensitive category, should ambiguity cause rejection, quarantine, redaction, or a user confirmation request—and what should be logged without itself retaining the sensitive claim?**
4. **When Kivi has retrieved an observation that the current permission mode forbids it to state, what trace may it expose without indirectly disclosing the observation?**
5. **When the same request is ordinary dictation versus an explicit Hey Kivi request, which memories should be technically unreachable rather than merely prompt-forbidden?**
6. **When a pattern is repeated many times but never confirmed, what should determine whether it remains an observation, decays, or is suppressed as unwanted?**
7. **When a user says “that’s not me anymore,” what exact scope should be blocked from re-derivation: the wording, the normalized claim, the evidence set, or a broader semantic family?**
8. **When “Forget” conflicts with auditability and proof that a correction sticks, what minimal suppression record may remain, and how can it avoid becoming the memory under another name?**
9. **When new evidence contradicts a stated memory, should Kivi silently supersede it, ask inline, retain time-scoped versions, or abstain from acting until resolved?**
10. **When a correction arrives inside a task request, what should determine whether it updates memory immediately, only affects the current answer, or requests confirmation?**
11. **When an entity name is ambiguous, how much uncertainty can dictation tolerate before Kivi must ask rather than silently “correct” the transcript?**
12. **When a hypothesis is invited for one Daari response, what must prevent the generated hypothesis and its latent characterization from entering later extraction or memory?**
13. **When Kivi abstains, which near misses can it reveal without exposing withheld, deleted, suppressed, or third-party memories?**
14. **When confirmations are budgeted, what should determine “highest value” without optimizing for engagement, sensitivity, or intrusive curiosity?**
15. **When provenance points to a transcript containing third-party personal content, what redacted or claim-scoped evidence can the user inspect?**
16. **When one source supports several claims with different retention rights, is provenance stored at transcript, span, claim, or derivation-step granularity?**
17. **When observations decay but their source transcripts remain, what must prevent the same observation from immediately regrowing during reprocessing?**
18. **When evidence is removed or corrected, which downstream summaries, embeddings, graph edges, caches, and generated observations must be invalidated?**
19. **When a user switches from Anbu to Koottu, should previously withheld observations become available immediately, only when relevant, or only after a fresh confirmation?**
20. **When the product reports “read, not kept,” what test can demonstrate non-retention across logs, embeddings, caches, traces, backups, and model-provider boundaries?**
21. **When an extraction error is individually high-cost but rare, should release criteria use average rates, worst-case tests, zero-tolerance categories, or confidence bounds?**
22. **When two people share a project name, preference, or artifact, what should prevent one user’s memory from crossing identity or tenancy boundaries?**

## 5. Evaluation gap

### What the paper measured

- Three multi-hop QA datasets: HotPotQA, TwoWikiMultiHop, and MuSiQue.
- Per dataset, 24 manually filtered training and 12 hold-out instances.
- Fifty sequential full-pipeline trials for each dataset × metric experiment; about 30 minutes per trial.
- Mean exact match, token-level F1, or DeepEval LLM-based correctness as the scalar optimization target.
- Hold-out performance of the best training configuration, with bootstrap confidence intervals over QA pairs.
- End-to-end output only. It does **not** report independent retrieval recall/precision, graph extraction accuracy, entity-resolution quality, provenance completeness, contradiction behavior, privacy leakage, latency distribution, dollar cost, abstention quality, correction persistence, or user outcomes.

Sources: [§4.1, pp. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=5), [§5, pp. 6–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=7), [§6, pp. 7–9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=8), [App. A.3, pp. 12–13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24478#page=13).

### Decisions its metrics can and cannot distinguish

| Decision(s) | Can EM/F1/correctness distinguish answers? | Why / same-score example |
|---|---|---|
| D3 chunking, D6 extraction prompt, D10 summaries, D11 retriever, D12 top-k, D15 QA prompt | **Sometimes, only end-to-end.** | Different choices can change the final string and therefore the score, which is why tuning finds gains. But the metric cannot locate the cause or distinguish a better graph from a prompt that emits shorter strings. |
| D1 input rights, D7 exclusions | **No.** | A system that retains third-party personal content and one that discards it can return the same gold answer. The unsafe system may even score higher if excluded content is predictive. |
| D2 deduplication/event identity | **Usually no.** | Retaining five occurrences versus one byte-deduplicated item can yield the same answer while producing different evidence counts and future observations. |
| D4 multi-store representation | **Often no.** | Text-only, graph-only, and three-store systems can emit the same answer; metrics do not compare maintenance cost, reversibility, or failure surface. |
| D5 epistemic schema | **No.** | “User stated X” and “system inferred X” can serialize to the same claim and produce the same answer, despite radically different action rights. |
| D8 identity resolution | **Only when it changes the sampled answer.** | A wrong merge that is never queried scores identically, even though it may later leak or misapply memory. |
| D9 contradiction policy, D18 correction policy, D19 aging | **No in this setup.** | Memory is cleared and rebuilt; no longitudinal correction, supersession, decay, or regrowth sequence is evaluated. |
| D13 ranking | **Only through final answers.** | Two rankings that both include the gold-supporting item within context score the same even if one foregrounds stale, weak, or disallowed claims. |
| D14 retrieval vs disclosure | **No.** | A system that retrieves and silently withholds an observation and one that never retrieved it can give the same answer; EM/F1 cannot test the trace or permission semantics. |
| D16 abstention | **No meaningful evidence.** | The curated questions are supported and answerable. A system that guesses on absent evidence is never separated from one that abstains correctly. |
| D17 user-visible provenance | **No.** | Cited, inspectable, and uncited opaque answers can contain the same tokens and receive the same score. Extra evidence may even reduce EM. |
| D20 objective choice | **Circular rather than discriminative.** | The selected metric defines the winner. It cannot establish whether correctness should outrank privacy, latency, cost, or confirmation burden. |
| D21 adaptation scope | **Dataset-level only.** | The study supports “configuration depends on benchmark/metric,” but cannot distinguish per-user, per-surface, or per-query adaptation, nor their predictability costs. |
| D22 nondeterminism | **No at the failure granularity Kivi needs.** | Stable mean scores can coexist with one unstable high-harm memory. No repeated-run per-claim analysis is reported. |

### The central evidence boundary

The paper provides evidence for one proposition: **within a static, answerable, multi-hop QA benchmark, end-to-end scores are sensitive to chunking, retrieval mode, context count, prompts, and summary preprocessing, and the preferred configuration varies by dataset and metric.**

It provides precedent, but no comparative evidence, for Kivi’s decisions about:

- write-path consent and exclusion;
- epistemic tiers and non-self-promotion;
- retention of third-party material;
- retrieval/disclosure separation;
- abstention on missing evidence;
- claim-level provenance visible to a normal user;
- inline correction, supersession, suppression, and non-regrowth;
- observation decay and hypothesis expiry;
- risk-weighted evaluation and hard trust constraints.

Two systems could answer all 36 held-out questions identically while making opposite choices on every item in that list. Under this paper’s evaluation they would be indistinguishable. For those choices, the paper is a precedent for modularity, not evidence for a decision.
