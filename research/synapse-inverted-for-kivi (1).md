# SYNAPSE, inverted: the decisions Kivi must make

**Paper:** Jiang et al., *SYNAPSE: Empowering LLM Agents with Episodic-Semantic Memory via Spreading Activation*, arXiv:2601.02744v3 (16 February 2026). [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02744)

**Comparison setting:** Kivi, as specified in *The Things Kivi Comes to Know*: a text-client memory system whose governing constraints are calibrated disclosure, non-retention of other people's personal information, and visible/attributable/reversible beliefs. Kivi separates ordinary dictation from “Hey Kivi,” distinguishes stated/observed/hypothesised memories, and treats removal as suppression against re-derivation.

## Reading key

- **Holds?** is not a recommendation. It reports whether SYNAPSE's apparent setting assumption matches Kivi's stated setting.
- **Partial** means the premise applies but the consequence, risk, or required boundary differs.
- **Underspecified** means the paper does not supply enough information; no implementation is inferred.
- Section/page references point to the arXiv PDF. “Alg. 1” and “Fig. 3” are in Appendix A (p. 12).

## 4. Questions the paper never faced, derived from Kivi

These questions come from Kivi's stated users, inputs, contracts, and failure modes—not from gaps in SYNAPSE alone.

1. **When application context contains another person's information needed for an immediate task, what exact transformation may survive after the response, and how is non-retention demonstrated to the user?** Kivi reads Slack/email context but promises not to keep third-party personal content.
2. **When a candidate memory mixes a work fact with an excluded personal fact, should the system reject the whole candidate, split it, or retain only a derived work-level residue—and what provenance may the residue reveal?**
3. **When the same evidence supports a stated fact, an observed pattern, and a hypothesis, what should determine which epistemic object is created, and can one source populate more than one tier?**
4. **When repeated behavior strongly supports an observation but the user never confirms it, what should determine whether it remains merely observed, expires, or stops being surfaced?** Kivi forbids self-promotion.
5. **When a user says “that's not me anymore,” what scope should the suppression cover: the exact wording, the normalized belief, the evidence set, nearby paraphrases, or a broader pattern class?** Too narrow permits regrowth; too broad can block future legitimate change.
6. **When a user corrects a memory inline during a task, which caches, derived nodes, links, observations, and prior outputs must be invalidated immediately?**
7. **When a correction conflicts with the source transcript rather than merely updating the world, should provenance show the original utterance, the correction event, or both—and which is authoritative?**
8. **When Kivi retrieves an observation that the current permission mode forbids it to say, what may that withheld memory influence indirectly—tool choice, wording, ranking, safety checks, or nothing?** “Disclosure” and “use” can diverge.
9. **When the interface says “Kivi noticed something here,” how much metadata can it reveal without disclosing the forbidden observation itself?** Even counts/categories can leak.
10. **When a Daari hypothesis is invited for one answer, what ephemeral state may be computed, logged, inspected, or retained without turning a one-request permission into a durable profile?**
11. **When ordinary dictation and Hey Kivi process the same transcript, what prevents observations or hypotheses from crossing into the lexical/entity path through shared embeddings, caches, or derived nodes?** Kivi promises structural separation.
12. **When a formatting preference improves transcription but could also be interpreted as behavioral profiling, where is the boundary between lexical/entity memory and preference memory?**
13. **When an excluded-category detector drops a candidate incorrectly, what should the user be able to see or contest without exposing the sensitive content in logs?**
14. **When a transcript produces no durable memory, what evidence should the product retain to prove that nothing crossed the write path, and for how long may that evidence itself persist?**
15. **When source transcripts are deleted but derived work-level memories remain, what should happen to attribution, auditability, and the derived memories' legitimacy?**
16. **When several people or projects share names, what should determine identity resolution before Kivi silently applies a memory in a draft or schedule action?**
17. **When a remembered fact drives an external action rather than an answer, what confidence, confirmation, and rollback requirements should depend on the action's failure cost?** A wrong recollection in prose and a wrong reschedule are not equivalent.
18. **When confirmation prompts are rationed, what should determine “highest value”: frequency, expected error cost, future reuse, user annoyance, ambiguity, or permission tier?**
19. **When a user pins a memory, which dynamics may still change it—contradiction, correction, time, source deletion, or exclusion-policy changes?**
20. **When Kivi's exclusion policy changes over time, what should happen to previously retained candidates that would now be forbidden, including their derivatives and logs?**
21. **When the Why panel lists retrieved, withheld, and used memories, how should it represent graph-derived paths without presenting correlation as causation or an observation as a stated fact?**
22. **When the system abstains, what should it reveal about searched scope, missing evidence, withheld evidence, and near matches so that the user can act without inferring that hidden sensitive memory exists?**
23. **When Kivi's corpus is initially sparse, what product behavior preserves trust during cold start rather than manufacturing observations from too little evidence?** SYNAPSE acknowledges cold-start inefficiency; Kivi also faces epistemic overreach.
24. **When a memory about the user's work necessarily names another person (for example, “Priya is the Acme contact”), what rule distinguishes permissible relational residue from impermissible third-party retention across role changes and departures?**

### What the paper measured

- LoCoMo long-horizon conversational QA over synthetic/fictional dialogues averaging about 16K tokens and up to 35 sessions.
- F1 and BLEU-1 for Single-Hop, Temporal, Open-Domain, Multi-Hop, and Adversarial categories; the reported weighted average excludes Adversarial. [§4.1 and Appendix A.3, pp. 4, 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02744#page=4)
- An LLM-as-a-judge semantic score intended to address lexical metric divergence. [Appendix D.3 and Table 10, pp. 13, 16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02744#page=13)
- Mechanism ablations, hyperparameter sweeps, low-vector-similarity subsets, three-seed stability, and cross-backbone results. [Tables 3, 6–9, 12, pp. 7, 15, 17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02744#page=7)

- Adversarial rejection and false-refusal rate for threshold selection. [Appendix C.2/Table 7, pp. 13, 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02744#page=13)

### Decisions its metrics can and cannot distinguish

| Decision(s) | Can the evaluation distinguish answers? | What would score the same or remain invisible |
|---|---|---|
| D01 atomic episode | **Weakly.** It evaluates the resulting QA, not source authority or granularity. | Turn nodes and claim/span nodes could produce the same answers; storing assistant text as evidence carries no penalty unless it changes QA output. |
| D02 write eligibility | **No.** | Retaining every sensitive/third-party fact and selectively refusing to say it can score identically—or better—than never storing it. No metric observes inappropriate retention. |
| D03 dual-layer representation | **Partly.** Graph-structure ablation supports structure for QA. | A provenance-aware claim graph, event-sourced relational model, or other structured representation could score similarly; the test does not isolate the two-layer ontology itself. |
| D04 abstraction/epistemic tier | **No.** | A stated preference, inferred pattern, and speculative hypothesis can all yield the same reference answer. No metric checks whether the system represents or speaks them with different certainty. |
| D05 five-turn consolidation | **Weakly.** | Per-turn, per-transcript, or asynchronous consolidation could return the same top-30 context. The paper does not ablate N or measure memory freshness after each write. |
| D06 deduplication | **No direct evidence.** | Silent false merges, false splits, and contradiction-preserving versions are not labeled. QA may remain unchanged until a collision matters. |
| D07 relation formation | **Partly.** Graph removal and low-similarity results support relational retrieval generally. | Window co-occurrence, typed LLM edges, exact provenance edges, and learned links are not compared. Incorrect relations that happen not to affect test answers are invisible. |
| D08 pruning/archive | **No.** | Archiving, deletion, retention, and privacy TTLs score the same if tested evidence remains retrievable. No long-duration or deletion-compliance test is reported. |
| D09 temporal mechanism | **Yes for QA recency, no for semantics.** | Ablation shows decay matters, but explicit supersession or validity intervals might score the same. The metric cannot tell “forgot old truth” from “represented change correctly.” |
| D10 contradiction policy | **No.** | Coexisting claims with recency reranking and a versioned supersession ledger can answer identically. Auditability and correction behavior are unmeasured. |
| D11 hybrid seeding | **Not isolated.** | BM25+dense, entity query+dense, or better dense-only seeds could yield the same final subgraph. No seed-only ablation is reported. |
| D12 propagation | **Yes, within the tested architecture.** | The activation ablation and depth sweep distinguish no propagation vs. propagation, but not spreading activation from other multi-hop search methods at matched graph, tokens, and latency. |
| D13 fan effect | **Yes for benchmark QA.** | It does not distinguish degree dilution from another hub-control method, nor measure unfair suppression of highly connected real-world entities. |
| D14 inhibition | **Yes for benchmark QA, incomplete for failure cost.** | The aggregate gain and jacket failure show a trade-off, but there is no cost-weighted distinction between missing a minor detail and admitting a distractor. |
| D15 hybrid final rank | **Partly.** | The paper supplies PageRank weight sensitivity and top-k sweep, but no full factor ablation. Ranking with authority/provenance features could score the same; no inspectability test exists. |
| D16 cache update timing | **No.** | Five-turn stale caches and immediate invalidation are indistinguishable in an offline QA run without correction/permission events. |
| D17 abstention gate | **Yes for LoCoMo's adversarial split.** | Different confidence models can occupy the same adversarial-F1/FRR point. The test does not distinguish absent, private, withheld, contradictory, or merely unretrieved evidence. |
| D18 generator grounding rule | **Weakly.** | Prompt-only verification, citations, entailment checking, or structured generation can produce the same answer string. Multi-hop inference versus unsupported inference is not claim-audited. |
| D19 user-visible evidence | **No.** | A black box and a fully attributable Why panel score identically. Experimental traces are not evaluated with users. |
| D20 retrieval vs disclosure | **No.** | Retrieving a sensitive observation and withholding it, never retrieving it, or leaking its influence through wording can all yield the same expected answer on ordinary QA. |
| D21 separate action paths | **No.** | A single memory path and structurally separate dictation/Hey Kivi paths cannot be distinguished because the benchmark contains only QA. |
| D22 correction/anti-regrowth | **No.** | Delete, archive, demote, supersede, and delete-plus-suppress are untested. There is no reprocessing test after user correction. |
| D23 storage/threat model | **No.** | Local, enclave, and centralized storage produce identical QA, latency aside. No privacy/security evaluation is performed. |
| D24 belief lifecycle | **No.** | Score decay and auditable state transitions can rank the same current answer. Promotion, expiry, provenance, suppression, and historical legibility are absent from the labels. |

### Bottom line of the gap

The paper supplies evidence for a narrow question: **given a synthetic long-conversation QA setting in which the history is presumptively retainable, does a sparse episodic-semantic graph with activation dynamics retrieve answer-bearing context efficiently?** Its ablations provide real evidence about graph structure, propagation, decay, hub dilution, inhibition, and rejection in that setting.

It supplies only precedent—not comparative evidence—for Kivi's defining decisions: what is allowed to become memory; whose information may persist; how statement, observation, and hypothesis differ; how contradictions and corrections change belief state; how removal prevents re-derivation; what different surfaces may retrieve; what permission allows the system to use or disclose; and what a person can inspect and contest. Two systems with opposite answers to all of those questions could achieve the same LoCoMo F1, BLEU, judge score, latency, token count, and rejection rate.
