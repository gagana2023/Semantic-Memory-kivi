# Inverting *Beyond Dialogue Time*: Decision Questions for Kivi

This document reverses the design of Su et al.'s Temporal Semantic Memory (TSM) into the questions its authors had to answer. It does not recommend answers. “Kivi fit” compares the paper's assumptions with the product position in *The Things Kivi Comes to Know*.

## Scope and notation

- **Paper**: Miao Su et al., “Beyond Dialogue Time: Temporal Semantic Memory for Personalized LLM Agents,” arXiv:2601.07468v2 (2 September 2026), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.07468).
- **Kivi position**: *kivi-semantic-memory-position (1)(6).md*, especially §§1–9 and Appendices B–C.
- **Load-bearing** marks a choice for which the paper supplies direct or strong experimental evidence that a different answer materially changes its reported results. It does not mean the same choice is necessarily load-bearing for Kivi.
- **Underspecified** means the paper does not provide enough detail to reconstruct the choice faithfully.

## 2. Forced choices

These are the paper's load-bearing answers: changing them either produced a material measured difference or would remove the mechanism the claimed result depends on.

| Decision | Why load-bearing in the paper | What the evidence actually establishes | What remains open for Kivi |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1 — Semantic/event time rather than dialogue time (D2)** | Removing temporal ranking/filtering reduced LONGMEMEVAL_S overall accuracy from 74.8 to 72.8 and temporal accuracy from 69.92 to 63.91. | Explicit temporal modeling helps this benchmark/model configuration, especially temporal questions. | Whether Kivi should treat semantic time as one field among several or the dominant organizing clock; how much parser error its scheduling use cases tolerate. |
| **F2 — Durative summaries in addition to episodic facts/raw turns (D5)** | Removing topic/persona summaries reduced overall accuracy (reported inconsistently as 73.4 in Table 3 and 73.6 in prose) and sharply reduced single-session preference accuracy from 40.0 to 23.33. | Some summary layer helps preference and temporal QA in this dataset, though it distracts on several categories and its overall gain is small. | Whether Kivi's acceptable layer is work-topic/state consolidation, evidence-linked observations, or persona; the ablation removes topics and personas together, so it cannot distinguish them. |
| **F3 — Persona plus topic summaries (D6)** | The headline “durative memory” implementation includes both, and the joint ablation materially affects preference questions. | The combined summary package can improve a narrow preference metric. | Persona is not separately ablated from topic. The paper therefore gives no evidence that psychological/user-trait abstraction is necessary, and Kivi's stated boundary makes the distinction central. |
| **F4 — Semantic retrieval plus temporal filtering/reranking (D14–D15)** | This is the second named contribution; its ablation lowers temporal performance by 6 points. | Time-aware utilization beats dense-only retrieval on LONGMEMEVAL_S under the tested setup. | Post-filter versus prefilter, lexicographic versus weighted/learned ranking, and whether temporal validity outranks epistemic authority. None is individually ablated. |
| **F5 — Preserve raw dialogue alongside abstractions (D4, D13)** | The case study says graph facts alone cannot answer the question; raw turns supply omitted detail. LOCOMO full text also beats retrieval when the whole history fits. | Lossy structured memories need a detail-preserving fallback for some questions. | Whether Kivi may preserve only permitted source spans, use redacted provenance, or avoid retrieval over raw transcripts. The paper does not test privacy-constrained variants. |
| **F6 — Move expensive consolidation off the recall path (D11)** | TSM reports P50/P95 recall latency of 1.57/2.39 s and attributes it to pre-built memory plus local reranking; sleep-time consolidation lowers token cost. | Precomputation can buy interactive latency at higher construction cost. | Kivi has no fixed latency/cost targets yet, and safety admission checks cannot inherit the same staleness tolerance as summaries. |

The paper does **not** make every implementation constant load-bearing. Monthly windows, GMM clustering on entity names, Top-K 25, spaCy parsing, and GPT-4o-mini construction are precedents, not isolated causal findings.

## 4. Questions the paper never faced

These arise from Kivi's users, constraints, and failure modes—not from gaps in TSM alone.

1. **When an input mixes Meera's dictation, another person's message, and application metadata, what should determine which spans are eligible to cross the write boundary?**
2. **When a retained work fact concerns another person (“Priya is the Acme contact”), what separates legitimate work-level residue from a prohibited memory about that person?**
3. **When provenance requires preserving a transcript that also contains barred third-party content, what exactly may be stored and later displayed?**
4. **When a candidate touches an excluded category indirectly or through implication, should category risk, factual utility, or user intent determine admission?**
5. **When an exclusion check and the extractor disagree, which component's judgment should control storage, and what should the inspection log reveal?**
6. **When a work-level fact can reveal a sensitive fact by combination with other memories, should each memory be judged independently or as part of an inference set?**
7. **When a preference is observed repeatedly but never stated, what should determine whether it may be surfaced, used, or merely retained?**
8. **When an observation has enough evidence to be statistically strong, why—if ever—should that change its epistemic permissions without confirmation?**
9. **When the user moves the permission dial after memories have already been retrieved, should the current answer, later answers, or the stored memories change?**
10. **When “what do you think?” invites Daari for one answer, what language or interaction boundary determines the start and end of that invitation?**
11. **When a hypothesis is generated for a Daari response but must not be written to memory, what diagnostic trace may persist without becoming a quiet hypothesis store?**
12. **When Anbu withholds an observation but shows “Kivi noticed something,” what should determine whether even that meta-disclosure violates the user's desired calibration?**
13. **When ordinary dictation mentions a known preference, what distinguishes a formatting preference permitted on the lexical/entity path from a behavioral observation barred from it?**
14. **When one request combines dictation and a Hey Kivi command, which contract—and therefore which retrieval path—governs each part?**
15. **When Kivi is wrong, what should a correction update: the claim, its evidence, its tier, its source interpretation, future extraction behavior, or all of them?**
16. **When the user says “forget” versus “that's not me anymore,” what should differ in provenance history, future re-derivation, and audit visibility?**
17. **When a correction concerns another person's work role, who is the person entitled to revise it, and how should later contradictory application context be treated?**
18. **When an observation decays, should its source evidence remain visible, remain retrievable for audit, or be removed entirely?**
19. **When an unconfirmed hypothesis expires, what should happen to the interaction in which it was shown and to any feedback short of explicit confirmation?**
20. **When confirmation prompts are rationed, what should determine “highest value”: expected future reuse, uncertainty, correction cost, interruption cost, or potential harm?**
21. **When no answer is found, what should determine which nearby memories Kivi may expose without turning abstention into accidental disclosure?**
22. **When a retrieved memory is permitted but not actually used in the answer, what level of trace detail makes the system legible without overwhelming the person?**
23. **When a source transcript is edited or deleted, should derived memories be invalidated, re-extracted, retained with broken provenance, or queued for confirmation?**
24. **When the same project name or person appears across clients, channels, and years, what should determine identity boundaries before any memory can affect drafting or scheduling?**
25. **When a requested action has external consequences, such as rescheduling, what should determine whether a memory may supply an argument silently or must be confirmed at execution time?**

## 5. Evaluation gap

### What the paper measured

TSM reports LLM-judged answer accuracy on two benchmarks:

- **LONGMEMEVAL_S**: 500 questions with roughly 115k tokens and 30–40 sessions per history, categorized as temporal, multi-session, knowledge-update, single-user, single-assistant, single-preference, and abstention tasks.
- **LOCOMO**: 1,986 questions categorized as single-hop, multi-hop, temporal, open-domain, and adversarial reasoning; the paper elsewhere describes LOCOMO histories as 16k–26k tokens and Appendix A.3 as roughly 300 turns/~9k tokens on average.
- **Efficiency**: total construction/update token counts and recall P50/P95 latency on LONGMEMEVAL_S.
- **Ablations**: remove temporal retrieval as a package; remove topic and persona summaries as a package.

The judge is intentionally permissive. Standard answers pass if they contain the gold answer; knowledge-update answers may also include stale information as long as the updated answer appears; preference answers need only use some rubric information; abstention passes for recognizing that information is incomplete; temporal answers are not penalized for off-by-one duration errors. LOCOMO grading is likewise “generous.” (Paper §4.1; Appendix A.4.)

### Decisions its metrics can and cannot distinguish

| Decision pair that could score the same | Why the evaluation cannot distinguish them | Consequence for interpreting the paper |
|---|---|---|
| **Retain all raw turns vs retain only privacy-permitted spans (D4, D20)** | No retention/privacy label or third-party test exists. Both systems receive the same credit if the answer matches. | Raw retention is a precedent, not evidence about Kivi's write boundary. |
| **Persona claim vs evidence-linked observation (D6, D17)** | Preference scoring checks whether personal information is used correctly, not whether the system was entitled to infer or apply it. | Persona effectiveness cannot establish acceptable epistemic status. |
| **Silent personalization vs permission-gated disclosure (D17)** | No user-control state, disclosure policy, or penalty for unwanted personalization appears in the benchmark. | TSM gives no evidence on Kivi's central calibration decision. |
| **Correct answer with provenance vs without provenance (D18)** | The judge sees answer content, not source trace correctness or usability. | Internal graph links do not validate a Why panel. |
| **Generic abstention vs diagnostic abstention (D19)** | “Information is incomplete” is enough to pass; searched scope, nearby evidence, and actionable explanation are unmeasured. | The paper validates abstention recognition only. |
| **Overwrite vs auditable supersession vs suppression (D9, D21)** | Knowledge-update grading explicitly accepts stale information alongside the correct update and does not reprocess old evidence to test regrowth. | Update accuracy gives no evidence that user corrections remain effective. |
| **One retrieval path vs structurally separated dictation/Hey Kivi paths (D22)** | Benchmarks contain conversational QA, not transcription contracts or capability-level access tests. | The paper cannot arbitrate Kivi's architectural separation. |
| **Monthly vs adaptive/project-bound windows (D8)** | Monthly granularity is not ablated; datasets do not report performance by event density or boundary crossing. | The fixed month is only an implementation default. |
| **Name-only GMM clusters vs relation/project-aware clustering (D7)** | Clustering method and cluster count are not ablated; final accuracy hides cluster purity and cross-context contamination. | The reported gains belong to the package, not this clustering answer. |
| **spaCy single-interval parse vs ambiguity-aware parsing (D12)** | No parser accuracy, ambiguity set, locale/timezone slice, or clarification cost is reported. Off-by-one duration errors are forgiven. | Temporal QA gains do not establish robustness for scheduling language. |
| **Post-filter vs prefilter vs joint temporal retrieval (D14)** | The ablation removes temporal ranking/filtering together and compares only with dense-only retrieval. | Evidence supports “use time,” not where or how to combine it. |
| **Binary lexicographic time priority vs graded/learned fusion (D15)** | No ranking alternatives are compared; only end-to-end answer accuracy is shown. | The priority rule is unvalidated separately. |
| **Top-K 25 vs adaptive K (D16)** | Only one K is reported; no recall-at-K, context precision, or sensitivity curve is provided. | K=25 is a precedent tied to the tested cost/context regime. |
| **Accurate entity merge vs false merge with coincidentally correct answer (D10)** | No entity-resolution precision/recall, merge reversibility, or cross-person leakage metric exists. | QA accuracy can conceal dangerous identity errors. |
| **Store excluded/sensitive inferences vs refuse them (D20)** | The datasets contain no candidate-level storage-policy gold labels and no audit-log check. | The paper is silent on Kivi's core trust claims. |
| **Decay/expiry vs indefinite memory (D21)** | Benchmark questions reward recall; no stale-characterization, expiration, or user-growth metric exists. | TSM's evolving persona language is not evidence of revisability. |
| **Low construction cost vs high cost with safer admission checks (D11, D23)** | Token totals aggregate stages but do not price privacy checks, correction handling, storage, user attention, or failure recovery. | Efficiency numbers transfer only after Kivi defines its missing cost and latency budgets. |

### Evidence boundaries

The paper provides meaningful evidence for a narrow claim: on the tested long-dialogue QA tasks, adding semantic-time-aware retrieval and a durative summary layer to a memory system can improve answer accuracy, with the clearest gain on temporal questions and a conditional gain on preference questions. It does not supply evidence for who or what may be remembered, the epistemic status of inferred patterns, disclosure permissions, user legibility, correction durability, third-party non-retention, or harm-weighted failures. On those decisions, TSM is a precedent only.
