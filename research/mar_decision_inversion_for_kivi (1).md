# MAR decision inversion for Kivi

## Scope and reading frame

This report inverts *MAR: Multi-Agent Reflexion Improves Reasoning Abilities in LLMs* into the questions its authors answered, explicitly or implicitly. “Kivi” means the system defined in *The Things Kivi Comes to Know*: a text-client semantic-memory product whose durable memories concern the user’s work, distinguish stated facts from observations and hypotheses, exclude sensitive and third-party personal material on the write path, expose provenance and retrieval traces, support correction/suppression/decay, and give ordinary dictation narrower memory rights than Hey Kivi.

The comparison is deliberately asymmetric. MAR’s “memory” is a short-lived optimization trace for solving benchmark items; Kivi’s memory is a durable, user-facing belief store. A MAR result can therefore be relevant precedent without transferring cleanly.

Citation form: **§section, p. N** refers to the paper’s printed section and PDF page. Links point to the cited PDF.

| D22 | Should the same memory and reflection machinery apply across all product surfaces? | The same framework is evaluated per benchmark; surface-specific access rights are not considered. | Separate stores/queries by interaction contract; capability-scoped memory; disclosure gates; per-tool retrieval policies. | Each dataset defines one homogeneous task interface. | **No.** Kivi gives regular dictation only lexical/entity memory and Hey Kivi broader tier-gated access. | D23 | Should the system abstain when evidence is missing or keep iterating toward an answer? | Iterate within a fixed trial/action budget; explicit calibrated abstention is not designed or evaluated. | Abstain with searched evidence; ask for clarification; return candidates; escalate; act only above confidence threshold. | Every benchmark item has a known answer and attempting is always appropriate. | **No.** Kivi explicitly treats abstention and showing what was searched as product features. | D24 | How should the system trade correction quality against API calls, tokens, latency, and energy? | Use multi-agent debate despite roughly 3× calls/latency; cap rounds/trials to limit costs. The difference pushes cost-quality evaluation to per-interaction and per-risk class rather than aggregate benchmark accuracy. | [Experimental Setup and Limitations, pp. 5–6; Societal Impact, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.20845). |
| D25 | What evidence should establish that multi-agent reflection, rather than extra computation, causes the improvement? | Compare baseline, Reflexion, and MAR while holding GPT-3.5 across MAR roles; no equal-token/equal-call control or persona ablation is reported. | Match compute; match number of samples; ablate debate, judge, personas, role labels, and context length; compare one stronger critic. | Keeping the base model fixed isolates the multi-agent contribution sufficiently. | **No basis for transfer.** Kivi cannot tell from this paper whether gains come from disagreement structure, more tokens, more samples, or the judge. The difference leaves implementation-level causality unresolved. | [Experimental Setup and Results, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.20845). |

## 2. Forced choices

These are load-bearing for MAR: changing them would alter either the intervention itself, the feedback loop that trains it in context, or the reported comparison materially.

| ID | Forced choice | Why load-bearing | What remains genuinely open for Kivi |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 (D3–D5) | Replace one self-reflector with multiple persona-guided critics. | This is the headline treatment. Without multiple differentiated critics there is no MAR, and the claimed escape from repeated reasoning modes has no mechanism. | Whether Kivi’s relevant independence comes from personas, validators, evidence partitions, model heterogeneity, or user adjudication. |
| F2 (D8–D9) | Let critics interact, then have one judge synthesize one reflection. | The paper attributes revision quality to surfaced disagreement plus aggregation. Independent sampling or retaining plural hypotheses would be a different system. | Whether disagreement should be collapsed at all when it concerns a durable belief rather than a one-shot solution. |
| F3 (D2, D16–D17) | Trigger reflection from a coarse evaluator failure signal and treat that signal as authoritative. | No failure signal means no MAR loop; a noisy signal demonstrably changes the trajectory, including away from correct answers. | What combination of task feedback, source conflict, confidence, privacy policy, and user correction should trigger Kivi’s review path. |
| F4 (D10–D13) | Store natural-language reflection in context for another attempt on the same item. | The method’s improvement channel is in-context verbal memory. Structured state or cross-item retrieval would change both mechanism and scope. | How Kivi atomizes, ranks, scopes, versions, and retrieves durable memories across interactions. |
| F5 (D14–D15, D24) | Bound debate and retry depth under a cost budget. | More or fewer rounds change compute, latency, context, and opportunity to correct. The paper says returns diminish and cost rises. | Kivi’s limits by task risk and interactive latency; the paper’s contradictory HumanEval trial counts prevent exact replication of this choice. |
| F6 (D5–D6) | Hand-engineer dataset-specific critic panels around observed failure modes. | The reported system is inseparable from those panels; no ablation shows that arbitrary or generic personas work. | Whether Kivi’s panel would track memory-specific risks and how roles would change as users/corpus evolve. |
| F7 (D16, D25) | Evaluate only final-answer correctness on two benchmarks without compute-matched controls. | These metrics define which retries count as improvement and therefore shape both the feedback and the conclusion. | Whether any apparent Kivi gain comes from a decision architecture rather than added inference, and whether it respects trust constraints. |

## 3. Unexamined defaults

These choices are present in the system but are not treated as design variables or tested against alternatives.

| Default | Where inherited/visible | Alternative this line of work rarely tests | Why it matters to Kivi |
|---|---|---|---|
| A model’s textual scratchpad is a faithful diagnostic object. | The actor’s “failed thoughts” are passed to the judge and critics (Extension loop, p. 4). | Diagnose from inputs, tool evidence, outputs, and counterfactual tests while withholding chain-of-thought. | Kivi must minimize exposure and cannot assume a generated rationale is the cause of an error. |
| More model instances equal more independent perspectives. | All roles use GPT-3.5; diversity is induced by role prompts (Experimental Setup, p. 5). | Partition evidence or validation authority so agents literally observe different evidence and cannot share the same unsupported premise. | Prompt diversity can leave correlated misattribution and privacy errors untouched. |
| Disagreement should terminate in a single consensus artifact. | Judge synthesis (pp. 4–5). | Treat irreducible disagreement as stored plural state with explicit uncertainty and no action authority. | Kivi’s hypothesis tier and contradiction history are built precisely to avoid premature settlement. |
| Natural language is the memory schema. | Reflexion background and consensus reflection (pp. 2, 4). | Store claims, evidence edges, counterexamples, timestamps, permissions, and executable validators separately. | Free text does not natively support demotion, suppression, subject boundaries, provenance, or decay. |
| The failed item is the natural scope of memory. | Trials retry the same HotPotQA/HumanEval item (pp. 3–5). | Attach a lesson to a user, domain, tool, source, time interval, or failure class and make scope explicit. | Scope errors in Kivi can leak one project/person’s context into another. |
| Benchmark inputs are safe to retain and redistribute internally. | No write-path eligibility or privacy stage is described. | Separate “may be processed” from “may be retained” and enforce subject/category exclusions before storage or critic fan-out. | This is one of Kivi’s three foundational promises. |
| Ground truth is timeless and singular. | Exact answers and unit tests determine success (pp. 2–3). | Model facts as time-indexed, source-relative, and legitimately contradictory. | People, projects, employers, preferences, and schedules change. |
| Silence has no semantics because the evaluator always responds. | Every attempt is automatically scored. | Treat absent user feedback as neither confirmation nor rejection, with explicit expiry. | Kivi explicitly refuses to promote hypotheses from silence. |
| Errors are symmetric. | All failures trigger the same debate structure. | Route by failure cost: privacy leak, wrong action, stale fact, stylistic miss, and benign abstention follow different paths. | Kivi’s failure costs are heterogeneous; a wrong disclosure is not equivalent to a formatting miss. |
| Internal logs substitute for user legibility. | Detailed logging supports researcher analysis; no end-user surface is described (Methodology, p. 3). | Design a user-readable trace as a first-class output and test whether users can predict/correct system state. | Kivi’s Why panel and memory surface are part of correctness, not debugging garnish. |
| Final-answer accuracy is the natural objective. | EM/pass@1 are the only quantitative outcomes (Performance Metrics, p. 3). | Evaluate calibration, evidence faithfulness, safe non-retention, correction persistence, non-regrowth, and appropriate abstention independently. | Two systems can answer equally well while only one satisfies Kivi’s trust contract. |
| A centralized judge does not need its own appeal mechanism. | Judge produces the final reflection (pp. 4–5). | Expose minority evidence, use independent adjudication, or allow a user/source to overturn the judge with persistent effect. | Kivi makes reversibility by the person represented a core principle. |

## 4. Questions the paper never faced

Derived from Kivi’s stated users, product contracts, and failure modes—not from gaps in MAR alone.

1. **When an interaction contains the user’s words and another person’s words, what should determine which subject a candidate memory is about?** The defensible answers include speaker identity, grammatical subject, ownership of the work fact, or explicit user confirmation.
2. **When third-party personal context is necessary to produce a humane reply, what representation may cross from transient generation context into candidate extraction?** Choices include nothing, a work-only derived residue, a redacted event, or consent-scoped retention.
3. **When a candidate falls near a sensitive-category boundary, should uncertainty cause exclusion, quarantine, user confirmation, or storage at a restricted tier?**
4. **When a memory is both useful for transcription and disallowed for inference, what should determine its capability scope?** A lexical store, field-level permissions, query-path separation, or post-retrieval filtering are distinct designs.
5. **When the same stored fact is retrieved in dictation and Hey Kivi, what should determine whether it can affect spelling, generation, disclosure, or action?**
6. **When Kivi retrieves an observation that the current permission mode forbids it to say, what should be visible: only a withheld count, a category, an affordance, or nothing?** Each reveals a different amount merely by acknowledging the memory.
7. **When repeated behavior supports an observation but the user has never endorsed it, what should determine whether Kivi asks for confirmation, surfaces it as a pattern, or remains silent?**
8. **When confirmation prompts impose attention cost, what should rank candidates for the weekly budget?** Candidate utility, uncertainty, recurrence, expected future use, risk, or user irritation yield different queues.
9. **When the user corrects a memory inline during another task, how much of the source-derived graph should the correction change?** The atom only, linked relations, downstream observations, or prior generated outputs are defensible scopes.
10. **When “Forget” and “That’s not me anymore” both remove a visible belief, what should remain internally to prevent re-derivation without violating the promise to forget?** A content hash, category-level suppression, rule, source tombstone, or no residue each trades privacy against correction persistence.
11. **When new evidence contradicts a stated memory, should recency, explicitness, source authority, repetition, or user confirmation determine supersession?**
12. **When a preference changes in one context but not another, should Kivi split it by audience/project/channel or supersede it globally?**
13. **When an observation ages without counterevidence, what should decay: retrieval rank, disclosure eligibility, confidence, or the record itself?**
14. **When an unconfirmed hypothesis expires, should its source evidence remain eligible to produce the same question again?** This is distinct from deletion and from explicit suppression.
15. **When provenance itself contains sensitive or third-party content, what should the user see when opening the source behind an otherwise permissible derived memory?**
16. **When a source transcript is edited or deleted, what should happen to memories derived solely or partly from it?** Options include invalidate, decrement evidence, retain with broken provenance, or request reconfirmation.
17. **When Kivi cannot answer, what should determine whether it shows nearby memories, asks permission to broaden the search, asks the user, or stops?**
18. **When a wrong memory has already influenced an email, schedule, or recall answer, what remediation should be attached to correcting the memory itself?** The product can update future state, identify affected outputs, propose undo, or merely record the correction.
19. **When source evidence and a user correction conflict, is user authority absolute over “what Kivi thinks,” or should the system preserve a distinction between preference/self-description and externally verifiable work fact?**
20. **When the permission dial changes, should previously produced observations, cached retrievals, and pending hypotheses be recomputed, hidden, or left in history?**
21. **When multiple people use or view one work artifact, whose permission dial and memory rights govern generation and disclosure?**
22. **When an extraction was dropped, what can be logged to prove the boundary without recreating the prohibited content in the audit log?** A count/category, salted fingerprint, redacted span, or validator record provides different proof.
23. **When a model upgrade changes extraction behavior, what should happen to old transcripts?** Reprocess all, only new, only user-selected, or never reprocess; suppression and non-retention guarantees change under each.
24. **When Kivi’s inference or exclusion policy changes over time, which policy version should govern old evidence and how should the user understand resulting memory changes?**
25. **When memories improve task completion but make users feel surveilled or mischaracterized, what observable should take priority and who decides the tradeoff?**

## 5. Evaluation gap

### What MAR measured

- **HotPotQA:** normalized exact-match accuracy on a curated set of 100 difficult questions, with up to five trials reported; ReAct 32, Reflexion 44, MAR 47 in the authors’ results. [Datasets, Methodology, Results, pp. 2–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.20845).
- **HumanEval:** whether a generated Python solution passes all hidden tests (reported as pass@1); baseline GPT-3.5 67.1, Reflexion GPT-3.5 76.4, MAR GPT-3.5 82.6. The number of allowed extension trials is inconsistent between the methodology and Appendix D. [Performance Metrics, Methodology, Results, pp. 3–5; Appendix D, p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.20845).
- **Qualitative logs:** examples of confirmation bias, repeated error forms, hallucinated specification drift, exact-match false negatives, and a correct answer being driven into a timeout. [Motivation, pp. 3–4; Appendices A, B, E, pp. 8–9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.20845).
- **Cost observation:** roughly 3× API calls/latency versus Reflexion, plus a separate claim of roughly 300–400 API calls per task. No detailed latency distribution, token accounting, dollar cost, or accuracy–cost frontier is provided. [Limitations and Societal Impact, pp. 5–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.20845).

### Decisions its metrics cannot distinguish

| Indistinguishable answers under MAR’s evaluation | Same score is possible because… | Decision IDs left without evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Persona diversity vs. independent samples vs. one critic given equal extra tokens | No compute-matched or sampling-matched ablation isolates the source of gain. | D3–D6, D25 |
| Interactive debate vs. independent critiques followed by aggregation | No debate-topology ablation is reported. | D8–D9 |
| Central consensus vs. preserving minority hypotheses | Only the final answer is scored; loss of justified dissent is invisible when the answer happens to match. | D9, D11, D18 |
| Free-text reflection vs. structured claims/tests/patches | Both can lead to the same final answer; maintainability, controllability, and provenance are unmeasured. | D10–D12 |
| Correct answer from valid evidence vs. correct answer from hallucinated or contaminated reasoning | EM/pass@1 score outcomes, not evidence faithfulness. Hidden tests can catch some behavioral code errors but not the provenance of the reasoning. | D7, D10, D16 |
| Safe write-path exclusion vs. retaining all personal/third-party context | Benchmarks contain no privacy-labelled cases and score no retention behavior. | D7, D21 |
| Correct subject attribution vs. a correct answer produced from a misattributed memory | There is no persistent person/entity memory or attribution test. | D18, D21; Kivi-only Q1–Q2 |
| Same retrieval policy across surfaces vs. capability-scoped paths | MAR has no dictation/Hey Kivi distinction and no authorization metric. | D22 |
| Retrieved-but-withheld vs. never retrieved vs. disclosed | Final benchmark answers do not measure permission-aware disclosure or the user’s understanding of withheld state. | D19, D22; Kivi-only Q5–Q6 |
| Silent guess vs. calibrated abstention | Every benchmark item expects an answer; abstention gets no defined credit. | D23 |
| Editable/suppressible memory vs. immutable reflection | No user correction, deletion, suppression, or reprocessing test exists. | D18–D20; Kivi-only Q9–Q10 |
| Temporal supersession/decay vs. accumulating stale facts | Benchmark truth is stationary over a few trials; no longitudinal evaluation exists. | D13, D18; Kivi-only Q11–Q14, Q23–Q24 |
| A system that exposes provenance and uncertainty vs. an opaque system | No end-user comprehension, trust calibration, or correction-success study is performed. | D19–D20 |
| One-round low latency vs. two-round higher accuracy at equal user utility | Only aggregate correctness is reported; no task-level latency, abandonment, or perceived delay. | D14–D15, D24 |
| Harmless wrong answer vs. privacy leak or wrong external action | All failures collapse into benchmark incorrectness; severity and reversibility are absent. | D16, D20–D24 |
| Item-local retry learning vs. transferable memory across interactions | Tests retry the same item and do not evaluate memory reused on later items/users/domains. | D12–D13 |
| User-confirmed fact vs. repeated observed pattern vs. model hypothesis | MAR has no epistemic-tier labels or tier-sensitive use policy. Equal answer accuracy supplies no evidence about legitimacy of acting on each. | D9–D10, D18–D20 |

### Bottom line of the gap

The paper supplies evidence for a narrow proposition: under its particular prompts, budgets, models, and benchmark feedback, a multi-persona debate plus judge produced higher final-answer scores than the reproduced single-reflector loop. It supplies precedent—but no discriminating evidence—for most decisions that define Kivi: write eligibility, third-party non-retention, memory typing, subject attribution, permission-aware retrieval/disclosure, provenance, correction persistence, temporal change, abstention, or user legibility.

Even within MAR’s narrower decision space, its evaluation does not separate the effect of structured disagreement from the effects of additional calls, tokens, samples, or a judge. The observed score gains therefore cannot by themselves choose among those alternatives.
