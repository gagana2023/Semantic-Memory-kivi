# CogMem inverted: the design questions it answers, tested against KiVi

**Paper:** Yiran Zhang, Jincheng Hu, Mark Dras, and Usman Naseem, *CogMem: A Cognitive Memory Architecture for Sustained Multi-Turn Reasoning in Large Language Models*, arXiv:2512.14118v1 (16 December 2025). [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118)

**KiVi basis:** *The Things Kivi Comes to Know: A position on semantic memory for Kivi* (attached working document).

## Reading frame

This is an inversion, not an endorsement. “Paper answer” records what CogMem chose. “Alternatives” maps plausible branches, including branches the paper does not test. “Fit for KiVi” compares the assumptions behind CogMem with the product described in the KiVi position paper; it does not choose KiVi’s answer.

The most important scope distinction is that CogMem’s stored object is chiefly a **reasoning trace for solving a task**, while KiVi’s proposed stored object is a **user-visible, attributable belief about the user’s work world**. CogMem is evaluated on synthetic hidden-rule games, not personal data, third-party content, user correction, or disclosure control. The paper itself acknowledges that safeguards for retention, privacy, and consent remain future work ([Ethical, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118#page=5)).

“Underspecified” means the paper does not disclose enough to infer an implementation choice safely. Appendix A says the missing hyperparameters, session protocols, and retrieval configurations are in an omitted internal repository ([Appendix A.6, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118#page=7)).

## 2. Forced choices

These are the answers most entangled with the reported gains or with CogMem’s ability to remain bounded. A different answer would plausibly change the headline results materially. “Forced” does not mean proven uniquely correct.

| Flag | Load-bearing choice | Why it carries the result | What the ablation does—and does not—establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Compress active context through FoA instead of continually appending full history** (D06–D07). | This is the mechanism responsible for bounded context and targeted recovery of earlier details. Removing it changes both token use and what evidence the reasoner sees. | FoA alone changes the difficulty profile but leaves total accuracy at 0.76; it improves hard cases from 0.47 to 0.53 while medium falls from 0.93 to 0.84. The paper does not test fixed windows, long-context baselines, alternative compressors, or retrieval policies. [Table 1, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118#page=4) |
| F2 | **Keep structured session notes in DA rather than reloading raw history** (D05). | DA produces the largest standalone incremental gain before LTM: total accuracy 0.76→0.84 and hard 0.53→0.66 over FoA. | The additive ablation supports “this DA implementation on this benchmark,” not the necessity of model-written notes, this schema, or this compression policy. [§3.2–3.3, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118#page=4) |
| F3 | **Persist distilled cross-session memory in LTM** (D04, D13). | LTM supplies the final 0.84→0.93 gain and is the only layer intended to accumulate strategies across sessions. | The benchmark supports cross-session reuse as configured; it cannot separate strategy distillation from extra retrieval, exposure to prior games, or the exact merge/update policy. [Table 1 and §3.3, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118#page=4) |
| F4 | **Use a three-level temporal hierarchy** (D03). | CogMem’s claimed contribution and all ablations are organized cumulatively around FoA→DA→LTM. The architecture’s cost and continuity story depends on information moving among these levels. | Because ablations only add layers in one order, they do not establish that three layers are better than a two-layer or flat system at matched tokens, calls, and storage. [§3.2, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118#page=4) |
| F5 | **Use semantic vector retrieval to activate persistent memories** (D08–D09, D19). | LTM can help only if useful prior strategies are found and admitted; retrieval quality controls both gains and contamination. | No retrieval ablation, recall/precision measurement, top-k sensitivity, hybrid baseline, or oracle retrieval is reported. The choice is load-bearing but evidentially unresolved. [Appendix A.2, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118#page=7) |
| F6 | **Distill and revise model-generated reasoning rather than preserve only source evidence** (D04–D05, D12–D14). | The system’s compactness and claimed recovery depend on summaries and notes standing in for prior turns. | Final accuracy cannot show whether a correct summary preserved the right evidence, whether an incorrect one later self-corrected, or whether unsupported content entered LTM. No memory-quality audit is reported. |
| F7 | **Reuse/inherit cached session state** (D01, D16). | The efficiency rationale explicitly depends on avoiding redundant computation and continuing from cached memories/notes. | No ablation isolates reuse/inheritance, and there is no invalidation or false-inheritance metric. Its contribution to Table 1 is not separately identifiable. [§2.5, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118#page=3) |
| F8 | **Update memory asynchronously** (D11). | The latency claim depends on moving maintenance off the response path; making writes synchronous would change response latency and possibly next-turn consistency. | The paper provides no wall-clock latency, tail latency, queue lag, race rate, or write-failure results. This remains a load-bearing implementation claim without direct evidence. |

For KiVi, D14, D20, D22, D23, D24, and D25 are also forced choices—but for **KiVi’s** trust contract rather than for CogMem’s published score. CogMem does not supply experimental evidence on them.

## 3. Unexamined defaults

These are choices the paper makes implicitly or inherits from memory-augmented LLM work without treating them as live design questions.

| Default | Where it appears | Alternative this line of work rarely centers |
|---|---|---|
| **The assistant may be the author, curator, and consumer of memory.** | A memory agent summarizes outputs, maintains notes, rewrites retrieval queries, and consolidates LTM. | Split authority: models propose; deterministic policy admits; users confirm contested classes; provenance remains authoritative. |
| **Assistant-generated text is eligible to become evidence for future assistant reasoning.** | The model response is part of each new turn record (D12). | Treat outputs as untrusted projections that may be cached but never support durable beliefs unless grounded in user input or tool results. |
| **“Relevant” means semantically similar.** | Milvus similarity retrieves all memories (D09, D19). | Define relevance as a constrained relation among identity, temporal validity, authority, permission, source ownership, and task—not a scalar similarity score. |
| **Memory is organized first by cognitive timescale.** | FoA/DA/LTM mirror a cognitive model. | Organize first by governance: who the memory is about, who supplied it, what may use it, what may disclose it, and how it can be revoked. |
| **A session is the natural unit of continuity and consolidation.** | Reuse, prefix inheritance, cache, cleanup, and LTM review all hinge on sessions. | Use projects, entities, commitments, or user-declared scopes; allow one utterance to participate in several scopes without merging them. |
| **Compression error is an accuracy problem, not a rights problem.** | Summaries and distilled notes replace full history in active reasoning. | Treat compression as a derived view that cannot change ownership, sensitivity, consent, epistemic tier, or source accessibility. |
| **Persistence is opt-out only in principle, not governed at write time.** | The system autonomously adds/merges/excludes; privacy and consent are deferred. | Purpose-limited writes with hard pre-storage exclusions and verifiable “read, not kept” outcomes. |
| **If a later answer is correct, recovery succeeded.** | Error mitigation is inferred from benchmark accuracy. | Require old beliefs to be superseded, invalidated in caches, prevented from regrowing, and visible in an audit history. |
| **Internal structure is enough to call a system interpretable.** | CogMem calls the context bounded and interpretable but defines no user surface. | Operational interpretability: show retrieved, used, withheld, and discarded items plus the source and governing rule. |
| **All retrieved memory may influence and surface in the answer.** | No retrieval/disclosure split exists. | Retrieve broadly for internal reasoning while independently gating what may be said, or enforce channel-specific stores with different rights. |
| **Synthetic non-personal tasks are an adequate proving ground for persistent memory architecture.** | All experiments use TurnBench-MS; no PII/sensitive data are used. | Evaluate memory specifically under consent withdrawal, third-party data, changing identity, ambiguous ownership, sensitive inference, and adversarial correction. |
| **Token count can stand in for operational efficiency.** | Appendix C reports tokens per turn; §2.4 asserts stable latency. | Measure end-to-end and tail latency, model calls, vector-search time, background work, cache memory, storage growth, invalidation cost, and cost per successful task. |
| **Chain-of-thought is ordinary internal material for the architecture.** | All configurations use CoT; persistent memory stores distilled reasoning patterns. | Store outcomes, evidence, failed assumptions, or procedures without retaining/generated free-form reasoning traces. |

## 4. Questions the paper never faced

These arise from KiVi’s stated users, surfaces, constraints, and failure costs—not from gaps in CogMem alone.

### Write-path eligibility and ownership

1. When one interaction contains the user’s dictation, another person’s message, application context, and KiVi’s generated reply, **which spans are eligible to create which memory types, and what proof of subject ownership is required?**
2. When third-party personal information is necessary to draft a humane response, **what representation can enter transient context while being structurally incapable of persistence, logging, embeddings, caches, or later distillation?**
3. When a work fact and a personal fact are entangled—“Priya moved the review because her mother is ill”—**should the system retain the operational residue, retain a redacted causal link, or retain nothing?**
4. When an excluded candidate is logged as dropped, **how much of the candidate may the log contain before the proof of non-retention becomes a second copy of the forbidden information?**
5. When content is both a lexical aid and sensitive personal information, **which right wins: transcription accuracy, temporary use, or categorical non-retention?**

### Epistemic state and change

6. When repeated behavior supports an observation but the user has explicitly rejected it, **what should dominate: new behavioral evidence, the suppression event, elapsed time, or a new confirmation request?**
7. When a user correction changes an entity relation (“Priya is at Northwind now”), **which derived episodes, aliases, retrieval indexes, cached contexts, and generated drafts must be invalidated or temporally scoped?**
8. When two stated memories conflict, **what should determine whether the newer statement supersedes the older one, describes a different context, or requires clarification?**
9. When a stated preference was true for one audience or project, **how should scope be represented so a general-looking rule does not silently overgeneralize?**
10. When an observation decays, **should its evidence remain visible, should its ranking weight fall, should it become inaccessible, or should the derived memory be deleted while sources remain?**
11. When an unconfirmed hypothesis expires, **must every trace disappear—including caches and logs—or only the active hypothesis record?**
12. When “Forget” and “That’s not me anymore” both suppress re-derivation, **what distinct future evidence, if any, may reopen each decision?**

### Retrieval, permission, and disclosure

13. When Anbu retrieves an observation it cannot disclose, **may that observation still influence word choice, task planning, ranking, or tool use, or must it be causally isolated from the answer?**
14. When the interface says “Kivi noticed something here,” **what information can that affordance reveal without itself disclosing the withheld observation?**
15. When Daari is invited for one answer, **which exact user phrases count as invitation, how is ambiguity handled, and what prevents that temporary permission from persisting in session state or caches?**
16. When a memory is relevant but comes from a surface with narrower rights, **should the current surface’s contract, the source surface’s contract, or the memory’s own policy determine use?**
17. When regular dictation can access lexical/entity memory but not observations, **how are mixed requests classified when one utterance both dictates text and asks KiVi to act or reflect?**
18. When several memories are relevant but differ in tier, provenance strength, recency, and user pinning, **which signal determines ranking and which merely determines eligibility?**

### Legibility, correction, and failure

19. When the Why panel exposes a source transcript containing third-party or excluded material, **what may be shown, redacted, or reconstructed while preserving meaningful provenance?**
20. When KiVi abstains, **what evidence of the search is useful to a normal user without revealing withheld content or creating false confidence that the search was exhaustive?**
21. When a memory affected a tool action rather than prose, **what must the trace show so the user can understand and reverse the action?**
22. When an asynchronous extractor writes after the user has corrected or deleted something, **which event ordering prevents stale work from resurrecting it?**
23. When a false entity match changes a real email, schedule, or reply, **what is the recovery contract: prevent sending, preview the grounding, undo the action, repair memory, or all as distinct layers?**
24. When source evidence is itself wrong or later edited, **does provenance make the belief trustworthy, or merely attributable—and how is that distinction communicated?**

### Scale and evaluation

25. As one user accumulates years of transcripts, **what should bound candidate generation, contradiction checks, suppression checks, and source retention without making old but pinned facts unreachable?**
26. When multiple people or organizations share a project, **what constitutes the boundary of “the user’s world” without turning third-party information into a shadow profile?**
27. When the memory system improves task completion but increases unwanted personalization, **which outcome counts as a regression?**
28. When the same correct response can be produced with or without a prohibited memory, **what evaluation can detect that the system used the wrong reason?**
29. When users rarely open the memory surface, **does that indicate trust, invisibility, or failure to notice incorrect beliefs—and what observable behavior distinguishes them?**
30. When confirmation prompts are rationed, **what should allocate the budget: expected task value, uncertainty, harm of being wrong, frequency of likely reuse, or user burden?**

## 5. Evaluation gap

### What the paper measured

- **Primary outcome:** rule-prediction accuracy on 45 TurnBench-MS classic-mode setups, split into easy, medium, and hard ([§3.1 and Appendix A.1, pp. 3, 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118#page=3)).
- **Ablations:** baseline Gemini 2.5 Flash + CoT; +FoA; +FoA+DA; +FoA+DA+LTM, with reported total accuracies 0.76, 0.76, 0.84, and 0.93 ([Table 1, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118#page=4)).
- **Efficiency:** a plotted comparison of tokens processed per turn; CogMem is reported to use less than half the baseline tokens after 15 turns ([Appendix C, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118#page=8)).
- **Scope:** one benchmark mode and one base reasoning model. The paper itself identifies this as a generalizability limitation ([Limitations, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14118#page=5)).

The paper does **not** report memory extraction precision/recall, retrieval recall, ranking quality, contradiction resolution, source faithfulness, privacy leakage, consent compliance, deletion correctness, cache invalidation, abstention calibration, user trust, correction effort, real latency, tail latency, storage growth, or dollar cost.

### Decisions its metrics cannot distinguish

| Indistinguishable alternatives under the reported evaluation | Affected decisions | Why the same score is possible |
|---|---|---|
| One correct rule answer produced from faithful source evidence vs from a hallucinated summary that happens to land on the right rule | D05, D06, D12, D14 | Final accuracy checks the rule output, not whether memory content is faithful or the reasoning path is grounded. |
| Vector-only retrieval vs hybrid, graph, structured, or oracle retrieval | D08, D09, D19 | No retrieval-level metrics or matched retrieval ablation are reported. Any method returning enough useful context can receive the same final score. |
| Three memory layers vs two layers or a flat store with matched context and calls | D03 | The cumulative ablation removes named components but does not hold information, tokens, model calls, or storage constant across alternative architectures. |
| Correct answer with minimal context vs correct answer with irrelevant or sensitive memory also present | D06, D20, D24 | Accuracy and token count do not test unnecessary influence or privacy leakage. |
| Immediate consistent writes vs delayed/racing asynchronous writes | D11 | No next-turn race, queue lag, failure, or latency distribution is measured. |
| Corrected answer while stale memory remains vs corrected answer after durable supersession and cache invalidation | D14, D16, D22 | The benchmark can award the same final answer in both cases and does not inspect stored state or replay after correction. |
| Memory that can be traced to a source vs unattributable distilled strategy | D04, D05, D21 | Provenance is not evaluated; both can supply the same rule. |
| Autonomous consolidation vs explicit user-confirmed promotion | D13 | TurnBench has no consent or personal-belief semantics. Both policies can preserve the same task clue. |
| Keep all content vs hard sensitive/third-party exclusions | D15, D24 | The benchmark contains no PII or sensitive information, so the policies are observationally equivalent. |
| Retrieve-and-disclose everything vs retrieve broadly and disclose by permission | D20, D23 | There are no modes, personal observations, or user-facing disclosure tests. |
| Guessing correctly vs calibrated abstention on absent evidence | D07, D25 | Only correctness on answerable rule tasks is reported; selective-risk/coverage is not. |
| Session-prefix inheritance vs project/entity/task scopes | D01, D16 | The benchmark does not report false merges, false splits, invalidation, or cross-scope leakage. |
| RAM/Redis + Milvus vs another storage topology | D17–D18 | Token usage and final accuracy do not expose durability, deletion, residency, operational cost, or GC behavior. |
| A user-visible, reversible memory surface vs invisible internal memory | D21–D22 | There is no user study or interaction metric; both produce identical benchmark outputs. |
| Storing assistant outputs as evidence vs excluding them from durable belief formation | D12 | If outputs happen to be correct in the benchmark, self-reinforcement is not exposed. |

### What evidence the paper does provide for KiVi

The experiment provides precedent—not a decision—for three narrower propositions:

1. On TurnBench-MS with the stated Gemini models, **compact, reconstructed context can avoid linear prompt growth without reducing total accuracy relative to the reported baseline**; FoA alone ties total accuracy while changing results by difficulty.
2. In that setup, **session-level structured notes correlate with a material incremental accuracy gain**.
3. In that setup, **cross-session persistent memory correlates with a further gain**.

It supplies no comparative evidence for KiVi’s content taxonomy, epistemic tiers, user permissions, third-party non-retention, sensitive exclusions, correction semantics, provenance surface, suppression against re-derivation, dictation/Hey KiVi boundary, or abstention contract. On those decisions, CogMem is a precedent only in the weak sense that it demonstrates one memory architecture can improve one reasoning benchmark.

## Bottom line without choosing

CogMem’s strongest evidence concerns **where compressed reasoning state can live across time**. KiVi’s hardest questions concern **what is allowed to become a belief, whose information it is, how belief and disclosure differ, and how a person can contest the result**. The overlap is real at the levels of bounded context, session notes, cross-session retrieval, and lifecycle management. The paper’s evaluation stops just before the choices that are load-bearing for KiVi’s product position.
