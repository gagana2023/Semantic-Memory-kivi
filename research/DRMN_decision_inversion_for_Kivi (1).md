# DRMN Decision Inversion for Kivi

This document inverts Ji et al., *A Neural Conversation Generation Model via Equivalent Shared Memory Investigation* (CIKM 2021), into the questions its authors answered—deliberately or implicitly—and tests the assumptions behind those answers against Kivi. It does **not** recommend answers. “Underspecified” means the paper does not supply enough information to infer one safely.

## Reference setting: Kivi

Kivi is a text-based, user-specific semantic-memory system. It processes transcripts, application context, and explicit “Hey Kivi” requests; retains attributable work-level entities, preferences, and episodes; distinguishes stated, observed, and hypothesised beliefs; excludes sensitive and third-party personal content on the write path; separates retrieval from disclosure; exposes provenance and withholding; supports correction, demotion, suppression, supersession, and decay; and is expected to abstain visibly when evidence is absent. Ordinary dictation has only lexical/entity memory, while Hey Kivi has tier-gated semantic memory. Storage/retrieval mechanics, extraction timing, thresholds, decay windows, budgets, cost, and latency remain deliberately open. [Kivi position, §§1–9 and Appendix B]

The paper solves a different task: predict the next judge or customer-service-agent utterance from the preceding conversation, helped by patterns in other same-domain conversations. Its “memory” is an ephemeral neural representation of retrieved corpus conversations, not a durable, editable record of one person. [Paper §§1, 3, 4.1]

## 1. Decision inventory

“Kivi fit” indicates whether the paper’s assumption transfers—not which alternative Kivi should choose. **FC** marks a forced choice expanded in §2.

| D9 | What participant information should affect meaning? | Concatenate a learned role embedding with every word because roles use different vocabularies. | No roles; identity embeddings; permissions/ownership; relationship graph; role-conditioned retrieval. | A small closed role set (judge/plaintiff/customer/agent) is stable and sufficient. | **Does not hold.** Kivi’s person identities, self/third-party boundary, and consent status are load-bearing; generic role embeddings are too coarse. | Paper §3.1, p. 3; Kivi §5. |
| D10 | What representation should initialize lexical meaning and participant roles? | 300-d word2vec word vectors; random 100-d role vectors; BiLSTMs. | Contextual embeddings; character/subword models; structured IDs; current LLM embeddings. | Training scale supports learning; vocabulary/OOV can be handled later by copying; inference stack is acceptable. | **Undetermined and weakly transferable.** Kivi’s open mechanics and proper-name accuracy push attention toward subword/entity representations, but the paper provides no Kivi-relevant comparison. | Paper §§3.1, 4.4. |
| D11 **FC** | When producing text, should the system generate, copy from memory, retrieve verbatim, or choose among them? | Learn a token-level pointer probability that mixes generated vocabulary with copies from ESM. | Pure generation; pure retrieval; span-level citation; constrained generation; tool/action output. | Reference text is safe and applicable; surface overlap is valuable; copying solves OOV. | **Often does not hold.** Kivi may use a memory without disclosing it, and copied third-party/sensitive text is specifically dangerous. | D12 | What should be optimized during training? | Teacher-forced next-utterance cross-entropy. | Retrieval relevance loss; factuality/provenance loss; abstention; privacy violations; preference/user-control loss; task success. | The historical next utterance is the correct target and lexical likelihood proxies product quality. | **Does not hold.** Kivi has multiple acceptable responses and non-linguistic correctness constraints. Pressure is toward evaluating memory behavior separately from response imitation. | Paper §3.3, p. 4; Kivi Appendix C. |
| D13 | What is discarded before or during memory construction? | Attention softly downweights material; no explicit content category is excluded. | D14 **FC** | When retrieved evidence conflicts or contradicts, what should determine which version influences output? | **Underspecified.** Similarity and attention weights implicitly arbitrate; no contradiction operation exists. | Recency; authority; user confirmation; retain conflict; supersede with history; abstain. | Similar dialogues share compatible patterns often enough; output imitation does not require belief reconciliation. | **Does not hold.** Kivi explicitly requires contradictions to supersede while preserving history; conflict errors can become durable beliefs. | Paper §§3.2–3.3 (absence); Kivi §9. |
| D15 | Should memory be durable and updated over time, or reconstructed per prediction from a fixed corpus? | Reconstruct an ephemeral ESM per target from historical corpus conversations; “update” means within-pass neural accumulation. Corpus update/deletion behavior is **underspecified**. | Persistent user store; event-sourced memory; streaming updates; scheduled consolidation. | Evaluation is static and offline; users do not inspect or revise memory. | **Does not hold.** Kivi is longitudinal and revisable; pressure is toward explicit lifecycle semantics. | Paper §3.2.2, Eqs. 10–11; §4.1; Kivi §§4, 9. |
| D16 | Should memories carry epistemic status—stated, observed, hypothesised—or be treated uniformly? | Uniform latent content; no epistemic tier or evidence-count semantics. | Typed tiers; confidence/calibration; facts vs patterns vs questions. | Corpus recurrence is enough to license use; there is no user-facing claim ledger. | **Does not hold.** Kivi’s product distinction rests on tiers and explicit confirmation. | Paper §§3.2–3.3 (absence); Kivi §4. |
| D17 **FC** | Should retrieval and disclosure be governed together or separately? | No separation: retrieved memory can directly affect/copy into output. | Retrieve broadly then gate disclosure; retrieve only permitted material; per-use permission. | Any relevant content may be shown; there is no user-adjustable permission model. | **Direct conflict.** Kivi’s central calibration principle separates knowing from saying and logs withheld memory. | Paper §3.3; Kivi §§1, 6, 8. |
| D18 | When evidence is weak or absent, should the system answer, retrieve a generic pattern, ask, or abstain? | Always generate; error analysis records failures on rare/absent words but no abstention mechanism. | Calibrated abstention; clarification; explicit search report; fallback tool. | A plausible turn is preferable to no turn; errors are scored at corpus level. | **Does not hold.** Kivi forbids plausible gap-filling and makes actionable abstention a designed result. | Paper §5.4, pp. 7–8; Kivi §8. |
| D19 | What should users see about memory use? | Only the generated utterance; researchers receive two case studies and attention heatmaps. | Per-answer sources; retrieved/used/withheld trace; editable memory surface; confidence. | End users need output, not inspectability; attention is adequate diagnostic evidence. | **Does not hold.** Kivi requires a user-readable Why panel and source transcript access. | Paper §5.3, Figs. 3–4; Kivi §§8–9. |
| D20 | When the system is wrong, who can correct it and what persists? | No runtime correction path; errors motivate future model/database improvement. | Inline user correction; deletion; demotion; suppression against re-derivation; appeal/review. | Failure cost is captured in aggregate evaluation and fixed by retraining. | **Does not hold.** Kivi requires one-action correction and durable suppression. | Paper §§5.4, 6; Kivi §§8–9. |
| D21 | How should old evidence lose influence as the world changes? | It does not; time/recency are not modeled. | Decay; time-aware ranking; validity intervals; supersession; user pinning. | Domain scripts are stationary enough; conversation age is irrelevant. | **Does not hold.** Revisability and observation decay are explicit Kivi commitments. | Paper §§3–4 (absence); Kivi §§1, 9. |
| D22 | How should the system distinguish surfaces with different contracts? | One next-utterance generation setup per domain; no separate rights by surface. | Separate retrieval paths; task-specific permissions; transcription-only path. | All inputs ask for conversational continuation. | **Does not hold.** Kivi separates ordinary dictation structurally from Hey Kivi. | Paper §§4.1.1–4.1.2; Kivi §7. |
| D23 | What evidence should establish generality? | Two large Chinese real-world datasets from legal and e-commerce domains. | Cross-user longitudinal evaluation; multilingual tests; temporal shift; privacy-critical corpora; live deployment. | Domain diversity demonstrates adaptability despite both tasks being formulaic next-turn prediction. | **Weak fit.** Kivi’s generalization axis is across time, users, tasks, and permissions—not merely domains. | Paper §§1, 4.1, 5.1. |
| D24 **FC** | What should count as success? | BLEU/ROUGE plus five NLP researchers rating relevance and fluency on 300 samples/model. | Factuality, source entailment, privacy leakage, calibration, abstention, correction durability, task success, user trust. | Reference overlap and immediate utterance quality dominate failure cost. | **Does not hold.** Kivi’s defining claims are invisible to these metrics. | Paper §4.3, Tables 3–4; Kivi Appendix C. |

## 2. Forced choices

These are load-bearing either because the paper tested them directly, because they define its task, or because changing them would invalidate how its result is interpreted.

| ID | Forced choice | Why load-bearing in the paper | What must be thought through for Kivi (without selecting an answer) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Corpus analogies vs personal/curated memory | ESM exists only if other conversations are treated as reusable memory. | Whether cross-user material may influence a personal assistant at all, and how that interacts with non-retention. |
| D2 | Last-turn-centered querying | The entire ESM attention path is conditioned on the last target utterance. | Whether task, entity, time, user history, and current request need independent query signals. |
| D4 | Sparse-then-semantic retrieval | Determines what can ever reach ESM; recall errors cannot be repaired downstream. | Which policy filters precede retrieval, what ranking objective means, and how retrieval failure becomes visible. |
| D5 | Fixed top-*k* | More retrieved conversations materially increased automatic scores, but also cost. | Whether weak evidence yields zero items and whether breadth varies by dictation, recall, drafting, or action. |
| D7 | Interactive ESM vs concatenation/no ESM | Ablation: removing ESM reduced BLEU by 34.3% on CDD and 41.3% on JDDC; concatenation also fell materially. | Whether an intermediate memory must be latent, structured, inspectable, or some combination. |
| D11 | Generate/copy mixture | It is the mechanism by which retrieved phrases enter output and addresses OOV. | Whether memory may shape an answer without appearing verbatim; what can be copied; how sources remain attributable. |
| D14 | Conflict resolution | The paper avoids it; Kivi cannot, because contradictions persist across time. | What qualifies as contradiction, what establishes authority/recency, and what the user sees. |
| D17 | Retrieval vs disclosure | The paper fuses them; Kivi’s central product promise separates them. | Whether gating occurs before retrieval, after retrieval, at generation, or at all three—with auditable traces. |
| D24 | Success definition | All reported superiority depends on overlap/relevance/fluency metrics. | Which independent tests establish retention boundaries, truth, calibration, correction, and lifecycle behavior. |

## 3. Unexamined defaults

These are choices the paper largely inherits from 2021 neural dialogue generation rather than frames as choices.

| Default | The alternative this line of work rarely tests | Why it matters here | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|
| The logged next utterance is ground truth. | Treat the next turn as one acceptable action among many; evaluate constraints and task outcomes. | A high-overlap Kivi answer can still expose forbidden memory or use a stale belief. | Paper §§3.3, 4.3. |
| Similarity implies permission to reuse. | Model eligibility/consent separately from relevance. | Kivi explicitly distinguishes readable context from retainable memory. | Paper §§3.2–3.3; Kivi §5. |
| More relevant context is monotonically beneficial until compute cost intervenes. | Measure privacy, contradiction, distraction, and calibration costs of additional context. | Kivi’s cost of an extra memory can be disclosure or durable mischaracterisation. | Paper §5.1. |
| Attention both selects and explains. | Require source entailment, faithful attribution, and a user-readable decision trace. | An attention heatmap does not prove why a claim was made or that withheld content stayed withheld. | Paper §5.3, Fig. 4. |
| “Memory” may be an opaque temporary vector. | Define memory as a user-visible, typed, reversible record with lifecycle rules. | The same word describes fundamentally different objects in DRMN and Kivi. | Paper §3.2; Kivi §§4, 9. |
| All corpus utterances are legitimate training and retrieval material. | Retain provenance, consent, subject, sensitivity, and deletion obligations through the pipeline. | The paper never models the person whom a fact is about. | Paper §4.1 (no governance discussion). |
| Conflict can be absorbed statistically. | Represent incompatible claims explicitly and sometimes refuse synthesis. | Longitudinal personal facts change; averaging them can fossilize the wrong state. | Paper §3.2 (absence); Kivi §9. |
| Failure means poor text, repaired by better modeling/data. | Treat unauthorized disclosure, false confidence, uncorrectable belief, and re-derivation as distinct failures. | Kivi’s highest-cost failures may be perfectly fluent. | Paper §5.4. |
| Static random splits demonstrate deployment utility. | Split by time, person, organization, and policy boundary; test updates and deletions. | Leakage of recurring templates can reward memorization without testing evolving personal memory. Split/dedup details are **underspecified**. | Paper §§4.1, 4.4. |
| Speaker role is sufficient social context. | Track information ownership, subject, audience, consent, and relationship. | “Priya said X” and “X is about Meera” have different memory rights. | Paper §3.1; Kivi §5. |
| The system should always emit a turn. | Make silence, abstention, clarification, or search disclosure first-class outputs. | Kivi explicitly treats abstention as a feature. | Paper §§3.3, 5.4; Kivi §8. |
| Model quality is separable from interface design. | Evaluate memory controls and explanations as part of system behavior. | Kivi’s permission dial, Why panel, and memory surface implement core semantics. | Paper §4.3; Kivi §§6, 8–9. |

## 4. Questions the paper never faced

These arise from Kivi’s users, constraints, and failure modes—not from gaps in DRMN alone.

1. **When a transcript contains the user, coworkers, clients, and bystanders, what should determine who a candidate memory is about and whether it may cross the write barrier?** [Kivi §§2, 5]
2. **When useful work facts and prohibited personal facts occur in the same sentence, should extraction redact, derive a narrower residue, drop the whole candidate, or ask?** [Kivi §5]
3. **When a fact is safe to retain but unsafe or unhelpful to disclose now, where should the permission decision be enforced and what trace should remain?** [Kivi §§1, 6, 8]
4. **When repeated behavior suggests a preference, what evidence count, diversity, and time span justify calling it an observation—without silently turning it into a rule?** [Kivi §4; Appendix B]
5. **When a user confirms, corrects, demotes, forgets, or says “not me anymore,” which source evidence remains, which future derivations are blocked, and for how long?** [Kivi §9]
6. **When a contradiction reflects genuine change rather than extraction error, what should be superseded, what should remain queryable as history, and what should be shown by default?** [Kivi §9]
7. **When evidence is absent, what search account is sufficiently specific to make abstention actionable without leaking withheld material?** [Kivi §8]
8. **When a memory is relevant to accurate transcription but not semantic assistance, what storage and retrieval boundary prevents observations/hypotheses from entering ordinary dictation?** [Kivi §7]
9. **When the user invokes Daari for one request, what may be computed transiently, what may be displayed, and what—if anything—may be written afterward?** [Kivi §6]
10. **When a hypothesis receives silence, rejection, partial agreement, or correction, what state transition and expiry rule follows?** [Kivi §§4, 8–9]
11. **When confirmation prompts impose cognitive cost, what should determine which uncertain memory is worth asking about within the budget?** [Kivi §8; Appendix B]
12. **When multiple sources support a memory but one becomes unavailable or is deleted, does the memory survive, lose standing, or require re-confirmation?** [Derived from Kivi §§4, 8–9]
13. **When an excluded candidate is logged to prove enforcement, how much can the log reveal without itself retaining the prohibited content?** [Kivi §2]
14. **When a user asks Kivi to forget, should suppression be keyed to wording, semantic proposition, source evidence, category, or some combination so the belief does not regrow?** [Kivi §9]
15. **When the same entity name refers to different people/projects, what evidence should resolve identity, and when should Kivi ask instead of merge?** [Kivi §§3–5]
16. **When memory improves task completion but makes the assistant feel intrusive, which observable behavior distinguishes helpful recall from an unwanted demonstration of surveillance?** [Kivi §§1, 3, 6]
17. **When an answer uses stated facts plus withheld observations, how can Kivi prove the withheld observation did not influence wording indirectly?** [Kivi §§6, 8]
18. **When users, organizations, or regulations require export, deletion, or retention limits, what is the atomic unit and how are derived memories and indexes covered?** [Derived from Kivi’s attribution/reversibility commitments]
19. **When memory quality, privacy, latency, and cost conflict, which failures get hard constraints and which become tunable tradeoffs?** [Kivi Appendix B]
20. **When a safe work-level fact is learned from third-party content, what makes the derived residue sufficiently non-personal and attributable without preserving the source’s private detail?** [Kivi §5]

## 5. Evaluation gap

### What the paper measured

- **Automatic:** ROUGE-1, ROUGE-L, and BLEU against one recorded next utterance. [Paper §4.3.1; Table 3]
- **Human:** five NLP researchers scored 300 outputs per model on relevance and fluency, using 0/1/2; highest and lowest ratings were removed, and mean and κ reported. [Paper §4.3.2; Table 4]
- **Ablation:** no ESM, simple target+similar-conversation concatenation, and top-1/top-2/top-3 ESM. [Paper §5.2; Tables 5–6]
- **Diagnostic:** two case studies and attention visualization. [Paper §5.3; Figs. 3–4]
- **Error analysis:** rare/absent terms, sparse product attributes, and long-sentence fluency failures. [Paper §5.4; Table 7]

### What those metrics can and cannot distinguish

| Decision(s) | Distinguishable by the paper’s evaluation? | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| D7 interactive ESM vs concatenation/no ESM | **Yes, within these datasets.** | Ablations support interactive ESM for overlap and rated immediate quality; they do not isolate every architectural component inside ESM. |
| D5 top-1 vs top-2 vs top-3 | **Partly.** | Automatic scores generally rise; human scores are mixed, and >3 or adaptive/zero-*k* are untested. |
| D1 use similar dialogues vs current context only | **Partly.** | -ESM and retrieval-oriented baselines show benefit, but personal memory and governed knowledge alternatives are absent. |
| D11 copy/generate mixture | **Weakly.** | PGN/CCN baselines and examples suggest copied rare phrases help, but there is no direct ablation of DRMN’s pointer or measurement of incorrect/forbidden copying. |
| D2 last-turn query vs full/state/multi-query | **No.** | No query ablation. Different answers could score the same. The paper supplies precedent only. |
| D3 conversation vs turn/fact/entity unit | **No.** | No retrieval-unit comparison; metrics could reward either if the final string overlaps. |
| D4 candidate/reranking strategy | **No.** | No recall@*k*, reranker accuracy, threshold calibration, or alternative retriever evaluation; top-50 adequacy and similarity supervision are underspecified. |
| D6 order-sensitive vs set/temporal aggregation | **No.** | No order shuffle or temporal test. |
| D8–D10 representation and role choices | **Mostly no.** | Broad baselines differ in many variables; no controlled component ablations establish these choices. |
| D12 likelihood objective vs constraint/task objectives | **No.** | All training/evaluation is aligned to next-turn imitation; multiple valid answers and constraint violations are not observed. |
| D13 filtering/discard policy | **No.** | BLEU/relevance/fluency cannot distinguish safe exclusion from unauthorized use when text quality is identical. |
| D14 contradictions | **No.** | No contradiction set, time-aware gold state, or conflict behavior. Two answers can score identically while preserving incompatible beliefs. |
| D15 and D21 durability/update/decay | **No.** | Static offline splits contain no longitudinal lifecycle test. |
| D16 epistemic tiers | **No.** | A stated fact and an inferred pattern expressed in the same sentence receive the same score. |
| D17 retrieval vs disclosure | **No.** | Outputs reveal neither what was retrieved nor what should have been withheld. Two internal policies can yield identical strings. |
| D18 abstention | **No—and likely penalized.** | A correct abstention differs from the recorded answer and loses overlap; relevance rubric presumes an utterance, not calibrated refusal. |
| D19 transparency/provenance | **No.** | Attention case studies are researcher diagnostics, not user-facing faithful explanations. Identical outputs with and without provenance score the same. |
| D20 correction/suppression | **No.** | No interaction, reprocessing, or regrowth test. |
| D22 surface-specific memory rights | **No.** | Only next-turn conversational generation is tested. |
| D23 deployment generality | **Weakly.** | Two domains show some domain breadth, not user/time/language/policy generalization. |
| D24 Kivi success criteria | **No.** | Privacy, attribution, reversibility, calibration, abstention quality, and failure recovery remain unmeasured. |

### Bottom line on transferable evidence

The paper provides evidence that, in two large, repetitive Chinese dialogue corpora, retrieving analogous conversations and interacting with them through a dedicated latent memory can improve reference overlap and immediate relevance/fluency over the tested baselines. It provides **no comparative evidence** for nearly every decision that defines Kivi as a personal semantic-memory product. On those decisions, DRMN is a precedent—not evidence.
