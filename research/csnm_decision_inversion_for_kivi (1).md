# Inverting *Cross-Session Narrative Memory* for Kivi

## Scope and citation convention

This document inverts Mario Martín Cuniglio's *Cross-Session Narrative Memory: A Cognitive Architecture for Longitudinal Human-AI Integration* (CSNM) into the design questions its authors faced, then tests the assumptions behind those answers against Kivi as described in *The Things Kivi Comes to Know*.

Paper citations use section and PDF page, e.g. **CSNM §3.1, PDF p. 4**. Kivi citations use the section number of the attached position document. “Underspecified” means the paper does not supply an operational answer; no implementation is inferred.

## 1. Decision inventory

| ID | Design question (at least two defensible answers) | Paper's answer | Alternatives rejected or not considered | Assumptions behind the answer | Does it hold for Kivi? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Which interactions should be eligible to enter persistent memory: every interaction, only user-designated material, or automatically selected candidates? | Merge **all** user-AI interactions into one evolving corpus; the UCC stores the “complete longitudinal history.” (**CSNM §§1, 2.3, 3.1; PDF pp. 3-4**) | Selective capture; explicit save-only memory; source-specific eligibility; non-persistent sessions; extraction of durable facts without raw-history retention. | Total capture is acceptable; storage and review scale with lifetime use; all interactions belong to one user biography; the value of completeness exceeds privacy and deletion costs. | **No.** Kivi admits candidates selectively, excludes sensitive categories before storage, and structurally blocks third-party content from the write path (**Kivi §§2, 5**). Kivi's setting pushes toward minimization because a text assistant routinely sees material that is useful transiently but illegitimate to retain. |
| D2 | When an interaction contains multiple people, whose information may become durable memory? | The corpus is described as the “complete longitudinal history of the user,” but the treatment of third-party content is **underspecified**. (**CSNM §3.1; PDF p. 4**) | User-only write boundary; consent by subject; work-level relational residue; redaction or partitioning by data subject; unrestricted retention of all participants. | A user-centered corpus can be constructed without separately modeling data subjects or consent; “user history” is an adequate privacy boundary. | **No.** Kivi explicitly reads third-party content for the immediate task and does not retain it, keeping only non-characterizing work residue (**Kivi §5**). Kivi raises a consent and subject-resolution problem CSNM does not face. |
| D3 | What kinds of user information should be representable: events and explicit facts only, or also emotions, psychology, aspirations, and inferred identity? | Represent personal events, reflections, emotional states, reasoning patterns, aspirations, developmental changes, cognitive transformations, identity, emotional arcs, intentions, and psychological patterns. (**CSNM §§3.1-3.2; PDF pp. 4-5**) | Work-level facts only; explicit preferences; episodes without personality inference; hard exclusions for sensitive or unverifiable characterizations; hypotheses stored only as questions. | Psychological interpretation is desirable, sufficiently valid, and safe; the system is entitled to form a biographical model; errors in characterization are tolerable or correctable. | **Directly opposite.** Kivi rejects psychological profiling, emotion/mood storage, character judgments, and several sensitive categories (**Kivi §2**). The difference pushes toward a narrower ontology because Kivi optimizes for attributable, reversible work memory rather than identity reconstruction. |
| D4 | What should be the primary representation of long-term memory: raw interaction history, atomic structured memories, a narrative model, or a hybrid? | A unified full-history corpus plus a dynamic narrative model; concrete data structures are **underspecified**. (**CSNM §§3.1-3.2; PDF pp. 4-5**) | Typed atomic records; event graph; vector-only episodic store; immutable log plus derived views; user-authored profile; separate lexical/entity/preference/episode stores. | Narrative is the correct organizing form for meaning; a coherent biography is more useful than independently attributable claims; narrative construction can preserve relevant detail. | **Mostly no.** Kivi specifies typed memories (entity, preference, episode) crossed with epistemic tiers and source IDs (**Kivi §4**). A narrative layer could collapse distinctions Kivi treats as product-critical, especially observation versus stated fact. |
| D5 | When evidence changes over time, should the system preserve competing claims, overwrite, supersede with history, or reconcile them into a narrative? | The NCL “constructs and updates” a dynamic model tracking evolving beliefs and continuity; conflict resolution is **underspecified**. (**CSNM §3.2; PDF p. 5**) | Append-only contradictions; recency wins; source reliability; confidence aggregation; explicit user arbitration; supersession with audit history; scoped coexistence. | Narrative integration can reconcile change without a formal contradiction policy; coherence is both achievable and desirable. | **Not established.** Kivi requires contradictions to supersede prior memories while retaining history, and corrections can suppress re-derivation (**Kivi §9**). Kivi needs operational semantics because stale beliefs and regrowth are explicit failure modes. |
| D6 | Should repeated observation automatically become a stronger belief or require explicit user confirmation before changing epistemic status? | Pattern detection and evolving models are endorsed, but promotion rules are **underspecified**. (**CSNM §§3.2-3.3; PDF p. 5**) | Automatic frequency-based promotion; Bayesian confidence; time-weighted evidence; explicit confirmation; permanent separation of observations and asserted facts. | Accumulated history legitimately strengthens the identity model; the user need not authorize a change in status. | **No.** Nothing self-promotes in Kivi; observation-to-stated promotion requires explicit confirmation (**Kivi §4**). The difference pushes toward consent-gated epistemic changes because silent inference would break Kivi's control promise. |
| D7 | What should be discarded before or after storage, and on what basis? | Nothing is identified for exclusion; the UCC is described as complete. (**CSNM §3.1; PDF p. 4**) | Sensitive-topic exclusions; third-party exclusions; low-value/transient filtering; retention periods; duplicate compression; source deletion propagation; user-defined boundaries. | More history is better; risks from irrelevant, sensitive, or third-party data do not outweigh completeness; cost is not constraining. | **No.** Kivi has a hard exclusion check, dropped-candidate logging, and a third-party write barrier (**Kivi §§2, 5**). Its trust case depends on proving non-retention, so “complete” memory pushes in the wrong direction. |
| D8 | Should old information decay, remain indefinitely, or change standing based on type, reaffirmation, and time? | Longitudinal persistence and developmental tracking are required; retention and decay are **underspecified**. (**CSNM §§3.1-3.3, 5; PDF pp. 4-7**) | Indefinite retention; time decay; type-specific expiry; user-set retention; event permanence with observation decay; confirmed facts retained until contradicted. | Long-term continuity benefits from durable history; storage and resurfacing of old states are acceptable; historical self-modeling outweighs risks of freezing the user in the past. | **Partly.** Kivi retains auditable history but observations decay and unconfirmed hypotheses expire (**Kivi §9**). Kivi's revisability requirement pushes toward type-specific lifecycle rules, absent from CSNM. |
| D9 | What should retrieval optimize for: semantic relevance, narrative coherence, recency, epistemic authority, source type, or task-specific constraints? | The LRE reasons across time using the longitudinal architecture; retrieval and ranking are **underspecified**. (**CSNM §3.3; PDF p. 5**) | Embedding similarity; structured query; hybrid ranking; recency; contradiction-aware ranking; tier gating; task-specific stores; full-context summarization. | A reasoning engine can access the needed history at feasible cost and latency; narrative integration itself yields useful selection. | **Open for Kivi.** Kivi explicitly leaves embeddings versus structured query versus hybrid unresolved (**Kivi Appendix B**), but requires different retrieval paths for dictation and Hey Kivi (**Kivi §7**). CSNM supplies no comparative evidence for the mechanism or ranking objective. |
| D10 | Should all product surfaces have the same memory access, or should access rights depend on the interaction contract? | A single architecture is proposed for user-AI interactions; surface-specific access is not discussed. (**CSNM §§1, 3; PDF pp. 3-5**) | Uniform access; capability-specific stores; lexical/entity-only dictation; full interactive retrieval; per-tool policies; session-local exceptions. | All interactions benefit from longitudinal integration; surface contracts do not require structural separation. | **No.** Kivi makes ordinary dictation structurally unable to load observations or hypotheses, while Hey Kivi can use tier-gated memory (**Kivi §7**). The difference pushes toward separate paths because unsolicited interpretation would violate transcription expectations. |
| D11 | Should the system retrieve only what it may disclose, or retrieve broadly and gate what may be said? | **Underspecified.** The paper does not separate knowledge access from disclosure. (**CSNM §§3.2-3.3; PDF p. 5**) | Retrieve-after-permission filtering; retrieve-all then disclose by permission; redact at generation; separate models/stores; never compute prohibited inferences. | No explicit assumption can be established beyond treating integrated history as generally available to reasoning. | **Materially different.** Kivi retrieves broadly for Hey Kivi, then its permission dial governs disclosure; withheld items remain traceable (**Kivi §6**). For regular dictation, however, access itself is structurally restricted (**Kivi §7**). CSNM does not distinguish these policies. |
| D12 | Who should control inference depth: the architecture by default, an explicit persistent setting, a per-request invitation, or context/tone inference? | The LRE performs pattern detection, intention modeling, anticipation, and consistency monitoring; user-controlled inference permission is not described. (**CSNM §§3.3, 5.2-5.3; PDF pp. 5-7**) | No inference; explicit dial; per-request escalation; inferred permission from tone; per-memory consent; role- or task-based policy. | Deeper modeling is intrinsically beneficial and within the system's remit; proactive anticipation is desirable; permission need not be separately negotiated. | **No.** Kivi has explicit Anbu/Koottu permissions and makes hypothesis-level Daari a per-request invitation; tone cannot grant permission (**Kivi §6**). Kivi's relationship model pushes control to the user. |
| D13 | How should uncertain interpretations be encoded and expressed: claims with confidence, observations with evidence, questions, or not stored at all? | Psychological patterns and intention trajectories are modeled; uncertainty representation is **underspecified**. (**CSNM §3.2; PDF p. 5**) | Confidence scores; evidence-linked observations; hypotheses as questions; abstention; non-storage; multiple competing interpretations. | Narrative construction can state or integrate interpretations without a formal epistemic grammar; ambiguity does not require user-visible status. | **No.** Kivi separates stated, observed, and hypothesized tiers; hypotheses are questions and never act as facts (**Kivi §4**). The difference pushes toward explicit epistemic typing because characterization errors have high trust cost. |
| D14 | When evidence is absent or ambiguous, should the system abstain, ask, retrieve adjacent material, or generate the most coherent continuation? | The paper claims historical grounding reduces hallucinations, but abstention and missing-evidence behavior are **underspecified**. (**CSNM §§3.3, 4.3, 5.4; PDF pp. 5-7**) | Explicit abstention; clarification; adjacent-results disclosure; confidence threshold; plausible completion; external lookup. | More historical context improves factual stability; grounding is sufficient to reduce error without a specified decision rule. | **No evidence it holds.** Kivi treats abstention as a visible product result showing what was searched and what adjacent material exists (**Kivi §8**). Kivi needs a defined failure response, not only more context. |
| D15 | What provenance should accompany a memory or answer: none, source links, evidence counts, transformation history, or a full response trace? | No user-facing provenance mechanism is specified. (**CSNM §§3-6; PDF pp. 4-7**) | Transcript-level citations; evidence spans; evidence counts; derivation chain; used/withheld/retrieved trace; developer-only logging. | Narrative coherence can establish trust without exposing derivation; users need not inspect the model; source attribution is not central. | **No.** Every Kivi belief is source-attributable, and the Why panel exposes retrieved, withheld, used, and source transcript information (**Kivi §§1, 8-9**). Provenance is load-bearing for Kivi's revisability and trust claims. |
| D16 | What should users be able to do when memory is wrong: edit, delete, demote, supersede, suppress re-derivation, or merely correct the current answer? | Correction and user controls are **underspecified**. (**CSNM §§3-7; PDF pp. 4-8**) | Inline correction; edit/pin/forget; version history; demotion; suppression tombstones; appeal or review queue; full reset. | The dynamic model can update accurately without explicit correction mechanics; identity continuity is not threatened by model errors. | **No.** Kivi requires edit, confirm, demote, forget, provenance, supersession history, and suppression against regrowth (**Kivi §9**). Its failure cost is a durable false characterization, so correction semantics must be first-class. |
| D17 | Should a user-visible system expose a coherent biography, atomic memories grouped by epistemic status, or only task outputs? | The proposal centers a narrative/identity model but specifies no user interface. (**CSNM §§3.2, 5.1, 6; PDF pp. 5-7**) | Biography or timeline; tier-grouped memory surface; task-only invisibility; searchable event log; profile cards; inspectable graph. | Presenting or internally maintaining a coherent identity is desirable; legibility and correction can be deferred. | **No.** Kivi exposes atomic memories grouped as “Things you told me / noticed / wondering about,” with evidence and actions (**Kivi §§4, 9**). A narrative presentation could make disputing individual claims harder. |
| D18 | What should determine whether the system acts on a memory: relevance alone, epistemic tier, explicit confirmation, task risk, or user permission? | The LRE uses history for planning, alignment, consistency, anticipation, and need prediction; action policy is **underspecified**. (**CSNM §§3.3, 5; PDF pp. 5-7**) | Act only on stated facts; use observations only as prompts; never act on hypotheses; risk-tiered confirmation; user-configurable autonomy. | Inferred patterns and intentions are reliable enough to shape behavior; benefits of anticipation exceed costs of mistaken action. | **No.** Kivi treats stated preferences as rules, observations as evidence-linked suggestions, and hypotheses as non-actionable questions (**Kivi §§4, 6**). Different failure costs push toward tier-dependent action rights. |
| D19 | What should determine the unit and timing of memory extraction: per turn, per transcript, scheduled batch, periodic narrative synthesis, or on demand? | **Underspecified.** The paper names layers, not ingestion or update timing. (**CSNM §3; PDF pp. 4-5**) | Synchronous per-turn extraction; post-session extraction; batched jobs; event-triggered consolidation; user-initiated save; periodic reprocessing. | No operational scale, latency, or cost assumptions are stated. | **Open.** Kivi explicitly leaves per-transcript versus batch extraction undecided and says thresholds need corpus data (**Kivi Appendix B**). CSNM contributes no constraints or evidence for this choice. |
| D20 | How should a lifetime corpus fit within compute, context, and latency budgets: full replay, hierarchical summaries, retrieval, compression, or bounded windows? | The LRE is expected to reason “across time”; scaling mechanics and budgets are **underspecified**. (**CSNM §§3.3, 4.3; PDF pp. 5-6**) | Full replay; hierarchical summaries; episodic retrieval; graph traversal; bounded recent window; cold/hot tiers; lossy consolidation. | Lifetime-scale access is computationally feasible or will be solved later; cost and latency do not constrain the conceptual claim. | **Unknown and likely consequential.** Kivi leaves cost/latency targets open (**Kivi Appendix B**) but is a product with interactive dictation and request paths. Tight latency would push away from undifferentiated full-history reasoning. |
| D21 | What security and isolation boundary should protect a longitudinal memory: ordinary application storage, encrypted per-user vault, local-only storage, purpose-separated stores, or no persistent sensitive data? | **Underspecified.** (**CSNM §§3-7; PDF pp. 4-8**) | Encryption and key separation; local-first memory; purpose limitation; tenant isolation; access logs; zero-retention categories; export and deletion guarantees. | The benefits of biographical persistence can be discussed independently of breach impact, access control, and governance. | **Does not hold.** Kivi's corpus can include work relations and user preferences even after exclusions; its explicit trust claims and third-party boundary make enforcement and auditability part of the design, not deployment detail (**Kivi §§2, 5, 8**). |
| D22 | When the model derives a narrative or psychological interpretation from sources, should deleting a source also delete, recompute, or preserve the derived belief? | **Underspecified.** The UCC and NCL are linked conceptually, but deletion propagation is not addressed. (**CSNM §§3.1-3.2; PDF pp. 4-5**) | Cascade deletion; provenance-aware recomputation; immutable historical derivation; user-selectable deletion scope; tombstone plus suppressed regeneration. | Derived narratives can be updated without formal lineage or deletion semantics. | **No.** Kivi requires source attribution and suppression so removed memories do not regrow (**Kivi §§1, 9**). Its reversibility promise pushes toward explicit dependency tracking. |

## 2. Forced choices

These are load-bearing in the paper's own thesis: changing them would materially alter what CSNM is or invalidate its claimed benefits. They are also the decisions Kivi cannot inherit merely as implementation precedent.

| Flag | Load-bearing choice | Why it is load-bearing for CSNM | Why Kivi must independently decide |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Completeness over selective retention** (D1, D7) | “Entire history,” “single longitudinal corpus,” and “complete longitudinal history” define the UCC. Selective capture would weaken the paper's novelty claim and its account of continuity. (**CSNM §§1, 2.3, 3.1; PDF pp. 3-4**) | Kivi's hard exclusions and third-party non-retention are foundational promises, not filters around a CSNM-like corpus (**Kivi §§1-2, 5**). |
| F2 | **Biography and identity as the object of memory** (D3, D4) | CSNM distinguishes itself from RAG by “identity reconstruction” and claims stable identity modeling as an AGI requirement. Removing identity, emotions, and psychological patterns leaves a different architecture. (**CSNM §§2.1, 3.1-3.2, 5.1; PDF pp. 3-7**) | Kivi explicitly positions itself as a colleague without a theory about the user and excludes psychological characterization (**Kivi §§2-3**). |
| F3 | **Narrative integration rather than independent recall** (D4, D5) | The NCL is one of three foundational layers and the paper attributes long-term coherence to narrative integration. (**CSNM §§2.2, 3.2; PDF pp. 3-5**) | Kivi's typed, evidence-linked records preserve contradictions and epistemic status; narrative synthesis may obscure precisely those distinctions (**Kivi §§4, 9**). |
| F4 | **Reasoning over time, not retrieval alone** (D9, D18, D20) | The LRE supplies planning, pattern detection, monitoring, anticipation, and the “temporal agent” framing. Without it CSNM collapses toward conventional memory/RAG. (**CSNM §3.3; PDF p. 5**) | Kivi must decide separately what each surface may retrieve, disclose, and act on; CSNM does not operationalize those boundaries (**Kivi §§6-8**). |
| F5 | **Longitudinal user modeling as alignment** (D12, D18) | The paper's alignment claim is that alignment becomes longitudinal rather than prompt-based. (**CSNM §5.2; PDF pp. 6-7**) | Kivi treats inference permission as explicit and hypotheses as non-actionable. Modeling intentions cannot silently substitute for current user instruction (**Kivi §§4, 6**). |
| F6 | **Historical grounding as the mechanism for reducing hallucination** (D14) | Hallucination reduction is one of the headline implications attributed to the LRE and historical grounding. (**CSNM §§3.3, 5.4; PDF pp. 5-7**) | Kivi's required behavior includes calibrated abstention and inspectable search. More history alone does not specify when evidence is sufficient (**Kivi §8; Appendix C.5**). |

## 3. Unexamined defaults

These are choices the paper treats as natural or invisible. Because the paper supplies no experiments or system details, “unexamined” here means the text neither frames the item as a tradeoff nor tests an alternative.

| Default | Where it appears | Alternative this line of argument does not try |
|---|---|---|
| **More retained history is presumed to improve cognition.** | Complete corpus and whole-history integration are treated as the remedy for fragmentation. (**CSNM §§1, 3.1; PDF pp. 3-4**) | Deliberately lossy memory whose quality is judged by what it refuses to retain; minimal sufficient memory tailored to a task contract. |
| **A coherent identity is presumed better than a plural, contextual, or contradictory one.** | Stable identity, cognitive continuity, and narrative coherence are goals. (**CSNM §§3.2, 5.1; PDF pp. 5-6**) | Preserve multiple context-bound selves or unresolved contradictions without synthesizing them into one narrative. |
| **The system is entitled to model the user psychologically.** | Emotional arcs, psychological patterns, cognitive needs, and development are normal representational targets. (**CSNM §§3.1-3.3; PDF pp. 4-5**) | A system that is technically capable of psychological inference but structurally prohibited from retaining or using it. |
| **Human autobiographical memory is the right design analogy.** | The NCL “mirrors autobiographical memory”; philosophy and cognitive psychology motivate the architecture. (**CSNM §§3.2, 4.1-4.2; PDF pp. 5-6**) | Treat assistant memory as accountable institutional recordkeeping: scoped purpose, provenance, retention limits, correction rights, and audit logs rather than human-like remembering. |
| **Narrative is a neutral integration mechanism.** | “Humans encode meaning narratively”; statistical encoding is cast as the mismatch. (**CSNM §2.2; PDF pp. 3-4**) | Treat narrative synthesis as an interpretive act that must expose authorship, uncertainty, omitted evidence, and competing narratives. |
| **Continuity and alignment point in the same direction.** | Longitudinal memory is said to enable consistent/human-centered alignment. (**CSNM §§1, 5.2; PDF pp. 3, 6-7**) | Current explicit instruction overrides the historical model by default; discontinuity is treated as legitimate change rather than misalignment. |
| **Pattern detection is beneficial unless technically wrong.** | Pattern detection and anticipation are listed as capabilities without a permission model. (**CSNM §3.3; PDF p. 5**) | Correct but unwelcome inference is treated as a failure; permission is independent of predictive accuracy. |
| **Grounding in personal history reduces hallucination.** | Historical grounding is asserted to improve factual/contextual stability. (**CSNM §§3.3, 5.4; PDF pp. 5-7**) | Evaluate whether stale, contradictory, misattributed, or over-retrieved personal history can increase confident error. |
| **One user's history is the relevant unit of organization.** | A single corpus is “the biographical memory of the user.” (**CSNM §3.1; PDF p. 4**) | Partition memory by project, role, relationship, data subject, or purpose, with cross-partition access requiring explicit authorization. |
| **User legibility can follow architecture design.** | The paper specifies internal conceptual layers but no inspection or correction surface. (**CSNM §§3-7; PDF pp. 4-8**) | Make contestability the primary architecture: no derived state exists unless it can be attributed, displayed, corrected, and prevented from regenerating. |
| **Non-institutional origin is evidence of paradigm novelty rather than a variable to evaluate.** | The abstract, introduction, and conclusion emphasize origin outside formal institutions. (**CSNM Abstract, §1, §7; PDF pp. 2-3, 8**) | Separate provenance of the idea from evidence for architectural novelty, feasibility, safety, and comparative performance. |

## 4. Questions the paper never faced, raised by Kivi

These questions derive from Kivi's users, product surfaces, constraints, and failure modes—not from gaps filled with generic memory-system concerns.

1. **When the same text contains the user's instruction and another person's private disclosure, what subject-resolution rule decides which candidate facts can cross the write boundary?** Kivi must use the disclosure to draft well while proving it was not retained (**Kivi §5**).
2. **When a work-level event necessarily implies something about another person, what boundary separates permissible relational residue from impermissible third-party memory?** “Priya is the client contact” survives; her family or emotional state does not (**Kivi §5**).
3. **When a candidate can fit both a permitted work fact and an excluded sensitive category, which classification controls, and how is the drop made inspectable without re-exposing the sensitive content?** (**Kivi §2**)
4. **When regular dictation and Hey Kivi consume the same source history, what schema or service boundary proves that dictation cannot retrieve observations or hypotheses?** This is a structural claim in Kivi, not merely a response-style preference (**Kivi §7; Appendix C.7**).
5. **When all eligible memories are retrieved but some cannot be disclosed in the current mode, what may the trace reveal about withheld content without defeating the permission boundary?** (**Kivi §§6, 8**)
6. **When the UI says “Kivi noticed something here,” how much metadata can it expose before the affordance itself becomes an unwanted inference or pressure to escalate?** (**Kivi §6**)
7. **When a behavior repeats many times, what evidence threshold makes it worth spending one of the user's limited confirmation prompts while still never self-promoting it?** (**Kivi §§4, 8; Appendix B**)
8. **When several uncertain memories compete for a weekly confirmation budget, what definition of “highest value” ranks them: expected future reuse, error cost, user effort saved, or correction urgency?** (**Kivi §8**)
9. **When an inline correction changes an entity during an active task, which current outputs, downstream memories, aliases, and past episodes should update immediately?** (**Kivi §8**)
10. **When the user says “That's not me anymore,” what exact scope does the suppression cover: the wording, the semantic claim, the evidence pattern, the domain, or a time-bounded version of the self?** (**Kivi §9**)
11. **When old transcripts still support a suppressed pattern but new behavior later supports it again, what should determine whether suppression remains absolute, expires, or requires renewed consent?** (**Kivi §9**)
12. **When a contradiction is genuine context-dependence rather than change over time, should Kivi supersede, preserve both with scopes, or ask?** Kivi states a supersession rule but its corpus can contain role- and audience-dependent preferences (**Kivi §§4, 9; Appendix A**).
13. **When a source transcript is corrected or removed, which evidence counts, observations, and suppression decisions must be recomputed, and which audit history must remain?** (**Kivi §§4, 9**)
14. **When a hypothesis is invited for one answer and that answer is not written to memory, what—if anything—may be retained about the invitation, the evidence used, and the user's correction?** (**Kivi §6**)
15. **When the user does not respond to a hypothesis, how long can the unconfirmed question exist, and may it be asked again without turning silence into implicit consent?** (**Kivi §§8-9; Appendix B**)
16. **When abstaining, how should Kivi distinguish “the answer is absent,” “the answer was deliberately not retained,” “the answer exists but is withheld,” and “retrieval failed”?** Its trust surface makes these materially different states (**Kivi §§5-8**).
17. **When showing adjacent search results during abstention, what prevents irrelevant personal material or third-party content from leaking through the explanation surface?** (**Kivi §§5, 8**)
18. **When lexical/entity memory improves transcription, how should Kivi handle an entity name that is ambiguous, changed, or contradicted without allowing broader semantic memory into the dictation path?** (**Kivi §7**)
19. **When the system logs dropped candidates to prove exclusions, what representation gives reviewers evidence that filtering occurred without creating a shadow store of the prohibited content?** (**Kivi §2; Appendix C.1-2**)
20. **When evaluating non-regrowth after correction, what reprocessing horizon and paraphrase variation are sufficient to distinguish real suppression from a test that simply failed to trigger extraction?** (**Kivi §9; Appendix C.6**)
21. **When the same observation is withheld in Anbu and surfaced in Koottu, what remains invariant so the evaluation tests permission rather than retrieval randomness or generation variance?** (**Kivi §6; Appendix C.4**)
22. **When the product promises “say it once,” what counts as successful reuse without silently turning a past instruction into an evergreen rule?** (**Kivi §§3-4**)

## 5. Evaluation gap

### What the paper measured

The paper reports **no experiment, dataset, prototype, baseline, ablation, user study, safety analysis, latency/cost measurement, or quantitative/qualitative evaluation protocol**. It makes capability claims—stable identity modeling, longitudinal alignment, emotional coherence, hallucination reduction, and extended cognition—but provides no results that test them. (**CSNM Abstract; §§5-7; PDF pp. 2, 6-8**)

Accordingly, the paper's evidence cannot distinguish any competing operational answers in the decision inventory. Its support is conceptual precedent and analogy, not comparative evidence.

| Decisions that different systems could answer differently yet receive the same score under the paper's evaluation | Why the paper cannot distinguish them |
|---|---|
| Complete retention vs selective extraction vs explicit save-only (D1, D7) | There is no corpus, retention audit, recall task, privacy test, or score. |
| Retaining vs excluding third-party and sensitive material (D2, D3, D7) | No privacy boundary or non-retention test exists. A system that stores everything and one that discards aggressively are not compared. |
| Narrative biography vs typed atomic memories vs event graph (D4) | No representation is implemented or ablated. “Narrative” is a theoretical commitment, not a measured variable. |
| Recency overwrite vs supersession history vs scoped contradictions (D5) | No contradiction sequence or temporal update benchmark is defined. |
| Automatic promotion vs explicit confirmation (D6) | There is no calibration, consent, correction, or user-control metric. |
| Permanent retention vs type-specific decay (D8) | No longitudinal deployment or stale-memory evaluation occurs. |
| Vector, structured, hybrid, recency, or epistemic ranking (D9) | Retrieval mechanics, queries, relevance judgments, and ranking metrics are absent. |
| Uniform memory access vs surface-specific structural isolation (D10) | The paper defines no separate interaction surfaces and no access-control test. |
| Retrieve-then-gate vs gate-before-retrieval (D11) | Neither retrieval traces nor disclosure policies are specified. |
| Automatic deep inference vs explicit permission modes (D12) | No user study measures usefulness, discomfort, predictability, or permission violations. |
| Confidence-scored claims vs questions vs non-storage (D13) | There is no epistemic calibration or characterization-error benchmark. |
| Plausible response vs clarification vs explicit abstention (D14) | “Reduction of hallucinations” is asserted, but hallucination, abstention quality, and answerability are not measured. |
| No provenance vs source citation vs full trace (D15) | No trust, inspectability, source-faithfulness, or error-diagnosis outcome is evaluated. |
| Delete vs edit vs demote/suppress/regenerate (D16, D22) | No correction workflow or reprocessing/regrowth test exists. |
| Biography UI vs tiered memory UI vs invisible memory (D17) | No interface is specified and no comprehension or controllability study exists. |
| Relevance-only action vs tier- or risk-gated action (D18) | No downstream action tasks or error-cost model is given. |
| Per-turn vs batch vs periodic consolidation (D19) | No ingestion pipeline, throughput, freshness target, or compute measurement exists. |
| Full replay vs summaries vs bounded/hybrid retrieval (D20) | No lifetime-scale corpus, latency budget, token budget, or cost curve is reported. |
| Centralized vs local/purpose-separated/encrypted storage (D21) | No threat model, breach analysis, tenancy model, or security evaluation exists. |

### Claims the paper's nonexistent evaluation especially leaves unresolved for Kivi

- **“Historical grounding reduces hallucinations.”** The paper never tests whether stale, contradictory, or misattributed history instead increases confident error. It therefore supplies no evidence for Kivi's abstention policy (D14).
- **“Narrative coherence” improves continuity.** There is no metric separating useful continuity from harmful over-coherence, such as flattening role-specific preferences or interpreting change as inconsistency (D4-D5).
- **“Longitudinal alignment” is better than prompt-based alignment.** No task tests conflict between current explicit instruction and inferred long-term intention (D12, D18).
- **Identity and emotional modeling benefit the user.** No study measures the failure cost of a wrong or unwanted characterization—the precise cost Kivi's exclusions are designed around (D3, D13, D16).
- **A complete corpus is necessary.** No ablation compares the full history with Kivi-like minimal, typed, source-attributable memory (D1, D4, D7).

The result is categorical: for every operational decision above, CSNM offers **a precedent or philosophical stance, not evidence that its answer dominates an alternative**. Where Kivi and CSNM diverge, the paper's evaluation provides no basis for resolving the choice.
