# SimpleMem, inverted: the decisions Kivi still has to make

**Paper:** Jiaqi Liu et al., *SimpleMem: Efficient Lifelong Memory for LLM Agents*, arXiv:2601.02553v3 (29 January 2026). [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02553)  
**Setting used for transfer:** the attached Kivi semantic-memory position: a dictation/interactive assistant whose invariants are calibration, non-retention of third-party personal material, and user-visible revisability.

## Reading key

- **Holds?** compares the paper's assumption with Kivi as specified—not with a hypothetical generic assistant.
- **Underspecified** means the paper does not provide enough detail to infer the choice safely.
- **Forced choice** means the paper's evidence indicates that changing the answer materially changes its reported result, or the choice is constitutive of Kivi's trust promise.
- This document maps choices; it does not recommend answers.

## 2. Forced choices

These are the decisions where the paper's answer is empirically load-bearing, or where transferring it unchanged would collide with a load-bearing Kivi invariant.

| Decision | Why it is forced | Evidence / consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Normalize and compress raw dialogue, or store ordinary chunks?** (#4–6) | Removing Semantic Structured Compression cuts average F1 from 43.24 to 31.29 and Temporal F1 from 58.62 to 25.40. | §3.4, Table 5. This establishes that *some* normalization/compression answer matters materially; it does not isolate joint extraction, atomization, or early timestamp normalization individually. |
| **Consolidate related facts before retrieval, or assemble only at query time?** (#10–12) | Removing online synthesis drops multi-hop F1 by 31.3% and average F1 by 11.6%. | §3.4, Table 5. The result supports consolidation in this benchmark, but not destructive merging, intra-session scope, or lineage loss. |
| **Adapt retrieval to query intent, or use fixed depth?** (#15–17) | Removing intent-aware retrieval drops open-domain F1 by 26.6%, single-hop F1 by 19.4%, and average F1 by 12.6%. | §3.4, Table 5. Fixed-depth details are underspecified, so the evidence is for the bundle, not each planner output. |
| **Bound retrieval context aggressively, or preserve full history?** (#15, #18, #27) | SimpleMem averages roughly 530–730 retrieval tokens depending on model versus about 16,910 for full-context LoCoMo, while scoring higher. Performance saturates near `k=3`. | §3.2, Tables 1 and 3; Appendix B.3, Table 6. This is load-bearing under the paper's token objective. |
| **Use write-time temporal normalization, or rely on later interpretation?** (#6) | The largest ablation loss is temporal, and the case study attributes performance to absolute timestamp resolution. | §3.4; §3.5. However, the ablation removes the whole compression stage, so causality is bundled. |
| **Treat retention permission as distinct from information value?** (#1, #2, #8) | SimpleMem does not face this choice; Kivi's non-retention claim collapses if “high information” implies “store.” | Kivi §§1, 2, and 5. This is forced by product identity and failure cost, not by SimpleMem's evaluation. |
| **Preserve epistemic tier through storage, synthesis, retrieval, and generation?** (#7, #10–11, #17–21) | Kivi's Anbu/Koottu/Daari behavior is impossible if stated facts, observations, and hypotheses become undifferentiated factual units. | Kivi §§4 and 6. A different answer changes what the system is permitted to assert and act on. |
| **Preserve lineage and user reversibility, or optimize only the current active representation?** (#5, #12, #22–25) | Kivi's claims of attribution, correction, supersession, decay, and non-regrowth all require durable lineage and lifecycle state. | Kivi §§1, 8, and 9. The paper supplies no evidence for the required mechanism. |
| **Separate dictation retrieval rights from interactive-assistant retrieval rights?** (#16, #21, #26) | Kivi defines ordinary dictation as a transcription contract; loading observations there violates that contract even if output accuracy improves. | Kivi §7. This is a schema/access-boundary choice, not a prompt-only choice. |
| **Make abstention inspectable, or return a generic missing-information answer?** (#20, #22, #28) | Kivi's trust model and absent-answer demonstration require search evidence and near misses; SimpleMem's fixed sentence cannot prove calibration. | Appendix A.3 versus Kivi §8 and Appendix C claim 5. |

## 3. Unexamined defaults

|---|---|---|---|

| **The basic durable object is a decontextualized factual sentence.** | Store a claim as a typed relation to evidence, speaker, inference method, uncertainty, and valid time. | “Self-contained” improves retrieval but silently removes the conversational act that determines what the claim means and who asserted it. | §2.1; Appendix A.1 |
| **More recent means more authoritative under contradiction.** | Resolve by source authority and epistemic tier, while preserving simultaneous or disputed claims. | Recency is specified as the conflict policy without argument or evaluation. | Appendix A.3 |

| **Abstraction is benign compression.** | Keep abstractions as revocable derived views whose atoms and source excerpts remain primary. | The paper calls compression “semantic lossless” but does not test whether synthesis invents conjunctions or erases exceptions. | Abstract; §2.2; Appendix A.3 |
| **One memory topology serves all interaction modes.** | Enforce separate capability-scoped stores or query interfaces for transcription, recall, action, and reflection. | The benchmark presents only homogeneous QA queries. | §2.3; §3.1 |
| **Retrieval relevance is the only gate before generation.** | Add independent authority, consent, disclosure, and actionability gates after retrieval. | The final generation prompt has faithfulness and recency rules, but no permission policy. | Appendix A.3 |

## 4. Questions the paper never faced, raised by Kivi

These come from Kivi's users, interaction contracts, and failure modes rather than from gaps filled speculatively from the paper.

1. **When third-party content and the user's instruction occur in the same context window, what should determine which derived work-level residue may cross the write boundary?**
2. **When a fact concerns both the user and another person, whose retention rights govern it, and at what granularity can it be separated?**
3. **When a candidate falls into a sensitive category, should the system record only a drop reason, a redacted fingerprint, or nothing—so that auditability does not recreate the prohibited memory?**
4. **When the same content could be a stated preference, an observed pattern, or a hypothesis, what event establishes its epistemic tier?**
5. **When evidence repeats, what should increase—retrieval rank, evidence count, confidence, prompt priority, or none—and what must never self-promote?**
6. **When synthesis spans transcripts, what constraints prevent a collection of episodes from becoming an unconfirmed characterization of the user?**
7. **When an abstraction is displayed, what minimum evidence must remain pointable-at for the user to verify or reject it?**
8. **When an explicit statement conflicts with a newer behavior pattern, which should govern action, which may be disclosed, and which should trigger confirmation?**
9. **When Kivi retrieves an observation in Anbu mode, what trace may reveal that something was withheld without disclosing the forbidden content itself?**
10. **When a user asks “what do you think?” and temporarily enables Daari, what scope and duration define that permission, and what—if anything—from the response may be written back?**
11. **When ordinary dictation includes a name whose spelling depends on semantic context, how can entity memory improve transcription without opening a path to observations or hypotheses?**
12. **When Kivi lacks an answer, what searched scope and near misses are sufficient to justify abstention without overwhelming or misleading the user?**
13. **When a user corrects a memory inline, which derived abstractions, indexes, cached retrieval results, and prior responses must be invalidated?**
14. **When a user says “That's not me anymore,” what should the suppression match: exact claim, semantic neighborhood, source evidence, memory type, time range, or future inference rule?**
15. **When new evidence resembles a suppressed belief, what should distinguish legitimate changed circumstances from forbidden regrowth?**
16. **When an observation decays, should it become undisclosable, unretrievable, archived, or eligible for reconfirmation—and should its sources remain visible?**
17. **When hypotheses expire, what audit trace, if any, can remain without turning an expired question into a durable psychological profile?**
18. **When confirmation prompts are budgeted, what should determine which uncertainty is worth spending the user's attention on?**
19. **When a memory is accurate but its disclosure would violate the selected mode, how should the system distinguish “known but withheld” from “not found” in both UI and telemetry?**
20. **When the system is wrong, which recovery action best preserves user trust: correction, demotion, supersession, deletion, explanation, or proof of non-regrowth—and how is success verified?**
21. **When a memory influences an external action (draft, reply, or scheduling), what confidence, provenance, and permission threshold should differ from merely mentioning it?**
22. **When the user inspects “read, not kept,” what evidence can prove non-retention across indexes, logs, embeddings, caches, and synthesized memories?**

### Decisions the metrics cannot distinguish

In every row below, two systems can make different design choices and still receive the same paper score. The paper therefore supplies precedent, not evidence, for Kivi's decision.

| Indistinguishable alternatives under the paper's evaluation | Decision(s) affected | Why the score is blind |
|---|---:|---|
| Store a gold-answer fact permissibly vs retain it from third-party personal content without consent. | #1, #8 | Both answer the QA item correctly; no retention-right label exists. |
| Preserve sensitive content vs drop it by policy when it happens to help a gold answer. | #2, #8, #28 | The benchmark rewards recall and may penalize the policy-compliant system. |
| Store a direct user statement vs infer the same sentence from behavior. | #5, #7 | F1/accuracy sees identical answer text, not epistemic provenance. |
| Keep stated/observed/hypothesised tiers vs flatten all content into facts. | #7, #21 | No metric checks what the system was entitled to assert or act on. |
| Preserve source transcript lineage vs store only the compressed sentence. | #5, #12, #18, #22 | Gold-answer scoring does not ask for evidence or attribution. |
| Correctly resolve an ambiguous pronoun vs guess the right benchmark entity by chance. | #4–5 | End-answer accuracy cannot separate faithful resolution from lucky reconstruction. |
| Normalize time with uncertainty and original text vs overwrite with one absolute timestamp. | #6 | If both yield the tested date, they score equally; unqueried normalization errors are invisible. |
| Merge facts faithfully vs create an unsupported conjunction whose tested component is correct. | #10–12 | F1 can reward the matching fragment despite synthesis hallucination elsewhere. |
| Keep repeated observations and evidence counts vs discard them as redundant. | #14 | If the final answer is unchanged, recurrence semantics do not affect the metric. |

### Bundled causality cautions

The ablations prove that the three *bundles* matter, not that every choice within them is established:

- **“Without Semantic Compression”** simultaneously changes filtering, coreference resolution, temporal normalization, atomization, and likely representation. The 56.7% Temporal F1 drop cannot identify which subdecision caused how much of the loss. (§3.4, Table 5)
- **“Without Online Synthesis”** supports consolidation but does not distinguish intra-session from cross-session, destructive from lineage-preserving, or automatic from user-confirmed synthesis. (§3.4, Table 5)
- **“Without Intent-Aware Retrieval”** supports adaptive planning as a bundle but does not distinguish view selection, query rewriting, complexity estimation, depth selection, union, or ranking policy. (§3.4, Table 5)
- **The generous LongMemEval judge** counts an answer correct when it merely touches the gold topic. That can conceal extraneous claims, unwanted disclosures, contradictions, or uncalibrated certainty—the failures Kivi cares about most. (Appendix A.4)

## Bottom line of the inversion

SimpleMem provides evidence for three narrow propositions in its benchmark setting: normalized compression can improve factual and temporal QA; early consolidation can improve multi-hop QA; and query-aware bounded retrieval can improve accuracy/token trade-offs. It does **not** provide evidence for the decisions that define Kivi's product boundary: who may be remembered, how epistemic authority survives compression, what may be disclosed, how provenance is exposed, how correction propagates, or how rejected beliefs stay rejected.

Those are not implementation details downstream of SimpleMem. They are separate design questions for which its metrics make many opposing answers look identical.
