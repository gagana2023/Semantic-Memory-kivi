# Memori Decision Inversion for Kivi

## Scope and reading key

This report inverts *Memori: A Persistent Memory Layer for Efficient, Context-Aware LLM Agents* into the design questions its authors answered, explicitly or implicitly, and tests the assumptions behind those answers against Kivi's stated product position. It maps the choice space; it does not recommend choices.

**Evidence labels:** **Holds** means Kivi's position explicitly supports the paper's assumption. **Partly holds** means the overlap is conditional. **Does not hold** means Kivi's stated constraints push in another direction. **Open** means Kivi deliberately leaves the issue undecided. **Underspecified** means the paper does not disclose enough to reconstruct an answer.

The paper is a nine-page white paper. Its architecture description is concentrated in §2.1; many operational details are absent. Those absences are retained below rather than inferred from the linked codebase.

## 2. Forced choices

These are the choices that are load-bearing for the paper's reported result. Changing them would alter what reaches the answer model, how much context it consumes, or how correctness is scored.

| Decision | Why it is load-bearing in Memori | What materially changes under another answer | Relevance to Kivi |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1 — Atomic triples instead of raw conversational chunks (D3)** | The main accuracy-and-cost claim attributes performance to low-noise, compressed semantic triples. | Raw chunks would raise context use and likely alter single-hop precision; richer event records could change temporal and multi-hop behavior. | Kivi's schema cannot collapse tier, provenance, suppression, and time into bare factual content without losing its control model. |
| **F2 — Dual representation: triples plus summaries (D4, D12)** | The authors explicitly use summaries to compensate for context stripped by triples; temporal and multi-hop results depend on both. | Triples alone would likely worsen chronology/context; larger source passages would weaken the token claim. | Kivi must choose whether narrative context is a generated proxy, source evidence, or both. The paper tests only one pairing. |
| **F3 — Hybrid dense + BM25 retrieval (D11)** | This is the stated retrieval mechanism feeding every answer. | Dense-only, lexical-only, structured, graph, or temporal retrieval would change recall, category performance, and footprint. | Kivi leaves this choice open; Memori provides no ablation isolating the hybrid search contribution. |
| **F4 — Recency resolves contradiction (D9, D10)** | The answer prompt directs the generator to prioritize the latest memory and normalize relative dates. | Preserving conflicts, weighting source authority, or requiring confirmation would change temporal answers and abstention behavior. | Kivi's tier system makes simple recency unsafe: a newer observation need not outrank an older stated fact. |
| **F5 — Short answer conditioned only on retrieved memory (D15–D16)** | Responses are constrained to under 5–6 words and judged against concise gold answers. | Explanations, uncertainty, abstention, or citations could change both token cost and how the judge labels an answer. | Kivi's trust surface is substantially richer, so Memori's outcome measure does not cover the interaction Kivi plans to ship. |
| **F6 — LoCoMo without adversarial questions (D25)** | The paper excludes 445 adversarial questions to align with prior reported comparisons. | Including them could materially shift overall accuracy and reveal false-premise or insufficient-evidence behavior. | Kivi deliberately needs absent-answer and abstention cases; this exclusion removes a nearby stress condition. |
| **F7 — LLM-as-a-judge with generous binary matching (D25)** | The reported 81.95% is defined by GPT-4.1-mini labeling whether a response “touches on the same topic” as the gold answer. | Exact match, evidence attribution, calibrated scoring, human grading, or policy-aware evaluation could reorder systems. | Kivi's failures include impermissible storage/use even when an answer is factually correct, invisible to this judge. |
| **F8 — Prompt-token footprint as the cost boundary (D13, D19, D25)** | The efficiency claim counts retrieved tokens added to the answer context and prices them at GPT-4.1-mini input rates. | Including extraction, summarization, embeddings, indexing, storage, corrections, traces, and confirmation prompts could change system economics. | Kivi's extraction checks and user-visible trace add costs the paper's accounting does not bound. |

## 3. Unexamined defaults

These are defaults the paper appears to inherit rather than frame as choices. “Nobody tries” below means the alternative is absent from this paper and the comparison it presents; it is not a claim that no system anywhere has implemented it.

| Default | What remains unexamined | Alternative outside the paper's frame | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Useful to recall = appropriate to retain.** | Retrieval value is treated as the criterion for memory creation; legitimacy, subject, and consent are not modeled. | A write-path policy in which useful facts can still be ineligible because of subject, sensitivity, verifiability, or surface. | Memori §2.1; Kivi §§2, 5. |
| **An extracted proposition is a fact.** | The system does not encode whether content was stated, observed, or hypothesised. | Make epistemic entitlement a first-class field that constrains use independently of semantic type. | Memori §2.1; Kivi §4. |
| **Subject–predicate–object is the natural atomic unit.** | Binary triples conceal modality, negation scope, source authority, n-ary events, and validity intervals unless augmented. | Treat claims as typed, time-bounded, source-bearing events or questions, with triples as one projection. | Memori §2.1; Kivi §§4, 9. |
| **A generated summary repairs the context removed by extraction.** | Summary error and contestability are not evaluated. | Use exact evidence spans as the auditable layer, optionally adding summaries as non-authoritative navigation. | Memori §2.1; Kivi §§8–9. |
| **Later means truer.** | Recency substitutes for conflict semantics and source authority. | Preserve contradiction as state; resolve by tier, explicit correction, validity interval, or abstention. | Memori Appendix A; Kivi §§4, 9. |
| **Retrieval relevance and disclosure permission are the same gate.** | The paper has no concept of a relevant memory that should be withheld. | Retrieve broadly for inspectability, then apply an explicit user-controlled disclosure policy; or structurally block retrieval by surface. | Memori §3.3; Kivi §§6–7. |
| **Every benchmark question should be answered.** | No abstention threshold exists even though absence and false premises are central production states. | Evaluate useful refusal, near-miss explanation, and evidence-bound uncertainty as first-class outcomes. | Memori Appendix A; Kivi §8, Appendix C. |
| **A single memory path can serve all application actions.** | API-layer interception treats calls homogeneously. | Grant distinct memory capabilities to transcription, drafting, scheduling, recall, and reflective requests. | Memori §2; Kivi §7. |
| **Memory mistakes are stateless scoring errors.** | The paper does not examine how a wrong stored claim persists, shapes later outputs, or resists deletion. | Evaluate error recovery as a sequence: creation → use → correction → reprocessing → proof of non-regrowth. | Memori §3; Kivi §9, Appendix C. |
| **Context tokens dominate cost.** | Offline extraction, summaries, embeddings, storage, and maintenance are absent from the cost numerator. | Compare total lifecycle cost per useful remembered fact and per corrected/expired fact. | Memori §§3.5, 3.9. |
| **Benchmark characters stand in for real people.** | Third-party privacy, organizational boundaries, and user rights disappear in dyadic scripted conversations. | Construct cases where transient third-party context is necessary for the task but forbidden from durable storage. | Memori §3.1; Kivi §5, Appendix C. |
| **Human legibility is optional.** | Provenance is useful internally, but no end-user inspection surface is evaluated. | Make the same trace serve trust, correction, abstention, and engineering inspection. | Memori §2.1; Kivi §§8–9. |

## 4. Questions the paper never faced

These arise directly from Kivi's product commitments and failure modes rather than from gaps in Memori alone.

1. **When an interaction mixes the user's words with application context written by someone else, what exact transformation can produce a user-world fact without retaining the other person's personal content?** Kivi §5 requires a structural write-path boundary, not post-hoc cleanup.
2. **When a work-level fact necessarily names another person, what distinguishes a permissible relationship fact from an impermissible characterization of that person?** “Priya is the Acme contact” survives; “Priya seemed annoyed” does not. The operational boundary still needs cases at the edge.
3. **When a candidate falls into both a permitted work category and an excluded sensitive category, which classification governs, and how is the drop made auditable without retaining the sensitive content in the log?** Kivi §2 requires both exclusion and proof.
4. **What information may a dropped-candidate log retain without becoming a shadow memory store?** Counts and reasons are intended to prove non-retention, but detailed logs could recreate what the primary store rejected.
5. **When the same content is stated by the user in one context and merely observed in another, what tier applies to the merged memory and to each piece of evidence?** Kivi's axes are independent, but evidence can cross tiers.
6. **When a user corrects a memory, does the correction modify content, source interpretation, tier, temporal validity, or all four?** Kivi requires cheap inline correction and audit history.
7. **When “That's not me anymore” is invoked, what semantic scope must suppression cover so the belief does not regrow without blocking legitimate future change?** Exact-string suppression is too narrow; category suppression may be too broad. Kivi §9 makes this a core failure mode.
8. **When “Forget” conflicts with the need to prove deletion or preserve an audit history, what metadata may survive and who can inspect it?** Kivi asks for removal plus suppression while also valuing provenance and history.
9. **When an old observation decays, should it remain visible in history, remain retrievable but undisclosable, or be removed from active retrieval entirely?** Kivi commits to decay but leaves the state transition unspecified.
10. **When a hypothesis expires, should the evidence that generated it remain eligible to produce the same question again?** Without a rule, expiry can become cyclical re-derivation.
11. **When an observation spans many transcripts, what evidence subset must be shown for the user to verify it without reviewing an overwhelming history?** Kivi promises pointable-at patterns and a normal-user surface.
12. **When the permission dial changes after retrieval but before generation, which policy snapshot governs the response and trace?** Kivi separates retrieval from disclosure, creating a consistency question Memori never has.
13. **When Anbu says “Kivi noticed something here,” how much can that affordance reveal without itself disclosing the observation it is withholding?** The existence, category, or timing of a memory may itself be sensitive.
14. **When Daari is unlocked through a phrase such as “what do you think?”, what linguistic evidence is sufficient to count as per-request permission, and how is accidental activation made visible?** Kivi rejects tone inference but still relies on intent recognition for escalation.
15. **When Daari generates a hypothesis that must not be written to memory, what may appear in telemetry, traces, caches, or safety logs?** Non-persistence must apply beyond the semantic-memory table if the promise is to hold.
16. **When regular dictation requires a formatting preference, how is that preference allowed through a path that otherwise permits only lexical/entity memory?** Kivi §7 names formatting preference as allowed, creating a boundary case with the broader “preference” type.
17. **When entity memory improves transcription but the same entity is linked to excluded third-party information, what projection is safe to load into dictation?** Structural separation must reach fields, not just tables.
18. **When Kivi abstains, what evidence threshold justifies “I don't have it” rather than “I may have missed it,” and how are near misses ranked for display?** Kivi's example makes a stronger claim than ordinary uncertainty.
19. **When confirmation prompts are scarce, what makes an uncertain memory high-value: recurrence, task impact, error cost, user irritation, or expected future reuse?** Kivi commits to a budget but not its allocation rule.
20. **When a memory is pinned, does pinning prevent decay, override suppression, increase retrieval rank, or only affect presentation?** Kivi lists pinning but does not define its semantics.
21. **When the user edits a derived observation, does the edited statement become stated, remain observed, or become a new user-authored memory linked to the old one?** The answer affects provenance and future promotion.
22. **When the Why panel exposes retrieved-but-unused memories, what privacy or cognitive cost does that create, and can the user narrow trace detail without losing contestability?** Kivi treats retrieval visibility as a virtue, but the trace can itself over-disclose.
23. **When a source transcript is later deleted or edited, what happens to memories derived from it and to claims assembled across multiple transcripts?** Kivi requires attribution and reversibility but does not yet specify dependency propagation.
24. **When an excluded candidate is falsely classified as permitted, what recovery mechanism finds and removes its downstream summaries, embeddings, caches, traces, and derived observations?** A hard write barrier needs a containment story for barrier failure.
25. **When multiple people use the same device, workspace, or account context, what establishes whose memory is being read or written?** Kivi's non-retention principle makes identity boundary failure higher-cost than a missed answer.

## 5. Evaluation gap

### What the paper measured

Memori processes LoCoMo conversations into triples and summaries, retrieves with hybrid dense/BM25 search, and asks GPT-4.1-mini to answer. The same model family grades generated answers as correct or wrong against a concise gold answer. The paper reports single-hop, multi-hop, temporal, open-domain, and weighted overall accuracy across three rounds, excluding the adversarial category. It also reports mean tokens added to the answer context and converts those tokens to an input-cost estimate. [Memori §§3.1–3.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19935v1#S3)

The evaluation therefore observes two principal outcomes:

- whether a short answer is judged to match the gold topic; and
- how many retrieved tokens enter the answer prompt.

It does **not** directly measure extraction precision/recall against annotated memories, retrieval recall independently of generation, summary faithfulness, citation correctness, calibration, abstention, policy compliance, lifecycle behavior, total cost, latency, or human trust.

### Decisions its metrics cannot distinguish

| Indistinguishable answers under the paper's evaluation | Why they can score the same | Decisions left without evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Retain only user facts **vs** also retain third-party personal facts | If both retrieve the benchmark answer, the judge sees no privacy distinction. | D1, D7, D25 |
| A permissible memory **vs** a sensitive or psychologically interpretive memory | The judge rewards factual topical overlap, not legitimacy of retention. | D2, D7, D25 |
| User-stated fact **vs** model-observed pattern **vs** hypothesis asserted as fact | Epistemic origin is absent from the gold-answer criterion. | D6, D22 |
| Exact source provenance **vs** conversation link **vs** unsupported derived summary | The generated answer is scored, not the evidence chain. | D4, D5 |
| Correctly merged identities **vs** accidental record conflation that happens to yield the right answer | No memory-level annotations or entity-resolution score are reported. | D8, D24 |
| Latest claim wins **vs** confirmed claim wins **vs** conflict preserved | On cases where the latest claim equals the gold answer, all policies score equally. | D9, D10 |
| Safe abstention **vs** unsupported guess | Adversarial questions are excluded, and the prompt assumes answer production; a lucky guess is rewarded. | D15, D25 |
| Answer-only UI **vs** cited/inspectable answer **vs** withheld-memory trace | Interface legibility and contestability are outside the benchmark. | D14, D16 |
| One universal retrieval path **vs** task-specific capability boundaries | LoCoMo supplies only QA, not dictation and Hey Kivi as different contracts. | D17 |
| Correctable memory **vs** immutable wrong memory | Evaluation is a single pass; no correction/replay sequence occurs. | D20, D21, D22 |
| Deletion **vs** suppression that prevents re-derivation | No reprocessing after a user rejection is tested. | D20, D21 |
| Observation decay **vs** indefinite retention | Benchmark accuracy treats old answerable facts as valuable and does not measure identity change harm. | D20 |
| Explicit dial permission **vs** tone-inferred permission **vs** no permission model | The judge sees only answer correctness, not whether the system was entitled to disclose it. | D14, D17 |
| Full lifecycle cost **vs** low prompt-token cost with expensive extraction | Only tokens injected at answer time enter the cost comparison. | D13, D19, D24, D25 |
| Low-latency fresh memory **vs** delayed batch memory | No ingestion latency, freshness lag, or service-level target is reported. | D19 |
| Local single-user FAISS **vs** durable multi-tenant storage with deletion guarantees | Storage operations and isolation are not exercised by QA accuracy. | D24 |
| Transparent exclusion log **vs** silent filter **vs** shadow retention in logs | Logging and non-retention proof are not part of the task or score. | D1, D7, D25 |

### Category-level evidence and its limits

- **Single-hop (87.87%)** supports the combined pipeline's ability to answer many direct fact questions efficiently. It does not isolate whether triples, extraction quality, hybrid retrieval, the summary link, or the answer prompt produced the gain.
- **Temporal (80.37%)** supplies evidence that the pipeline can answer many time-sensitive questions, but Memori trails Zep and LangMem here and acknowledges that isolated triples lose temporal context. It does not compare ingestion-time normalization, validity intervals, conflict-preserving storage, or user-confirmed supersession.
- **Multi-hop (72.70%)** shows some cross-memory synthesis, but the paper contains an internal wording error: it says Memori “outperform[s] Zep (69.16%) and trail[s] LangMem (61.06%),” even though 72.70 exceeds both figures. Table 1 should govern the numerical reading. [Memori §3.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19935v1#S3.SS8)
- **Open-domain (63.54%)** indicates weak coverage for broad synthesis and exposes the tension between context breadth and token minimization. It does not establish where Kivi should place that tradeoff because Kivi's target tasks and error costs differ.
- **Overall (81.95%)** is a weighted aggregate dominated by 830 single-hop questions out of 1,529 included questions. It is not a neutral summary of Kivi's claims, which emphasize policy and lifecycle sequences absent from the benchmark. [Memori Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19935v1#A3)
- **Token footprint (1,294 mean added tokens)** establishes a narrow inference-context measurement. It does not establish 20× total-system savings because the denominator excludes creation and maintenance work and the comparison values come from prior papers rather than a fully disclosed, controlled end-to-end rerun. [Memori §3.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19935v1#S3.SS9)

### Bottom line of the evidence

The paper offers evidence for a **combined precedent**: extracting compact triples, pairing them with conversation summaries, retrieving them through hybrid similarity, and prompting a small LLM can achieve strong LoCoMo short-answer accuracy with a small answer-context footprint. It offers little or no discriminating evidence for Kivi's decisions about retention legitimacy, third-party boundaries, epistemic tiers, permission, abstention, user-visible provenance, correction, suppression, expiry, surface-specific rights, or total lifecycle economics.
