# Corpus Persona: Meera Sethi

## Why this persona

Meera is a product lead at a small design studio. This preserves the cast and work already fixed in the product-position document—Meera, Priya, Arun, Nandini, Atlas, and Acme—while giving the six-month corpus enough routine coordination, drafting, rescheduling, and revision to test Kivi's actual memory model.

Her work produces the three supported memory types cleanly:

- **Entities:** named people, clients, projects, channels, documents, and their work-level relationships.
- **Preferences:** stated rules when Meera explicitly gives one, and observed patterns only when repeated transcripts support them.
- **Episodes:** dated events such as decisions, handoffs, deadline moves, launches, and cancellations.

The corpus must not turn Meera into a psychological profile. It should contain mundane traffic as well as useful evidence: acknowledgements, typo fixes, room changes, lunch orders, duplicate recaps, and drafts that create no durable memory.

## Corpus frame

- **Period:** Monday 5 January through Tuesday 30 June 2026.
- **Expected volume:** about 500 transcript-like records from this one user.
- **Source rule:** only Meera's dictation, her authored Hey Kivi turns, and her explicit confirmations or corrections are evidence. Quoted Slack messages, pasted email, and meeting notes written by others may shape an immediate draft but are not evidence about those people.
- **Time rule:** dates below are the source of truth. Arrival order may vary in test imports, but event time does not.

## The work

### Atlas — Acme customer-portal redesign

- **Purpose:** replace Acme's legacy fleet-manager portal with a responsive portal covering vehicle status, driver access, invoices, and support.
- **Timeline:** already active on 5 January; client beta on 18 May; production launch on 22 June; closeout through 30 June.
- **Meera's role:** product lead and primary owner of scope, review cadence, client narrative, and acceptance decisions.
- **Key ownership:** Arun Menon owns backend delivery; Sofia Martins owns design; Priya Raghavan is the Acme client contact.
- **Review cadence:** Tuesdays at 15:00 GMT early in the period; changed to Thursdays at 15:00 GMT on 12 February.
- **Current status on 30 June:** launched; monitoring two non-blocking defects and preparing a 7 July closeout.

### Meterline — Stripe Billing replacement discovery

- **Purpose:** test whether Acme should replace Stripe Billing with a custom usage-led invoicing service called Meterline.
- **Timeline:** discovery begins 12 January; technical spike runs 2–20 February; recommendation drafted in March; project stopped on 9 April.
- **Meera's role:** discovery lead, responsible for the recommendation and decision record.
- **Decision:** the early working direction was to build Meterline in Q2. On 9 April Acme chose to keep Stripe Billing and add a thin export service instead.
- **Current status on 30 June:** killed. The decision record is final; no implementation work remains.

### Harbor — driver onboarding pilot

- **Purpose:** reduce the number of steps required for a fleet administrator to invite and verify a new driver.
- **Timeline:** proposed 23 March; formally starts 6 April; prototype tests 4–15 May; pilot moves from 8 June to 13 July.
- **Meera's role:** product lead; Omar runs the research logistics and Sofia owns the interaction design.
- **Current status on 30 June:** active, with the pilot build in progress and a 13 July pilot date.
- **Scope boundary:** Harbor covers invitations, identity checks, and first login. It does not cover driver training or payroll.

### Q3 pricing page

- **Purpose:** rewrite Ternary's public pricing page so prospects can distinguish fixed-scope discovery from retained product work.
- **Timeline:** starts 4 May; first copy review 21 May; legal review 9 June; publication target 2 July.
- **Meera's role:** internal product owner; Jonas Reed writes the first two copy passes, then Sofia takes over final page design.
- **Decision history:** the first draft used three named packages. On 28 May the team reverses this and uses two engagement models with “Talk to us” pricing.
- **Current status on 30 June:** approved for publication on 2 July, awaiting one accessibility check.

### Cedar — Northwind analytics handoff

- **Purpose:** transfer the completed Northwind dashboard prototype and research archive to Northwind's internal product team.
- **Timeline:** handoff work runs 5–30 January; final walkthrough on 29 January; access confirmed on 2 February.
- **Meera's role:** outgoing product lead.
- **Current status on 30 June:** complete and archived. References after February are normally about retrieving an old artefact, not active delivery.

## Tools and contexts

### Slack dictation

- **Where:** walking between meeting rooms or at her desk, mostly for `#atlas-delivery`, `#design-review`, and direct messages.
- **Register:** short, conversational, and action-first. Fragments are acceptable. She names the owner and due date when assigning work.
- **Typical shape:** “Morning—quick Atlas one. Arun, can you add the export failure case by Thursday? Link is in the thread. Thanks.”
- **Noise profile:** room names, channel names, and pasted messages from colleagues. Quoted messages are application context, not memory evidence.

### Client email dictation

- **Where:** at her desk, usually after a client review.
- **Register:** complete prose, calm and direct, with context before the ask. No chat abbreviations. Usually two or three short paragraphs.
- **Typical shape:** “Hi Priya, thanks for the review today. We have incorporated the navigation change and will share the revised prototype on Thursday. Could you confirm the invoice labels by noon Tuesday? Best, Meera.”
- **Contrast:** an email draft must not inherit Slack fragments or channel shorthand.

### Design and decision documents

- **Where:** quiet desk blocks, often in Notion; raw dictation is converted into headings and prose.
- **Register:** neutral and evidence-led. Decisions use the labels **Context**, **Decision**, **Rationale**, and **Open questions**. Dates are absolute, not “next week.”
- **Typical shape:** “Decision: retain Stripe Billing for the 2026 release. Rationale: the custom service does not reduce reconciliation work enough to justify Q2 delivery risk.”

### Meeting recaps

- **Where:** phone dictation immediately after Zoom or a meeting-room session, before opening the next task.
- **Register:** compressed but structured: date, attendees, decisions, actions, then unresolved items. These produce many time-bearing episodes.
- **Typical shape:** “Atlas review, 12 February. Priya, Arun, Sofia, me. Decision: move the recurring review to Thursday at three GMT. Arun owns the invoice export check for the nineteenth.”

### Private scratch dictation

- **Where:** commute or end of day.
- **Register:** terse fragments and disposable reminders: groceries, a book title, a sentence to rewrite, or “remember charger.” Most should produce no durable work memory.
- **Boundary:** scratch material is not a licence to infer mood, health, relationships, finances, motivations, or character.

### Hey Kivi

- **Where:** desktop, when Meera deliberately asks for recall, a draft, or an internal schedule change.
- **Register:** direct questions and commands: “Who owns Atlas backend?”, “Draft the follow-up to Ria,” or “Move the Harbor pilot to 13 July.”
- **Boundary:** ordinary dictation does not silently become Hey Kivi because it contains a question or sounds reflective.

## Demonstrated preferences

Meera never states the items in this section as rules. They may become **observed preferences** only after repeated, independently locatable examples. Repetition increases evidence; it never promotes an observation to stated. Silence after Kivi surfaces one is not confirmation.

1. **Slack openings:** in first messages sent before noon, she opens with “Morning—” and then a short topic label such as “quick Atlas one.” In replies within an active thread, she skips the greeting.
2. **Assignments contain owner and date:** action requests nearly always name one owner and an explicit weekday or calendar date. She rewrites vague phrases such as “soon” into a date before sending.
3. **Client email structure:** she writes client emails in prose rather than bullet lists, usually with context in the first paragraph and the ask in the second.
4. **Client sign-off:** she closes external client emails with “Best, Meera.” She does not use that sign-off in Slack or internal documents.
5. **Recap order:** meeting recaps consistently list decisions before actions, even when actions were discussed first in the meeting.
6. **Decision formatting:** in design and decision documents she uses the four headings **Context**, **Decision**, **Rationale**, and **Open questions**, in that order.
7. **Deadline wording:** she uses absolute dates in documents and client email, but weekdays without dates in short internal Slack messages when the date is unambiguous that week.
8. **Disallowed wording in her own drafts:** she repeatedly replaces “leverage” with “use” during revisions, but never says that this is a standing rule.

To remain demonstrable, each pattern should have exceptions and neutral records around it. For example, one rushed email may contain bullets, and many Slack acknowledgements will contain neither an owner nor a date. An observation is about a repeated pattern, not a universal trait.

## Speech signature

- **Voice and pace:** Meera speaks at roughly 145 words per minute in desk dictation and closer to 175 while walking. She pauses briefly before names and dates but runs clauses together in meeting recaps.
- **Fillers:** “okay,” “right,” and “quick one” occur often at the start of raw dictation. “Actually” usually precedes a correction within the same utterance. The formatted text normally removes these unless they carry meaning.
- **Self-repair:** she corrects herself with “sorry” or “no, make that,” for example: “Tuesday—sorry, no, make that Thursday the twelfth.” The final formatted text should use the corrected value while raw ASR preserves the repair.
- **Punctuation speech:** in longer documents she says “new paragraph,” “heading,” “colon,” and “open questions.” In Slack she rarely dictates punctuation.
- **Date style:** she says “the twelfth of Feb” and “Thursday at three GMT”; formatted client text uses “12 February” and “15:00 GMT.”

## Rhythm

- **Typical weekday volume:** three to five short Slack dictations, one or two email or document passages, and a meeting recap on review days. Some accepted records are duplicates caused by retrying a mobile upload; these must not increase evidence.
- **Monday:** planning notes between 09:30 and 10:30, then assignment messages after the studio stand-up.
- **Tuesday:** Atlas preparation in the morning. Until 12 February, the Atlas client review produces a recap around 20:45 IST; after the cadence change, Tuesday becomes quieter.
- **Wednesday:** longest document-dictation block, usually 14:00–16:00 IST.
- **Thursday:** after 12 February, a burst of Atlas review follow-ups between 20:30 and 21:15 IST because the client meeting is at 15:00 GMT.
- **Friday:** short internal recaps and next-week scheduling; little long-form drafting after 16:00.
- **Weekends:** normally silent. A few Sunday-evening scratch notes exist, but they are mundane and mostly memory-ineligible.

Quiet periods with locatable work reasons:

- **26–30 January:** fewer new dictations while Meera is running Cedar handoff sessions; the records that do exist are mostly access checks and acknowledgements.
- **23–27 February:** Atlas prototype test week; she records one consolidated recap per day instead of many live Slack drafts.
- **13–17 April:** the studio is closed for a planned office move and public-holiday break. There are only two remote scheduling records.
- **18–22 May:** Atlas beta week creates a spike rather than a lull: many terse bug-routing messages and little design-doc prose.
- **1–5 June:** reduced volume during the Priya-to-Ria handoff; most records are forwarding notes, ownership confirmations, and rescheduled meetings.

Timing and volume are corpus texture, not evidence that Meera is stressed, productive, avoidant, behind, or any other characterisation.

## Out of scope

The corpus intentionally contains no reliable answer to the following. Kivi should say what it searched and abstain rather than fill the gap.

- Meera's age, birthday, home address, family, relationships, health, religion, politics, salary, savings, or personal finances.
- Her personality type, emotional state, competence, motivations, insecurities, self-image, or reasons for working late.
- Private facts about Priya, Ria, Arun, or anyone else, including health, family, mood, personal absence reasons, or character judgements. If such text appears in pasted application context, it is read for the immediate reply and not retained.
- What Arun “really thinks” about the Atlas architecture, unless Meera's own dictation records a specific work statement with a date. Kivi must not infer views from task ownership.
- Any statement by Arun about an **auth migration**. The history includes Arun's Atlas backend work and Priya's database-migration discussion, but no Arun/auth-migration episode; this is a deliberate near-miss test.
- Acme's revenue, contract value, payment status, or procurement negotiations. Work-level pricing-page choices are present; company finances are not.
- Live email, Slack, calendar, web, or Figma state. The corpus contains replayed text and links, not external-system access.
- Events after 30 June 2026, including whether the 2 July pricing-page publication, 7 July Atlas closeout, or 13 July Harbor pilot actually happened.
- A complete biography, rolling project narrative, psychological profile, or inferred causal model of Meera. Kivi stores independently correctable entities, preferences, and episodes—not summaries of who she is.
